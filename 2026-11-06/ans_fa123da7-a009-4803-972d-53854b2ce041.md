## Answer

 Treatment depends on the **spondyloarthropathy subtype and affected domains**—axial disease, peripheral arthritis, enthesitis, skin disease, bowel disease, or uveitis.

- **Axial spondyloarthritis/ankylosing spondylitis**
  - **NSAIDs are first-line**; trials of different NSAIDs may be appropriate if response is inadequate.
  - For NSAID-resistant disease, effective options include **TNF inhibitors**—adalimumab, infliximab, etanercept, golimumab, or certolizumab pegol— as well as **IL-17 inhibitors** such as secukinumab or ixekizumab and **JAK inhibitors** such as tofacitinib or upadacitinib.
  - If there is concomitant **uveitis or inflammatory bowel disease**, a monoclonal TNF antibody should be used.
  - **Sulfasalazine** may help peripheral arthritis but is not effective for spinal or sacroiliac disease. Methotrexate has no convincing benefit for ankylosing spondylitis; IL-6 inhibitors and abatacept are ineffective.
  - Corticosteroids have minimal benefit and may worsen osteopenia.
  - All patients should receive instruction from a physical therapist regarding **postural exercises and a safe exercise program**. Early rheumatology referral is recommended. (1) (2)

- **Psoriatic arthritis**
  - Treatment should be **domain-based**, addressing peripheral arthritis, axial disease, enthesitis, dactylitis, psoriasis, nail disease, inflammatory bowel disease, and uveitis.
  - Options include **TNF inhibitors, IL-17 inhibitors, IL-12/23 or IL-23 inhibitors, apremilast, and JAK inhibitors**.
  - For axial disease, TNF inhibitors, IL-17 inhibitors, and JAK inhibitors are specifically recommended; methotrexate may be considered for non-axial disease and skin involvement.
  - Corticosteroids are less effective and may precipitate pustular psoriasis during tapering. (3) (4)

- **Reactive arthritis**
  - **NSAIDs** are the mainstay.
  - For chronic chlamydial-associated disease, rifampin combined with doxycycline or azithromycin was more effective than placebo in the cited trial.
  - NSAID-nonresponsive patients may respond to **sulfasalazine or methotrexate**. Anti-TNF agents or other biologics may be considered for recent-onset refractory disease. (5) (6)

- **IBD-associated spondyloarthritis**
  - Treating the intestinal inflammation usually improves the arthritis.
  - NSAIDs may help mild arthritis but can worsen inflammatory bowel disease.
  - **TNF inhibitors, JAK inhibitors, and ustekinumab** can treat both bowel and joint/axial inflammation. (6)
