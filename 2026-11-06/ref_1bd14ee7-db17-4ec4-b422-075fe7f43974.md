## TREATMENT

NSAIDs remain first-line treatment of axSpA ([Table 5–4](/content.aspx?legacysectionid=papacmdt26_ch5tb4)). Because individual patients differ in their response to particular NSAIDs, empiric trials of several different NSAIDs are warranted if the response to any given NSAID is not satisfactory. TNF inhibitors have well-established efficacy for NSAID-resistant axial disease; responses are often substantial and durable. TNF inhibitors may also have disease-modifying effects and slow radiographic progression. [Etanercept](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658754), [adalimumab](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658863), [infliximab](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658773), [golimumab](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659061), or certolizumab pegol is reasonable for patients whose symptoms are refractory to NSAIDs ([Table 23–4](/content.aspx?legacysectionid=papacmdt26_ch23tb4)). No particular TNF inhibitor is preferred except in patients with concomitant uveitis or IBD, for whom monoclonal antibodies to TNF should be used. [Secukinumab](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659204) and [ixekizumab](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659285) (monoclonal antibodies against soluble IL-17A) and [tofacitinib](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659141) and [upadacitinib](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659447) (small-molecule JAK inhibitors) are highly effective and FDA-approved for the treatment of radiographic and nonradiographic axSpA. There is no convincing evidence that [methotrexate](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659863) benefits patients with ankylosing spondylitis. Inhibitors of IL-6 and [abatacept](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658959) are ineffective. [Sulfasalazine](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659959) (1000 mg orally twice daily) may be useful for peripheral arthritis but lacks effectiveness for spinal and sacroiliac joint disease. Corticosteroids have minimal impact in ankylosing spondylitis and can worsen osteopenia. All patients should be referred to a physical therapist for instruction in postural exercises and a safe exercise program.

## TREATMENT

Guidelines for the treatment of psoriatic arthritis recommend a “domain-based” approach (peripheral arthritis, axial disease, enthesitis, dactylitis, skin psoriasis, nail disease, IBD, uveitis) and outline therapeutic options specific to each “domain.” A patient may have activity in some, but not all, domains and treatment should be tailored as such. For musculoskeletal and cutaneous features of psoriatic arthritis, TNF inhibitors, IL-12/23 inhibitors ([ustekinumab](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659088)), IL-17 inhibitors ([secukinumab](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659204), [ixekizumab](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659285), [brodalumab](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659307), bimekizumab), IL-23 inhibitors ([guselkumab](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659333), risankizumab), phosphodiesterase 4 inhibitors ([apremilast](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659183)), and JAK inhibitors ([tofacitinib](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659141), [upadacitinib](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659447)) are recommended ([Table 23–4](/content.aspx?legacysectionid=papacmdt26_ch23tb4)). For axial disease, TNF inhibitors, anti-IL-17, and JAK inhibitors are specifically recommended. For non-axial domains of psoriatic arthritis and skin, conventional DMARDs, specifically [methotrexate](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659863), may be considered.

Corticosteroids are less effective in psoriatic arthritis than in other forms of inflammatory arthritis and may precipitate pustular psoriasis during tapers.

# 23-34: Inflammatory Bowel Disease–Associated Spondyloarthritis

One-fifth of patients with IBD have arthritis. IBD-associated arthritis can have a peripheral arthritis pattern with or without axial involvement. The peripheral arthritis is usually a nondeforming, asymmetric, oligoarthritis of large joints. Peripheral arthritis may pre-date the onset of the IBD and the activity may be independent of intestinal symptoms. Axial involvement with IBD-associated spondyloarthritis is indistinguishable by symptoms or radiographs from axSpA and follows a course independent of the bowel disease. About 50% of these patients are HLA-B27 positive.

Controlling the intestinal inflammation usually eliminates the arthritis. NSAIDs can be effective for mild arthritis but may exacerbate IBD. TNF inhibitors, JAK inhibitors ([tofacitinib](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659141), [upadacitinib](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659447)), and anti-IL-12/23 ([ustekinumab](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659088)) can treat both the bowel and joint/axial inflammation.

Whipple disease should be considered in the differential diagnosis for a patient with GI and joint symptoms. Two-thirds of patients with Whipple disease experience arthralgia or arthritis, most often an episodic, large-joint polyarthritis. The arthritis usually precedes the GI manifestations by years and often resolves as the diarrhea develops.
