 ## Management of acute diarrhoea

### 1. Assess severity and need for investigation
Most acute diarrhoeal episodes are mild and self-limited. Stool microbiological evaluation is indicated with:

- **Profuse diarrhoea with dehydration**
- **Bloody stools**
- **Fever ≥38.5°C**
- Symptoms lasting **>48 hours without improvement**
- Recent antibiotic use
- A suspected community outbreak
- Severe abdominal pain in patients >50 years
- Age ≥70 years or immunocompromised status (1)

The **Algorithm for the management of acute diarrhoea** is shown in **Figure 49-3** (1).

### 2. Replace fluids and electrolytes
Fluid and electrolyte replacement is central to treatment:

- Mild cases may require fluid replacement alone.
- Start **oral sugar-electrolyte solution/oral rehydration solution (ORS)** promptly, particularly with severe diarrhoea or in elderly and vulnerable patients.
- Patients with profound dehydration, inability to drink, or vomiting that prevents oral therapy require **intravenous rehydration**, such as Ringer’s lactate; ORS should be given when oral intake becomes safe (2; 3).
- Oral rehydration remains the easiest and most efficient method of rehydration, including in severe diarrhoeal illness (4).

### 3. Continue nutrition
- Begin feeding as soon as possible; early refeeding is safe, well tolerated, and beneficial.
- Continue breastfeeding in infants unless contraindicated (4).

### 4. Symptomatic treatment
For **moderately severe, nonfebrile, nonbloody diarrhoea**, antimotility or antisecretory agents such as **loperamide** may be useful adjuncts (2).

Avoid antimotility agents:

- In **febrile dysentery**
- In **bloody diarrhoea**, because of concerns about prolonged fever, toxic megacolon, and haemolytic-uraemic syndrome (HUS) (2; 4).

**Bismuth subsalicylate** may reduce vomiting and diarrhoea, but should be avoided in immunocompromised patients and those with renal impairment because of the risk of bismuth encephalopathy (2).

### 5. Antibiotics
Antibiotics should be used **selectively**, particularly in severe illness, traveller’s diarrhoea, or selected high-risk patients. They may shorten the duration and severity of illness, but antimicrobial resistance is a concern (2; 3).

Empirical treatment may be considered for moderately to severely ill patients with febrile diarrhoea, for example with:

- **Azithromycin** 500 mg as a single dose, or
- **Ciprofloxacin** 500 mg twice daily for 3–5 days

Suspected giardiasis may be treated empirically with **metronidazole** 250 mg four times daily for 7 days. Antibiotic selection should otherwise depend on the suspected pathogen, local resistance patterns, and clinical circumstances (2).

Most patients with **dysentery—bloody diarrhoea with fever—**should receive empirical antimicrobial treatment while awaiting diagnostic testing. A macrolide may be preferred for suspected *Campylobacter* infection because of widespread fluoroquinolone resistance (3).

Avoid inappropriate antimicrobial therapy when **STEC/EHEC infection** is suspected because of the risk of HUS; the selected references do not provide a specific antimicrobial recommendation for this situation.

### 6. Investigate suspected infectious diarrhoea
For severe acute infectious diarrhoea, stool testing may include culture-independent molecular testing, culture, toxin assays, or ova-and-parasite evaluation depending on the clinical and epidemiological context. Testing should particularly be pursued in severe, inflammatory, persistent, nosocomial, or outbreak-associated illness (1; 5).

### 7. Manage complications
Electrolyte abnormalities and dehydration should be actively corrected. In toxic megacolon, frequent medical and surgical assessment is required; persistent colonic distention after 48–72 hours may require colectomy, while perforation requires surgery and intensive medical support (4).

### 8. Prevention
Handwashing after defecation or handling children’s faeces and before food preparation is recommended (4). Clinicians should identify possible diarrhoeal outbreaks and promptly alert public-health authorities (2).
