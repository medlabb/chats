## Answer

 ## Management of measles

### 1. Supportive treatment
- There is **no specific antiviral therapy approved** for measles.
- Provide **antipyretics and fluids**.
- Treat bacterial superinfections, such as **pneumonia and otitis media**, and manage complications including **seizures and respiratory failure**. (1)
- Antibiotic prophylaxis during outbreaks may prevent complications, but further study is needed. (1)

### 2. Vitamin A
Vitamin A is suggested for:
- **Children with severe measles**
- **Children with measles in resource-limited settings**, regardless of severity (2)

Oral dosing once daily for **two days**:
- **<6 months:** 50,000 IU
- **6–11 months:** 100,000 IU
- **≥12 months:** 200,000 IU

A third dose may be given **4–6 weeks later** to children with clinical signs of severe vitamin A deficiency. (3)

### 3. Ribavirin in selected patients
Ribavirin is suggested for:
- Patients **<12 months with measles pneumonia**
- Patients **≥12 months with measles pneumonia requiring ventilatory support**
- **Immunosuppressed** patients with measles (2)

### 4. Infection control
- Use **airborne precautions** for four days after rash onset in otherwise healthy inpatients, and for the duration of illness in immunocompromised patients.
- Suspected or confirmed cases should be isolated; in outpatients, use a separate waiting area or private room, preferably with negative pressure.
- Patients and staff should wear appropriate masks/respirators.
- Patients not admitted should remain isolated at home through **four days after rash onset**.
- The room occupied by a suspected case should remain unused for **two hours** after departure.
- Suspected or confirmed cases should be reported to the local public health department. (rf_77) (4)

### 5. Post-exposure prophylaxis
- For nonimmune individuals without contraindications, administer **MMR vaccine within 72 hours of first exposure**; give a second dose at least 28 days later.
- If MMR is not given within 72 hours, **21 days of quarantine** is indicated.
- Immunocompromised individuals and nonimmune pregnant individuals should receive **IVIG within six days of exposure**. (5)
- Detailed age-specific regimens for MMR and immune globulin are provided in the referenced **postexposure prophylaxis table**. (6)

### 6. Outbreak management
- Determine immunity and vaccinate eligible nonimmune individuals without contraindications.
- In an outbreak, nonimmune individuals should receive **two MMR doses separated by at least 28 days**.
- Immune globulin should not be used for general outbreak control.
- Individuals unable to receive MMR because of pregnancy or immunocompromise should minimize exposure to outbreak settings. (7)
