## Answer

 ## **Labetalol**

- **Class/mechanism:** Combined **alpha₁-, beta₁-, and beta₂-adrenergic blocker**; it lowers blood pressure through alpha- and beta-blockade. (1) (2)
- **Uses:** Treatment of hypertension; IV labetalol is indicated for severe hypertension and is used in hypertensive emergencies. Oral labetalol may be used for chronic hypertension, although it is generally not preferred as initial monotherapy. (3) (4)
- **Onset:** Oral onset is approximately **20 minutes to 2 hours**; IV onset is within approximately **5 minutes**. (5)

### Common adult dosing examples
- **Chronic/resistant hypertension:** Oral initial dose **100 mg twice daily**, titrated every 2–3 days; usual range **200–800 mg/day in two divided doses**. (4)
- **IV hypertensive treatment:** **20 mg over 2 minutes**, followed by **20–80 mg every 10 minutes** until the target is reached; continuous infusion may use **0.5–2 mg/minute**. (4)
- **Acute aortic syndromes/dissection:** IV beta-blockade may target a heart rate of **60–80 beats/minute** and systolic blood pressure ≤120 mm Hg, with intensive monitoring. (4)

### Important precautions
Labetalol may cause **bradycardia, heart block, hypotension, orthostatic hypotension, and bronchospasm**. It should be avoided or used cautiously in patients with asthma/COPD, heart failure, bradycardia, conduction disease, hepatic impairment, peripheral vascular disease, or Raynaud disease. (2) (6) (7)

Patients receiving IV labetalol should remain supine during administration and for up to **3 hours afterward**, because symptomatic postural hypotension may occur. Monitor blood pressure, heart rate, pulse, and ECG; prolonged monitoring is needed with IV infusion. (8) (1)

In pheochromocytoma or other hyperadrenergic states, adequate alpha blockade should precede beta-blockade because inadequate alpha blockade may cause paradoxical hypertension. (7) (2)

### Pregnancy and breastfeeding
Labetalol crosses the placenta. If used during pregnancy, monitor fetal growth and monitor the newborn for 48 hours for bradycardia, hypoglycemia, and respiratory depression. Oral labetalol is listed as a preferred agent for hypertension during pregnancy, and IV labetalol is recommended for hypertensive emergencies in pregnant or postpartum patients. (1)

Labetalol is present in breast milk, but breastfeeding is generally considered compatible with therapy; monitor the infant for bradycardia, particularly if premature. (1)
