Breast milk was sampled over 24 hours following a single oral dose of linezolid 600 mg to a lactating patient (days postpartum not stated). The maximum concentration (12.36 mcg/mL) was observed 2 hours after the dose; linezolid was measurable in breast milk for up to 24 hours (Sagirli 2009).

Following administration of oral linezolid 600 mg twice daily, sampling occurred over 12 hours on days 1 and 14 of treatment. Peak concentrations of linezolid in breast milk occurred at 4 hours and 3 hours after the dose on days 1 and 14, respectively (Rowe 2014).
Linezolid serum concentrations were below the limit of detection in 1 exclusively breastfed infant during maternal therapy of linezolid 600 mg twice daily (Lim 2017).
A review article notes that severe adverse events have not been reported in infants exposed to linezolid via breast milk; however, a concern for adverse events cannot be ruled out (Algharably 2023). In general, antibiotics that are present in breast milk may cause non–dose-related modification of bowel flora. Monitor infants for GI disturbances, such as thrush or diarrhea and vomiting (WHO 2002).
Case reports describe use of linezolid for mastitis unresponsive to other antibiotics in breastfeeding as well as nonbreastfeeding women (Appalaraju 2011; Rowe 2014; Yasar 2011).
According to the manufacturer, the decision to breastfeed during therapy should consider the risk of infant exposure, the benefits of breastfeeding to the infant, and the benefits of treatment to the mother.
Dietary ConsiderationsSome products may contain sodium and/or phenylalanine. Avoid consuming large amounts of tyramine-containing foods/beverages. Some examples include aged or matured cheese, air-dried or cured meats (including sausages and salami), fava or broad bean pods, tap/draft beers, Marmite concentrate, sauerkraut, soy sauce, and other soybean condiments.
Monitoring ParametersWeekly CBC, particularly in patients at increased risk of bleeding, with pre-existing myelosuppression, on concomitant medications that cause bone marrow suppression, in those who require >2 weeks of therapy, in those with chronic infection who have received previous or concomitant antibiotic therapy, severe renal impairment, or moderate to severe hepatic impairment; more frequent monitoring may be warranted in certain scenarios based on clinical judgement (Bishop 2006; Choi 2019; Gervasoni 2015). When used for treatment of tuberculosis (TB) disease, guidelines recommend CBC at baseline, every 1 to 2 weeks for the first 6 to 8 weeks of therapy, then monthly, as well as monitoring for peripheral neuropathy and visual disturbances (ie, visual acuity, color discrimination) at baseline and monthly, through the end of therapy (ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA [Saukkonen 2025]).
Signs/symptoms of rhabdomyolysis (eg, muscle pain, tenderness, weakness; dark urine; elevated CPK).
Peripheral sensory and visual function with extended therapy (≥3 months) or in patients with new-onset neuropathic or visual symptoms, regardless of therapy length (any symptoms of visual change or impairment warrant immediate ophthalmic evaluation); in patients with renal impairment, monitor for hematopoietic (eg, anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia) and neuropathic (eg, peripheral neuropathy, optic neuritis) adverse events when administering for extended periods. Periodic serum bicarbonate with extended therapy. Consider monitoring lactic acid in patients with renal dysfunction (Mori 2018). Monitor for signs and symptoms of lactic acidosis (eg, recurrent nausea and vomiting, unexplained acidosis, low bicarbonate levels). Monitor serum sodium and for signs/symptoms of hyponatremia and/or syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH).

## Myelosuppression
Linezolidhas been associated with suppression of all blood cell lineages; the incidence is less than 1 percent and is generally dependent on dose and duration of treatment [100]. Thrombocytopenia (<100,000 platelets/microL) is the most common presentation of linezolid-induced myelosuppression [101].
Risk factors for linezolid-induced myelosuppression include low baseline blood cell counts, renal impairment, and duration oflinezolidtherapy ≥14 days [77,92,100-105]. Monitoring for myelosuppression during linezolid administration is discussed below. (See'Monitoring'below.)
Count recovery typically occurs within one to three weeks after discontinuation oflinezolid[100,106].
There are no known effective measures for prevention of linezolid-induced myelosuppression. Use of pre-emptivepyridoxinewas not associated with prevention of myelosuppression in one study including 24 patients treated with a prolonged course oflinezolid[107]. The time to recovery varies with the cell line and degree of suppression [100].
Data comparing the incidence and severity of linezolid-induced thrombocytopenia with that oftedizolidcome primarily from trials for treatment of skin and skin structure infections. In these studies, the incidence of tedizolid-associated myelosuppression was similar or lower to that observed withlinezolid[108,109].
