```
Using anticoagulant with thrombocytopenia in a neonate which anticoagulant is more safe
```

- - -

## Answer

 In a neonate with **thrombocytopenia or increased bleeding risk**, **unfractionated heparin (UFH)** is generally the safer anticoagulant option because it has a **shorter half-life** and can be **completely reversed with protamine sulfate**. (1)

**Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH/enoxaparin)** is otherwise preferred for many neonatal thromboembolic events because its pharmacokinetics are more predictable, but protamine only **partially reverses** its effect. (2) (1)

Both agents can cause bleeding, and heparin-induced thrombocytopenia is a serious possible adverse effect. Platelet count, bleeding status, indication, and thrombosis severity should therefore be assessed with neonatal hematology/thrombosis expertise. The selected references do **not** provide a specific platelet-count threshold for starting or withholding anticoagulation.
