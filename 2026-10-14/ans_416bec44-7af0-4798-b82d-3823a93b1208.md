 ## Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS): supported updates

- **Definition and presentation:** PCOS is a heterogeneous endocrine/metabolic syndrome characterized by varying combinations of **androgen excess**, **ovulatory or menstrual dysfunction**, and **polycystic ovarian morphology**. Common manifestations include irregular periods, hirsutism, acne, scalp hair loss, and sometimes obesity. (1) (2) (3)

- **Frequency:** Reported prevalence varies according to the diagnostic criteria used. Global studies generally estimate **10–13%** among unselected reproductive-age females, while other cited estimates range from approximately **5–10%** or **6.5–10%**. (1) (2) (4)

- **Metabolic and psychological associations:** PCOS is associated with increased risk of **insulin resistance, obesity, metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, sleep apnea, fatty liver, depression, cardiovascular disease, and endometrial carcinoma**. Some of these risks appear to be influenced substantially by coexisting obesity, insulin resistance, or family history rather than PCOS alone. (2) (5)

- **Fertility:** Ovulatory dysfunction can cause subfertility or infertility. Fertility medications may be required to induce ovulation in individuals with PCOS who wish to become pregnant. (2) (3)

- **Current pathophysiologic model:** The cited model identifies **functional ovarian hyperandrogenism** as the central feature in most cases. It can account for hirsutism, oligo-anovulation, and polycystic ovarian morphology. Approximately half of cases also involve insulin resistance with compensatory hyperinsulinemia, obesity, or luteinizing-hormone excess; these factors may worsen androgen production and ovulatory dysfunction. (6)

- **Genetic understanding:** PCOS is considered a complex genetic trait influenced by multiple variants and environmental factors such as diet and obesity. Potentially involved pathways include androgen production and action, gonadotropin signaling, ovarian folliculogenesis, insulin secretion and action, and weight regulation. Genome-wide association studies have identified shared genetic loci associated with PCOS, hyperandrogenism, gonadotropins, testosterone, obesity, and type 2 diabetes, although the mechanisms remain incompletely understood. (1) (7)

- **Diagnosis:** Classic presentations may include hirsutism, irregular menstrual cycles, and polycystic ovarian morphology on transvaginal ultrasound. However, there remains controversy regarding diagnostic criteria when not all classic features are present.
