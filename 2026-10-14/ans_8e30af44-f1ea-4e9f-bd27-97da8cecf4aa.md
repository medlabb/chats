 ## Classification of peritoneal tumors and their origin

### 1. **Primary epithelial Müllerian carcinomas**
These include carcinomas involving the **ovary, fallopian tube, and peritoneum**. The main histologic subtypes are:

- **High-grade serous carcinoma (HGSC)** — 70–80%
- **Endometrioid carcinoma** — about 10%
- **Clear cell carcinoma** — about 10%
- **Mucinous carcinoma** — about 3%
- **Low-grade serous carcinoma (LGSC)** — less than 5% (1)

**Likely origins:**
- **HGSC:** may originate from fallopian-tube precursors, particularly serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma (**STIC**).
- **LGSC:** may arise from endosalpingiosis or Müllerian rests.
- **Primary peritoneal serous carcinoma:** is considered to arise from **ovarian epithelial rests** or related Müllerian cells left during ovarian descent into the pelvis, rather than from mesothelial cells. (1) (2)

### 2. **Primary peritoneal carcinoma—site assignment**
When tubo-ovarian and peritoneal disease coexist, the primary site is assigned using pathologic criteria:

- **Fallopian-tube primary:** STIC, invasive mucosal carcinoma in the tube, or a tube incorporated into a tubo-ovarian mass.
- **Ovarian primary:** no STIC or invasive tubal mucosal carcinoma, with thorough examination of the tubes.
- **Peritoneal primary:** both ovaries and both tubes are grossly and microscopically normal, but peritoneal carcinoma is present. (3)

### 3. **Peritoneal mesothelioma**
**Malignant peritoneal mesothelioma** is a primary tumor arising from the **mesothelial lining cells of the peritoneum**. It usually produces diffuse peritoneal nodules, plaques, or masses. (4) (5)

Histologic subtypes are:

- **Epithelioid**
- **Sarcomatoid**
- **Biphasic/mixed**, containing both epithelioid and sarcomatoid components

Rare, generally more indolent peritoneal mesothelioma variants include:

- **Well-differentiated papillary mesothelioma**
- **Multicystic mesothelioma** (5) (4)

### 4. **Secondary metastatic tumors to the peritoneum**
Peritoneal carcinomatosis may result from tumors originating outside the peritoneum, including:

- **Ovary**
- **Urinary bladder**
- **Colon**
- **Stomach**
- **Breast**
- **Pancreas**
- **Lung**

Ovarian and bladder malignancies, as well as peritoneal mesothelioma, commonly produce peritoneal carcinomatosis. Gastrointestinal, breast, pancreatic, and lung cancers may produce peritoneal disease and/or liver metastases. (6)

### 5. **Lymphomatous involvement**
**Lymphomas** may involve the peritoneal/serosal surfaces and can cause lymphatic obstruction and chylous ascites. In primary effusion lymphoma, malignant lymphocytes may involve serosal surfaces without a detectable mass lesion. (6)
