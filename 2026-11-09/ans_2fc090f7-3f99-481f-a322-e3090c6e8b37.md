## Answer

 ## Treatment lines in autoimmune hepatitis

### **Before treatment**
Treatment is guided by symptoms, biochemical severity, liver biopsy findings, and potential treatment toxicity. Asymptomatic adults with minimal/no aminotransferase or IgG elevation and no active biopsy inflammation may not need immunosuppression. (1) (2)

### **First-line: induction of remission**
- **Prednisone/prednisolone monotherapy** is generally preferred.
  - Typical starting dose: **40–60 mg daily**, followed by gradual tapering.
  - For mild disease: **20–30 mg daily** may be used. (2) (3)
- If glucocorticoid toxicity is a concern, use **lower-dose prednisone plus an antimetabolite**, such as:
  - **Azathioprine**
  - **Mycophenolate** (2) (3)
- **Budesonide** is an alternative in patients **without cirrhosis**, usually combined with a thiopurine. (3) (4)

### **Second-line/maintenance after biochemical response**
For patients achieving complete biochemical response, maintenance with an **antimetabolite rather than long-term glucocorticoids** is generally preferred:
- **Azathioprine**
- **Mycophenolate**
- **6-mercaptopurine** may also be used. (2) (5)

Thiopurine therapy should be guided by **TPMT activity**, with monitoring for cytopenias, hepatotoxicity, and pancreatitis. (2) (6)

### **If azathioprine is not tolerated or ineffective**
- Continue **glucocorticoid monotherapy**, or
- Substitute another antimetabolite, most commonly **mycophenolate**. (7)

### **Refractory or treatment-intolerant disease**
Other agents that may be used include:
- **Tacrolimus**
- **Cyclosporine**
- Rarely, **infliximab** or **rituximab**. (4)

### **Severe disease and cirrhosis**
- Patients with **active disease and cirrhosis** may receive prednisone/prednisolone-based therapy. Drug therapy is generally not used for cirrhosis without active inflammation. (8)
- **Budesonide is contraindicated in cirrhosis.** (6)
- Decompensated cirrhosis or very severe disease may require **liver-transplant evaluation**. (2) (4)

The selected references do not provide a single formal “first-, second-, and third-line” treatment algorithm beyond these induction, maintenance, and rescue strategies.
