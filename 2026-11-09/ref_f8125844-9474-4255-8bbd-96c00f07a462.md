## Paracentesis for large-volume ascites
If the patient has tense ascites and/or bothersome symptoms (eg, abdominal pain from distension), we suggest an initial therapeutic paracentesis in addition to diuretics. We typically remove 4 to 5 L of ascites, which usually results in more rapid improvement in symptoms compared with diuretic therapy alone. Removal of less than 5 liters of fluid does not appear to have hemodynamic or hormonal consequences, and post-paracentesis colloid infusion does not appear to be necessary [51].
For larger paracenteses (>5 L), albumin (6 to 8 g/L of fluid removed) should be administered [52]. The rationale for this is discussed in detail elsewhere. (See"Ascites in adults with cirrhosis: Refractory ascites".)
We prefer to treat diuretic-sensitive patients with diet and diuretics and to reserve serial large volume paracentesis (LVP) for patients who are truly diuretic resistant. Repeated LVP may result in protein and complement depletion. In theory, this may indirectly predispose to ascitic fluid infection, though no definitive data are available [53,54].

## Patients awaiting or ineligible for definitive treatment

### Serial large-volume paracentesis

- **Volume and frequency**— Generally, the goal with LVP is to remove as much fluid as possible without excessive volume shifts. Patients who are consuming 88 mEq (2000 mg) of sodium per day and are excreting no sodium in the urine should require paracentesis of approximately 8 liters every two weeks  (table 1) [5]. Patients who need removal of larger volumes may be consuming more sodium. (See"Diagnostic and large-volume abdominal paracentesis".)

- Scheduling paracentesis every two weeks works well in our experience. The goal is to avoid a tense abdomen since this can lead to discomfort, decreased appetite, and decreased oral intake due to gastric compression. Exact volumes can vary based on the patient’s body habitus, and some patients may require more frequent paracentesis for comfort.

- **Routine fluid tests**— We obtain an ascites fluid cell count and differential from each paracentesis to rule out spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP). We obtain ascites fluid cultures only if the fluid is cloudy or the patient has symptoms suggestive of SBP because the prevalence of occult ascitic fluid infection in asymptomatic outpatients undergoing LVP for refractory ascites is low [25,26]. (See"Evaluation of adults with ascites", section on 'Cell count and differential'.)

- **Albumin infusion**— If a paracentesis removed more than 5 L of ascites fluid, we administeralbumin solution(6 to 8 g for every L of fluid removed) to reduce the risk of post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction (PCD) [27-30].

- There are several formulations ofalbumin solutionavailable. Albumin 5 percent and 25 percent solutions are available in the United States; the 25 percent solution is most commonly used. Both solutions are isotonic, with sodium concentrations of 130 to 160 mEq/L. The 5 percent solution provides five times the sodium load of the 25 percent solution. The 25 percent solution is typically given if the patient is hypervolemic, and the 5 percent solution if dehydration is suspected. Other concentrations (eg, 4 percent, 4.5 percent, or 20 percent) are available outside the United States; the 20 percent formulation is most commonly used in Europe and other countries where the 25 percent solution is not available.

- No high-quality trial has shown a direct survival advantage of post-paracentesis volume expanders, such as albumin, compared with placebo (or no expansion). One meta-analysis of randomized trials demonstrated lower mortality withalbumin solutioninfusion compared with alternate volume expanders (eg, dextran, gelatin,hydroxyethyl starch, and hypertonicsaline) (12 versus 14.4 percent, OR 0.64; 95% CI 0.41-0.98), but risk of bias was an issue with many included trials [31].

- Albumin administration reduces the risk of PCD. In a randomized trial of 105 patients with tense ascites undergoing LVP, those who received albumin (10 g/L of ascites removed) were less likely to show signs of hemodynamic deterioration compared with those who did not receive albumin [32]. Patients who received albumin were also less likely to develop hyponatremia or kidney impairment (2 percent versus 21 percent).
