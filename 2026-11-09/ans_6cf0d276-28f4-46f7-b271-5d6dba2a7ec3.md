 ## Autoimmune hepatitis (adults): treatment lines

### **Before treatment / general measures**
- Refer to **hepatology** for long-term management.
- Check **TPMT activity**, hepatitis B serologies, and bone density before immunosuppression.
- Vaccinate against **hepatitis A and B** if nonimmune; use standard age-appropriate vaccinations.
- Avoid alcohol, particularly heavy alcohol use. (1)

Patients with minimal or no aminotransferase/IgG elevation and no active inflammation on biopsy may be observed without immunosuppression. (1)

### **First-line induction therapy**
- **Prednisone or prednisolone monotherapy:** usually **40–60 mg daily**, followed by gradual taper.
- For mild disease, such as asymptomatic disease with aminotransferases below 10 times the upper limit of normal, **20–30 mg daily** may be used. (2; 1)

An alternative is lower-dose glucocorticoid plus an antimetabolite, especially when steroid toxicity is a concern:

- Prednisone around **30 mg daily**, then after approximately 2 weeks add:
  - **Azathioprine**, if TPMT activity is normal or intermediate, or
  - **Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF)**. (2)

Initial therapy may also include a glucocorticoid with **azathioprine or 6-mercaptopurine**. (3; 4)

### **Alternative first-line steroid**
- **Budesonide** may be used in selected patients **without cirrhosis**, usually with a thiopurine.
- It should not be used in cirrhosis and is not appropriate as rescue therapy after failure of prednisone.
- Initial dose described: **3 mg three times daily**, later reduced to twice daily after biochemical response. (5)

### **Second-line / management of nonresponse**
Nonresponse is suggested by less than a **50% fall in aminotransferases after 4 weeks**. First confirm adherence and exclude other causes or overlap disease. (6)

Depending on the initial regimen:

- **Steroid monotherapy:** add azathioprine, for example **50 mg daily**, or MMF **500 mg twice daily**. (6)
- **Prednisone plus azathioprine:** increase azathioprine gradually up to **2 mg/kg/day**, maximum **200 mg/day**, if tolerated. If inadequate, increase prednisone to **40–60 mg/day**. (6)
- If already taking azathioprine, thiopurine metabolite levels may help guide dose adjustment. (6)

### **Maintenance therapy after biochemical remission**
Once complete biochemical response is achieved, maintenance with an **antimetabolite**—usually azathioprine or MMF—is preferred over chronic glucocorticoid therapy to reduce steroid toxicity. (1)

For patients responding to prednisone plus azathioprine:

- Gradually taper prednisone to approximately **10 mg/day or the lowest effective dose**.
- Continue azathioprine at the established dose, with later dose reduction considered after sustained response. (6)

### **Refractory or intolerant disease**
Options described for patients who fail or cannot tolerate standard therapy include:

- **Mycophenolate mofetil:** commonly **1 g orally twice daily**, starting at 250–500 mg every 12 hours and increasing as tolerated. Monitor blood counts. It is teratogenic and generally should not be used during pregnancy. (7)
- **Tacrolimus** or **cyclosporine:** may be considered in selected refractory cases, but evidence is limited and adverse effects include hypertension, renal insufficiency, hyperlipidemia, and infection. (5; 8)
- Other reported but non-routine options include **sirolimus, methotrexate, infliximab, and rituximab** because efficacy and safety data are limited. (5)

### **Liver transplantation**
- Evaluate for **liver transplantation** in patients with **decompensated cirrhosis** or very severe disease. (1; 8)

### **Treatment goals and duration**
The goals are symptom improvement, reduced hepatic inflammation, normalization of aminotransferases and IgG, and prevention of progression. (1)

Treatment is generally continued until remission, treatment failure, or unacceptable toxicity. Relapse is common after withdrawal, so withdrawal should be considered only after sustained biochemical response; patients with prior nonresponse often require long-term maintenance. (6; 8)
