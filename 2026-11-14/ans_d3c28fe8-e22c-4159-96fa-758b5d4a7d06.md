 ## Tic disorders

**Tics** are rapid, recurrent, nonrhythmic motor movements or vocalizations that are involuntary or semivoluntary and sudden in onset. They may be **simple**—involving one muscle group or sound—or **complex**, involving coordinated movements or multiple sounds. (1)

### Examples

| Motor tics | Vocal tics |
|---|---|
| Eye blinking, shoulder shrugging, facial movements, sustained eye closure | Throat clearing, coughing, sniffing, blowing |
| Touching, jumping, hitting, gestures | Syllables or words, phrases, repeating one’s or others’ words |
| Tensing abdominal or limb muscles | Barking, changes in speech rate, rhythm, or volume |

These examples are also summarized in **Table 47–1, “Examples of Tics.”** (1)

Tics often occur in bouts and may change in frequency, location, number, complexity, and severity. They can be temporarily suppressed, although suppression may be followed by increased tic activity. Concentration may reduce tics, whereas stress, excitement, boredom, fatigue, heat, or relaxation after stress may worsen them. Many individuals experience a preceding uncomfortable urge or “just right” sensation. (1)

### DSM-5-TR categories

- **Provisional tic disorder:** one or more motor and/or vocal tics for **less than 1 year**.
- **Persistent motor or vocal tic disorder:** motor or vocal tics, but not both, for **more than 1 year**.
- **Tourette disorder/syndrome:** multiple motor tics and at least one vocal tic for **more than 1 year**.
- **Other specified** or **unspecified tic disorder:** symptoms do not meet the criteria for another category. (2)

For these diagnoses, tics must begin before age 18, must not be caused by a substance or medical condition, and typically wax and wane in frequency. (3)

### Course and associated conditions

Tourette disorder commonly begins with simple motor tics around age 6–7; vocal tics often begin around ages 8–9. Symptoms frequently improve by early to mid-adolescence and are greatly diminished in most individuals by early adulthood. (4)

Commonly associated psychiatric difficulties include **ADHD, obsessive-compulsive symptoms or OCD, anxiety, mood symptoms, aggressive dyscontrol, and learning or academic problems**. (4) (5)

### Treatment

Mild tics that do not cause significant distress or impairment may require only active monitoring. Treatment is considered when tics cause pain or discomfort, interfere with social interactions, or impair educational, occupational, or daily functioning. (6)

**Behavioral treatment is the cornerstone and first-line approach**, particularly for mild-to-moderate tics. Medication may be considered when behavioral interventions are insufficient or when symptoms significantly interfere with functioning. Treatment is individualized and aims to reduce distress and improve control and functioning rather than necessarily eliminate every tic. (6)
