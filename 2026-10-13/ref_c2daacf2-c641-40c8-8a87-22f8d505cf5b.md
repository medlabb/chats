#### E. Clinical Use

Fluoroquinolones are effective in the treatment of infections of the genitourinary and gastrointestinal tracts caused by gram-negative organisms, including *E coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, C jejuni, Enterobacter, P aeruginosa, Salmonella,* and *Shigella species*. They have been used widely for respiratory tract, skin, and soft tissue infections, but their effectiveness is now variable because of the emergence of resistance. [Ciprofloxacin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660460) and [ofloxacin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660532) in single oral doses have been used as alternatives to [ceftriaxone](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660417) or [cefixime](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660477) in gonorrhea, but they are not currently recommended because resistance is now common. [Ofloxacin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660532) eradicates *Chlamydia trachomatis*, but a 7-d course of treatment is required. [Levofloxacin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658799) has activity against most organisms associated with community-acquired pneumonia, including chlamydiae, mycoplasma, and legionella species. The later generations (eg, [moxifloxacin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658882), [delafloxacin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659331)) have the widest spectrum of activity, which includes both gram-positive and gram-negative organisms, atypical pneumonia agents, and some anaerobic bacteria. Fluoroquinolones have also been used in the meningococcal carrier state, in the treatment of tuberculosis, and in prophylactic management of neutropenic patients.

#### F. Toxicity

Gastrointestinal distress is the most common adverse effect, including an increased risk for opportunistic infections caused by *C difficile*. Neurologic adverse effects also have been described including headache, dizziness, insomnia, altered mental status, and peripheral neuropathy. Tendonopathy, including tendinitis and tendon rupture, can occur early in the course of therapy; therefore, the fluoroquinolones are not recommended for children, the elderly, or pregnant individuals because they may damage growing cartilage and cause arthropathy. Fluoroquinolones may also cause skin rashes, abnormal liver function tests, hypoglycemia, and phototoxicity. [Ciprofloxacin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660460) inhibits CYP1A2, which may increase the plasma levels of certain drugs (eg, methylxanthines, [clozapine](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660480)), enhancing their toxicity. Certain fluoroquinolones ([levofloxacin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658799), [moxifloxacin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658882)) prolong the QTc interval. They should be avoided in patients with known QTc prolongation and those taking certain antiarrhythmic drugs (see [Chapter 14](/content.aspx?legacysectionid=katzrev14_ch14)) or other drugs that increase the QTc interval.
