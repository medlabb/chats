```
Can levofloxacin cause severe QT prolongation if pt already have it, or it can be given but with caution
```

- - -

## Answer

 Yes. **Levofloxacin can prolong the QT interval and has been associated with ventricular arrhythmias, including torsades de pointes (TdP)**, although its QT effect may be lower than that of some other fluoroquinolones. (1)

If the patient already has **marked baseline QT prolongation—particularly QTc >500 ms—or a history of drug-induced TdP**, the risk is higher. Other risk factors include hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypocalcemia, bradycardia, structural heart disease, renal/hepatic impairment, older age, and use of multiple QT-prolonging drugs. (1)

Therefore, in a patient with significant pre-existing QT prolongation, **an alternative antibiotic should generally be considered rather than simply giving levofloxacin “with caution.”** If use is unavoidable, the references support careful review of interacting medications and monitoring for QTc prolongation and ventricular arrhythmias; combinations with high-risk QT-prolonging antiarrhythmics are listed as **avoid**. (2) (3)

The selected references do not provide a definitive QTc cutoff at which levofloxacin is absolutely contraindicated, beyond identifying baseline QTc >500 ms as a major risk factor.
