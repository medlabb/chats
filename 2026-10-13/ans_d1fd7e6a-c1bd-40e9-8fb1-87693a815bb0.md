## Answer

 ## Treatment of hypertension in children

### 1. Confirm persistent hypertension
Blood pressure should be measured on **three separate occasions** before starting an intervention strategy. Proper cuff size and technique are important. (1)

### 2. Lifestyle modification
For children with **asymptomatic mild hypertension without target-organ damage**, initial treatment is usually lifestyle modification:

- **Weight loss**, especially when hypertension is obesity-related  
- Diet rich in fresh fruits, vegetables, fiber, and nonfat dairy  
- Reduced sodium intake; the **DASH diet** may be used  
- Aerobic exercise for **30–60 minutes on most days**
- Reduce sedentary activity to **less than 2 hours daily** (2)

### 3. When to start medication
Pharmacologic treatment is indicated for:

- Symptomatic hypertension  
- Stage 2 hypertension without a modifiable risk factor  
- Hypertension with **diabetes or chronic kidney disease (CKD)**
- Persistent hypertension despite lifestyle measures (2)

Medication is generally started as **one drug at a low dose**, then increased until the BP goal is reached. If the maximum dose is reached or adverse effects occur, a second medication from another class may be added. (2)

### 4. Initial medication choices
Acceptable initial agents include:

- **ACE inhibitors**
- **Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs)**
- **Thiazide diuretics**
- **Calcium channel blockers** (2)

The choice should be tailored to the cause of hypertension when possible. Recommended pediatric doses are listed in **Table 494.9**. (3)

For children with **CKD**, ACE inhibitors or ARBs are preferred, particularly with proteinuria, with close monitoring of renal function and electrolytes. Thiazide or loop diuretics may help when salt and fluid retention contribute to hypertension. (4)

### 5. Treatment target
Treatment should generally achieve:

- **BP below the 90th percentile for age**, or  
- **Below 130/80 mmHg**, whichever is lower  

For children and adolescents with CKD, a lower ambulatory BP target—24-hour mean arterial pressure below the 50th percentile—is recommended. (3)

### 6. Severe or emergency hypertension
Severe hypertension accompanied by symptoms such as headache, vomiting, visual changes, seizures, encephalopathy, heart failure, or acute kidney injury requires **ICU admission and carefully monitored intravenous treatment**. Options include IV labetalol, nicardipine, or sodium nitroprusside. BP should be lowered gradually; overly rapid reduction may compromise organ perfusion. (5)
