## Answer

 **Budd–Chiari syndrome** is hepatic venous outflow obstruction involving one or more major hepatic veins and/or the intrahepatic or suprahepatic inferior vena cava. (1)

### Diagnostic approach

1. **Consider the diagnosis clinically** in patients with acute liver failure, acute hepatitis, or chronic liver disease—especially when risk factors for Budd–Chiari syndrome are present. Symptoms may include abdominal pain, ascites, hepatomegaly, gastrointestinal bleeding, jaundice, or hepatic encephalopathy; some subacute or chronic cases are asymptomatic. (2) (3)

2. **First-line test: Doppler ultrasonography**
   - It can usually establish the diagnosis noninvasively.
   - Evaluate the portal and splenic circulation as well, to assess for concurrent portal or splenic vein thrombosis.
   - Suggestive findings include absent or abnormal hepatic-vein flow, reversed or turbulent flow, nonvisualization or narrowing of hepatic veins, intrahepatic collaterals, and a “spider-web” appearance near the hepatic-vein openings. (4) (2)

3. **Contrast-enhanced CT or MRI**
   - Use these to confirm the diagnosis, assist with treatment planning, or when Doppler ultrasonography is unavailable or nondiagnostic despite high clinical suspicion.
   - Findings may include absent or delayed filling of the hepatic veins, hepatic parenchymal enhancement abnormalities, inferior vena cava narrowing or nonopacification, reduced-caliber hepatic veins, and comma-shaped intrahepatic collaterals. (4) (5)

4. **Venography**
   - Perform it when noninvasive studies are negative or nondiagnostic but clinical suspicion remains high.
   - It can confirm the diagnosis, define the involved vessels, measure venous pressure gradients, and assist in planning treatment. Hepatic venography is described as the diagnostic gold standard. (5)

5. **Liver biopsy**
   - It is generally **not required**, because the diagnosis can usually be made with imaging.
   - It may be considered when the diagnosis remains unclear or in selected subacute cases where assessment of fibrosis, cirrhosis, or severe congestion would affect treatment decisions. (5)

6. **Evaluate for predisposing conditions** after diagnosis, including prothrombotic disorders and other underlying inflammatory, immunologic, or thrombotic conditions. (6) (7)
