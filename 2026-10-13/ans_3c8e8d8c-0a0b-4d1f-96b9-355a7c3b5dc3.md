## Answer

 ## Frontal fibrosing alopecia (FFA)

**Frontal fibrosing alopecia** is a primary lymphocytic **cicatricial (scarring) alopecia** that usually causes progressive, symmetric, band-like hair loss along the frontal and temporal hairline. It occurs most often in postmenopausal women and may result in permanent, disfiguring hair loss. (1) (2)

### Clinical features
- Recession of the frontotemporal hairline, often in a band measuring approximately **1–8 cm**. (3)
- Loss of visible follicular openings within the affected area. (4)
- Perifollicular redness or scale, hypopigmentation, itching, burning, tingling, tenderness, or trichodynia. (3) (4)
- **“Lonely hair sign”**: isolated hairs remaining within the band of alopecia; this is shown in picture 2D and picture 1A. (3) (4)
- Eyebrow loss—often involving the lateral eyebrows—may occur before scalp hairline recession. Eyelash and other body-hair loss are also possible. (5)
- Facial papules, particularly on the temples, cheeks, or chin, may occur, along with facial erythema or pigmented macules. (5)

### Diagnosis
Diagnosis is suspected from the clinical pattern and associated findings, but **a biopsy is necessary to confirm FFA**. Dermoscopy may be a useful additional tool. (4)

### Pathogenesis
The cause is not well understood. Scalp pathology typically shows a dense inflammatory infiltrate targeting the hair follicle, including the follicular bulge; damage to this region is thought to contribute to permanent hair loss. Genetic, hormonal, immune, fibrotic, and environmental factors have been proposed. (6)
