```
regarding rectal cancer, what is the difference between high, low and ultra low anterior resection?
In which circumstances APR is done instead of low anterior resection?
```

- - -

## Answer

 The selected references do not provide formal anatomical definitions distinguishing **high**, **low**, and **ultra-low anterior resection**. They do support the following distinctions:

- **Anterior resection/LAR:** Partial or total rectal resection followed by restoration of bowel continuity with either a **colorectal or coloanal anastomosis**. (1)
- **Upper and middle rectal tumors:** Usually amenable to a sphincter-sparing procedure such as LAR if a curative resection and adequate anorectal function can be preserved. (2)
- **Ultra-low rectal cancer:** The references mention techniques involving **intersphincteric dissection and a coloanal anastomosis**, but do not define a specific distance from the anal verge for “ultra-low” resection. (1)
- For upper rectal cancers, a tumor-specific mesorectal excision with approximately **5 cm of distal mesorectum** is described; for other tumors, the required distal margin may be **2 cm**, or **1 cm** in selected cases after total mesorectal excision. (3) (4)

**APR instead of LAR is performed when:**

- An adequate **negative distal margin cannot be achieved** with sphincter-preserving surgery. (5)
- The required gross distal margin—generally **1–2 cm**, depending on tumor location and treatment context—cannot be obtained; this is an indication to convert LAR to APR, which extends the resection to the anus. (4)
- The tumor is in the **lower/distal rectum**, particularly within approximately **5 cm of the anal verge**, and curative resection cannot be achieved while preserving the sphincter. (2)
- The patient has **poor pre-existing anorectal sphincter function**; in this situation APR with a permanent colostomy is preferred over aggressive sphincter-preserving surgery. (6)
- Locally advanced disease involves the **anal sphincter complex**, levator muscles, adjacent pelvic organs, or bones, requiring a wider or multivisceral resection. Low tumors involving the levators may require an extralevator APR. (7) (2)

For selected very low tumors, **intersphincteric resection** with coloanal anastomosis may provide an alternative to APR when the tumor does not involve the external sphincter or the intersphincteric plane, although postoperative continence may be worse. (8)
