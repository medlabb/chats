 ## Management of cold sores in children

### Supportive care
- **Encourage fluids** to prevent dehydration. A child unable to drink enough may need hospitalization for intravenous fluids. (1)
- Give **acetaminophen or ibuprofen** for pain if the child can take them. Severe pain preventing sleep may require oral opioids under medical supervision. (1)
- Apply a **barrier cream such as petroleum jelly** to the lips to help prevent lip adhesions, particularly in young children with gingivostomatitis. (1)
- Avoid rubbing the sore; rubbing may worsen it or spread it to nearby skin. Wash hands before and after applying treatment. (2)

### Antiviral treatment
- For **primary herpetic gingivostomatitis** in an otherwise healthy child, topical antivirals such as acyclovir, penciclovir, or docosanol are **not recommended** because they are not helpful. (3)
- If there is severe pain or severely reduced fluid intake and symptoms began within **96 hours**, a clinician may prescribe oral acyclovir. The referenced regimen is **15 mg/kg by mouth, maximum 200 mg per dose, five times daily for 5–7 days**. Treatment may shorten symptoms by approximately 3–6 days. (4)
- For presentation **96 hours or more after onset**, the referenced approach is supportive care; intravenous fluids may be required if dehydration develops. (4)
- In **immunocompromised children**, oral or intravenous acyclovir is recommended; severe or potentially life-threatening disease may require intravenous treatment. (5)

### Recurrent cold sores
For recurrent cutaneous lesions, the referenced approach includes oral acyclovir started early during each recurrence. Long-term suppressive acyclovir may be considered for frequent, painful, or disruptive recurrences. These decisions and doses should be made by the child’s clinician. (6)

The selected references do not provide further detailed guidance on routine school or daycare exclusion, specific topical cold-sore products for children younger than 12 years, or individualized treatment decisions.
