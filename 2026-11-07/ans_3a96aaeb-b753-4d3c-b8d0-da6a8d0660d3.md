## Answer

 **DVT treatment** primarily involves **therapeutic anticoagulation** to prevent the clot from enlarging, new clots from forming, and clot migration to the lungs. Anticoagulants do not directly dissolve the existing clot; they allow the body time to remove it naturally. (1) (2)

- **Initial anticoagulation:** For most patients, a direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC)—such as **rivaroxaban, apixaban, dabigatran, or edoxaban**—is an option. Other choices include injectable low-molecular-weight heparin, fondaparinux, or heparin. The choice depends on medical history, kidney function, preferences, and cost. (3)
- **Duration:** Anticoagulation is recommended for a **minimum of 3 months**. Treatment may be limited to about 3 months when the DVT was associated with a reversible risk factor; people without a known risk factor may require longer or indefinite treatment after discussing benefits and bleeding risks with their clinician. (3)
- **Warfarin:** Requires regular blood tests to monitor clotting. DOACs generally do not require this type of monitoring. (3)
- **Bleeding precautions:** Take the medication exactly as prescribed, follow instructions regarding diet and other medicines, and promptly report abnormal bleeding. (2) (4)
- **IVC filter:** May be considered when anticoagulants cannot be taken or are insufficiently effective. (4)
- **Thrombolysis or thrombectomy:** Not routinely used for most acute lower-extremity DVT. They are generally reserved for severe cases such as **phlegmasia cerulea dolens**, massive iliofemoral DVT, or failure of therapeutic anticoagulation. (5)
- **Activity:** In stable, fully anticoagulated patients whose symptoms are controlled, early ambulation is preferred over bed rest. Routine compression stockings are not recommended solely to prevent post-thrombotic syndrome, though they may help symptoms or established post-thrombotic syndrome. (6)

The specific medication and duration should be determined by a clinician based on the individual clinical situation.
