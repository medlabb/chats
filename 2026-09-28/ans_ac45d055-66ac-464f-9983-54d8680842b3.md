## Answer

 This neonate has **systemic features** (fever, poor feeding, elevated CRP) and **pustules at multiple sites**, so this should be managed as neonatal SSTI requiring hospitalization, close monitoring, parenteral antibiotics, and drainage of any fluctuant or abscess-like lesions. (1) (2)

- **Continue parenteral therapy while awaiting the pus culture and susceptibility report.** Neonates with more-than-localized SSTI are generally treated parenterally until 48-hour culture results are available. (2)  
- A “no growth” blood culture does not establish the cause of the skin lesions; the **lesion culture** is important because *S. aureus* is the commonest pathogen in neonatal SSTI. (3)
- The empiric regimen should be guided by the **local susceptibility pattern**, and should be narrowed once the organism and sensitivities are known. The selected references do not provide enough information to decide whether to add or substitute cloxacillin in this specific baby. (4)
- **Do not add cloxacillin solely because lesions remain painful at 48 hours** if the infant is otherwise improving; reassess the lesions carefully, ensure adequate sampling/drainage of purulent lesions, and review the regimen with the neonatologist/microbiology team when the report arrives. - If the lesions are confined to skin and soft tissue, the usual **total treatment duration is 7–14 days**, depending on clinical response and absence of complications. (4)
