## Answer

 ## Migraine treatment

Management combines **trigger avoidance**, lifestyle regularity, and—when needed—**acute (symptomatic)** and **preventive** medication. Common triggers include stress, irregular sleep, missed meals, odors, alcohol, bright light, loud noise, menstruation, and oral contraceptives. Regular sleep, meals, and hydration may help; a headache diary can identify triggers. (1) (2)

### Acute treatment

- Rest in a **quiet, dark room**.
- Take a simple analgesic early, such as **aspirin, acetaminophen, ibuprofen, or naproxen**. Limit simple analgesics to **15 days or fewer per month** and other acute medications to **10 days or fewer per month** to reduce medication-overuse headache. (3)
- **Triptans**—including sumatriptan, zolmitriptan, rizatriptan, naratriptan, almotriptan, frovatriptan, and eletriptan—are options for acute attacks. Combining a triptan with **naproxen 500 mg** may provide greater benefit. (3)
- **Lasmiditan** is an option without the vasoconstrictive properties of triptans; dizziness and sleepiness are common, and driving should be avoided for 8 hours after taking it. (2)
- **CGRP antagonists** for acute treatment include rimegepant, ubrogepant, and zavegepant. (2)
- For nausea or emergency-department treatment, **prochlorperazine** or intravenous **metoclopramide** may be useful. **Opioids should be avoided** because of rebound headache and medication-overuse headache. (2)
- Noninvasive options include single-pulse transcranial magnetic stimulation, vagus-nerve stimulation, transcutaneous trigeminal stimulation, and remote electrical stimulation. (2)

**Important restrictions:** Triptans should be avoided in pregnancy, hemiplegic or basilar migraine, prior stroke or transient ischemic attack, and uncontrolled hypertension; they are contraindicated with coronary or peripheral vascular disease and Prinzmetal angina. Ergotamine preparations should be avoided during pregnancy, in cardiovascular disease or its risk factors, and with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors. (3) (2)

### Preventive treatment

Prevention may be considered when attacks occur **more than two or three times per month** or cause substantial disability. Options include: (2)

- **Topiramate** or **valproic acid**
- **Propranolol**, candesartan, guanfacine, or verapamil
- **Amitriptyline** or venlafaxine
- CGRP-targeted treatments such as **erenumab, fremanezumab, galcanezumab, eptinezumab, atogepant, or rimegepant**
- **Acupuncture**, biofeedback, magnesium, riboflavin, coenzyme Q10, botulinum toxin A, or neuromodulation devices
