## Answer

 **Wound dressings** may be used to:

- Protect a wound and absorb drainage; sterile material is recommended for lacerations. (1)
- Keep abrasions clean and moist; topical antibiotic ointment or protective dressings may be appropriate. (1)
- Manage wounds healing by secondary intention, including silver or other antimicrobial dressings, wet-to-dry dressings, and negative-pressure wound therapy. (2)
- Provide negative-pressure therapy: a sponge is placed in the wound, sealed with a dressing, and connected to suction. This promotes granulation tissue, removes interstitial fluid, and draws wound edges together. (3)
- Treat contaminated burns, for which topical antibiotics and absorptive dressings are useful. (4)

Primarily closed wounds are generally re-epithelialized within **24–48 hours**, when a surgical dressing may be removed. (5)
