 ## Onychomycosis management

- **Oral antifungal therapy is usually required** for complete cure, except occasionally in classic superficial white onychomycosis. For toenail disease, combined clinical and mycologic cure rates after approximately 12 weeks are about **20%–40%**, despite higher reported mycologic cure rates. (1)
- FDA-approved topical options for **mild-to-moderate disease** require daily treatment for **48 weeks**:
  - **Efinaconazole 10% solution:** combined cure rate about **15%–20%**
  - **Tavaborole 5% solution:** about **6%–10%**
  - **Ciclopirox olamine 8% solution:** about **6%–10%** (1)
- Application schedules:
  - Ciclopirox: apply daily for 1 week, remove with alcohol, and repeat for 48 weeks.
  - Efinaconazole and tavaborole: apply daily for 48 weeks without periodic removal. (2)
- Suggested adult systemic regimens in **Table 77.13** include:
  - **Terbinafine:** 250 mg daily for 12 weeks for toenails, or 6 weeks for fingernails.
  - **Itraconazole:** 200 mg daily for 12 weeks, or pulse therapy for 3–4 months in toenail disease; shorter courses are listed for fingernails.
  - **Fluconazole:** 150–450 mg weekly until the nails are clear. (3)
- **Terbinafine** has reported mycologic cure rates of approximately 70% for toenails and 80% for fingernails; recurrence is significant, particularly in toenail disease. Terbinafine showed a higher combined clinical and mycologic cure rate than itraconazole after 12 weeks of continuous therapy in one comparison. (4)
- Factors associated with more severe disease and poorer treatment response are listed in **Table 77.14**, including matrix involvement, total dystrophic disease, involvement of more than 50% of the nail bed, thick subungual hyperkeratosis, slow nail growth, immunosuppression, peripheral arterial disease, and poorly controlled diabetes. (1)
- Recurrence is common. Preventive measures include **breathable footwear and socks, antifungal or absorbent powders, frequent nail clipping, avoiding barefoot exposure in locker rooms, and discarding or disinfecting old shoes**. (1)
- Combination topical corticosteroid–antifungal products are associated with an unacceptable failure rate in dermatophyte infections because of corticosteroid-related effects and treatment-duration limitations. Confirmatory testing and antifungal stewardship are emphasized, particularly because terbinafine-resistant dermatophytes have emerged. (1)
