## Answer

 CKD management aims to **treat its cause, slow progression, manage complications, and plan for kidney replacement therapy when needed**:

- **Slow progression:** Treat the underlying cause, control blood pressure, manage cardiovascular risk factors, pursue weight loss if obesity is present, and treat metabolic acidosis. If appropriate, antiproteinuric treatment may include an ACE inhibitor or ARB, an MRA, an SGLT-2 inhibitor, or a combination. Monitor laboratory results after starting or adjusting these medicines; an early eGFR dip may occur, but treatment can continue if kidney function stabilizes and there is no concerning hyperkalemia. (1)
- **Diet:** Arrange assessment by a renal dietitian for individualized guidance. The reference describes protein intake of 0.6–0.8 g/kg/day as potentially slowing progression, a usual sodium goal of 2 g/day, and potassium restriction when GFR is below 10–20 mL/min/1.73 m² or earlier with hyperkalemia. In advanced CKD, lower elevated phosphorus toward normal; dietary restriction alone may not suffice in ESKD. (2)
- **Medication safety:** Adjust drug doses for GFR and avoid nephrotoxins such as NSAIDs and intravenous contrast. The reference also advises avoiding magnesium- or phosphorus-containing medicines and using PPIs only when medically necessary and for short courses. (rf_86)
- **Manage complications:** For CKD mineral-bone disorders, correct calcium, phosphorus, and 25-OH vitamin D toward normal and mitigate hyperparathyroidism. For anemia, evaluate treatable causes and iron status; iron or an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent may be considered as described in the reference. (3; 4)
- **Advanced disease:** When GFR declines to 5–10 mL/min/1.73 m², kidney replacement therapy options include hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, or transplantation. (rf_86)
