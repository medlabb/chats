## Answer

 For **acute cerebral edema associated with intracerebral hemorrhage**, initial management includes airway support and ventilatory assistance if needed (1). **IV mannitol or hypertonic saline** may be used to reduce edema, generally as a temporary measure while surgery is considered; medical treatment alone rarely changes the eventual outcome (2).

Table 3-6 lists **mannitol 1.5–2 g/kg IV over 30 minutes to 1 hour**, or hypertonic saline as a **3% continuous infusion** or a **20 mL bolus of 23.4% or 29.2% solution**, with a stated serum sodium target of **145–155 mEq/L** (3). Surgical clot evacuation is indicated for cerebellar hemorrhage with neurologic deterioration, brainstem compression, or hydrocephalus, and may be appropriate for superficial hemorrhage causing mass effect (3).

If you mean **idiopathic intracranial hypertension**, the reference identifies acetazolamide as mainstay treatment; refractory cases may require optic nerve sheath fenestration or shunting (4).
