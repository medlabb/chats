 ## Iron-deficiency anemia (IDA): diagnosis and treatment

**The selected references provide pediatric-focused guidance and do not identify a specific 2026 guideline.**

### Diagnosis in children
- IDA is defined by **low hemoglobin plus low ferritin**. The references use ferritin **<15 µg/L** and anemia thresholds of hemoglobin **<11 g/dL** for ages 6 months to <5 years, and **<11.5 g/dL** for ages 5 to <12 years. (1)
- For a typical nutritional presentation in a child under 3 with dietary risk factors, obtain a **CBC**; ferritin can add useful information. For an atypical presentation, consider CBC with indices, reticulocyte count, ferritin, and peripheral smear; stool occult blood testing may also be considered. (2)
- Low MCV and elevated RDW commonly support IDA. A normal MCV does not rule it out when anemia and clinical risk factors are present. Ferritin can be misleadingly normal or elevated with inflammation or infection. (3) (2)

### Treatment and follow-up in children
- For presumed IDA, use **oral iron plus dietary counseling**. A suggested regimen is ferrous sulfate providing **3 mg/kg elemental iron once daily**, in the morning or between meals. Avoid milk or dairy for about one hour before and two hours after the dose. (rf_a0) (4)
- Recheck a CBC to assess response: generally within **four weeks** for mild anemia and **one to two weeks** for moderate-to-severe anemia. A hemoglobin rise of **>1 g/dL within four weeks** in mild anemia, or within two weeks in moderate-to-severe anemia, supports response. (4)
- If responding, continue treatment for **at least three months** and generally for at least one month after hemoglobin normalizes; retest the CBC. Reassess if the response is inadequate, including adherence and dosing, diet, the diagnosis, blood loss, or malabsorption. (rf_a0) (4)
- Dietary counseling includes adequate iron-rich foods and limiting milk to **less than 20 oz/day** in children over 12 months. (rf_a0)
