## Answer

 **Diminished ovarian reserve (DOR)** means a woman of reproductive age has fewer or lower-quality remaining eggs than expected, often showing up as reduced fertility or a lower response to ovarian stimulation despite regular periods. There is no universally accepted diagnostic definition. (1)

**Assessment** may include:
- **Day-3 FSH and estradiol** (sometimes measured on cycle days 4 or 5)
- **Anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH)**
- **Antral follicle count (AFC)** on ultrasound

These tests are often considered together. A day-3 FSH around **10–15 mIU/mL** may be considered borderline, and higher levels may suggest reduced reserve; an estradiol level of **80 pg/mL or above** alongside elevated FSH can also be suggestive. Thresholds vary by assay and clinical context. One cited approach defines poor ovarian response when two of three features are present: age 40 or older or another risk factor, a previous IVF response of three or fewer eggs, and an abnormal reserve test such as AFC below 5 or AMH around 1 ng/mL. (1) (2) (3)

**What the results mean:** These markers can help predict response to ovarian stimulation, but they do not reliably predict natural conception or live birth on their own. Age remains an important factor in fertility prognosis. (1)

Interpretation and next steps depend on age, fertility history, test results, and other factors; consultation with a reproductive endocrinologist can help guide evaluation and options. (3)
