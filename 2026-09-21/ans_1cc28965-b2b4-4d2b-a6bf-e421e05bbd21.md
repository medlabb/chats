## Answer

 There is **no specific minimum age for commercial air travel** stated in the selected references.

A baby born at 27 weeks and now at **40 weeks post-conceptual age** is below the **46-week post-conceptual-age** threshold. The references advise avoiding **high-altitude travel** for premature infants below 46 weeks post-conceptual age, particularly when there is a history of oxygen requirement, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, or pulmonary hypertension. (1)

For commercial flying, infants with **unstable medical conditions should not fly**. Airlines may require a clinician’s medical certificate or clearance, especially if the infant needs supplemental oxygen; requirements vary by airline, so the airline should be contacted directly. (2)

The references do not provide a definitive rule stating when a former 27-week infant may safely fly. Clearance should therefore be obtained from the infant’s neonatologist/pediatrician, with assessment of current oxygen saturation, respiratory status, oxygen requirement, and history of bronchopulmonary dysplasia or pulmonary hypertension.
