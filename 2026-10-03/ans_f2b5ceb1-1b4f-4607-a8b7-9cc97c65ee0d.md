 ## Chickenpox (varicella) management

- **Uncomplicated varicella:** Oral acyclovir may be given at **20 mg/kg per dose (maximum 800 mg), four times daily for 5 days**. It is most effective when started as early as possible, preferably within **24 hours** after the rash begins; benefit is reduced if started more than **72 hours** after rash onset. Treatment is particularly important for people at increased risk of moderate-to-severe disease, including those older than 12 years and certain children with chronic cutaneous or pulmonary disorders or corticosteroid or long-term salicylate use. (1)
- **Oral alternatives:** Valacyclovir may be used in children **2 to <18 years** at **20 mg/kg per dose (maximum 1,000 mg), three times daily for 5 days**. Patients receiving antivirals should be well hydrated; renal function and white blood cell counts should be monitored during prolonged use. (1)
- **Severe or high-risk disease:** Intravenous acyclovir is indicated for severe varicella and for immunocompromised patients, even when treatment begins more than 72 hours after rash onset. Signs of disseminated disease—such as pneumonia, severe hepatitis, thrombocytopenia, or encephalitis—warrant immediate treatment. The listed IV regimen is **10 mg/kg or 500 mg/m² every 8 hours**, continued for **7–10 days** or until no new lesions have appeared for 48 hours. (1)
- **Acyclovir-resistant varicella:** Intravenous foscarnet may be used; cidofovir is also described as useful. Because of toxicity concerns, these treatments should be started in consultation with an infectious diseases specialist. (1; 2)
- **Newborns:** Newborns whose mothers develop varicella from **5 days before to 2 days after delivery** should receive VariZIG as soon as possible. If neonatal varicella lesions develop, the reference describes IV acyclovir; severe community-acquired neonatal disease, especially with pneumonia, hepatitis, or encephalitis, should also be treated with IV acyclovir. (3; 4)
- **After exposure:** For healthy children, varicella vaccine given as soon as possible within **3 days and up to 5 days** after exposure may prevent or modify disease. VariZIG is recommended after exposure for certain high-risk people, including immunocompromised children, pregnant women, and newborns; it should be given as soon as possible and may be effective up to 10 days after exposure. (3)
