## Answer

 - **Acute ischemic stroke/TIA:** If a cardiac embolic source—such as atrial fibrillation, mitral stenosis, or a mechanical valve—appears responsible, the reference indicates anticoagulation with IV heparin followed by warfarin, or another oral anticoagulant. It does not specify a general timing for starting anticoagulation after stroke. (1)
- **Secondary prevention:** Long-term warfarin is recommended after stroke/TIA for valvular atrial fibrillation or a mechanical aortic/mitral valve; nonvalvular atrial fibrillation is treated with an oral anticoagulant. Short-term anticoagulation (about 3 months) is indicated after stroke/TIA with acute MI or cardiomyopathy complicated by mural thrombus. (2)
- **Antiplatelets:** Aspirin is recommended for presumed **noncardiogenic** TIA or acute ischemic stroke—not as the general treatment for cardioembolic stroke. Low-dose aspirin may be added to warfarin for a patient with a mechanical valve if bleeding risk is low. (1; 2)
- **Important exceptions:** After alteplase, do not give anticoagulants or antiplatelets during the first 24 hours. After endocarditis-related TIA or stroke, avoid anticoagulants, antiplatelets, and thrombolytics for at least 2 weeks because of intracranial-hemorrhage risk. (1; 3)
