## Answer

 There is **no treatment known to slow or alter the natural history of progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP)**. Care focuses on symptom relief and supportive, multidisciplinary care. (1; 2)

- **Levodopa:** A trial can be reasonable for troublesome or disabling parkinsonism, particularly in PSP with predominant parkinsonism. Benefit, if present, is often modest or temporary; stop ineffective treatment rather than continuing it long-term. One suggested approach is levodopa 1000–1200 mg/day, if tolerated, for at least one month. Watch for adverse effects such as hallucinations, and consider reducing or stopping levodopa if suspected side effects occur. (3; 2)
- **Other symptom-directed medicines:** Botulinum toxin can help focal dystonia and drooling. Amantadine may provide temporary benefit for gait difficulties, dysphagia, drooling, or dyskinesia in some patients. Evidence for other drugs is limited; donepezil may worsen motor function. (3)
- **Therapies and practical support:** Speech and language therapy, swallowing assessment, nutrition support, and dietary or posture adjustments can help address speech and swallowing problems. Occupational therapy supports daily activities; physical therapy and exercise can focus on gait, balance, mobility, and fall prevention. Walking aids and visual or rhythmic cues may help with walking and freezing. (rf_d7; 2)
- **Planning and support:** A multidisciplinary team and caregiver support are important. Address advance care planning early, including future care preferences. (rf_d7; 2)

The selected references do not provide a disease-modifying treatment.
