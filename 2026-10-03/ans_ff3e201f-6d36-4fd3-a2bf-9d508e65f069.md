## Answer

 ## Nephrology MCQs

**1. Which finding is the hallmark of kidney disease that begins primarily with glomerular dysfunction?** (1)  
A. Electrolyte disorders  
B. Albuminuria  
C. Polyuria  
D. Urinary obstruction  

**Answer: B. Albuminuria.** Tubular disorders may instead first present with electrolyte or urine-concentration abnormalities. (1)

---

**2. Which urine profile is most consistent with prerenal azotemia?** (2)  
A. Urine sodium >40 mmol/L and osmolality <350 mosmol/L  
B. Urine sodium <20 mmol/L and osmolality >500 mosmol/L  
C. Fractional excretion of sodium >2% and muddy-brown casts  
D. Urine/plasma creatinine ratio <20  

**Answer: B.** Prerenal azotemia typically has avid sodium retention and concentrated urine. (2)

---

**3. Which urinary sediment finding is highly predictive of acute tubular necrosis (ATN)?** (2)  
A. Red blood cell casts  
B. Hyaline casts only  
C. Dark, muddy-brown granular casts  
D. No casts  

**Answer: C. Dark, muddy-brown granular casts.** Tubular epithelial cells may also be present. (2)

---

**4. Which combination best describes nephrotic syndrome?** (3)  
A. Proteinuria >3.5 g/24 h, hypoalbuminemia, edema, and hyperlipidemia  
B. Hematuria, red blood cell casts, and pyuria  
C. Oliguria, azotemia, and urinary sodium <20 mmol/L  
D. Bacteriuria, fever, and flank tenderness  

**Answer: A.** These are characteristic features listed for nephrotic syndrome. (3)

---

**5. In a person with normal nephrons, approximately how much albumin is excreted in the urine per day?** (4)  
A. 8–10 mg  
B. 80–100 mg  
C. 0.8–1 g  
D. 3.5 g  

**Answer: A. 8–10 mg per day, on average.** (4)

---

**6. Which finding supports a diagnosis of chronic renal failure in the nephrologic syndrome table?** (3)  
A. Azotemia for more than 3 months  
B. A single episode of hematuria  
C. Urine sodium below 20 mmol/L  
D. Bacteriuria above 10⁵ cfu/mL  

**Answer: A. Azotemia for more than 3 months.** (3)

---

**7. What is the usual initial imaging clue to postrenal azotemia from urinary tract obstruction?** (2)  
A. Bilateral small kidneys  
B. Dilation of the ureters and renal pelvis on ultrasound  
C. Increased kidney length  
D. Glomerular capillary thickening  

**Answer: B.** Ultrasound usually identifies ureteral and renal pelvic dilation, although it may be negative early in obstruction. (2)

---

**8. Which statement about daily protein excretion and nephrotic syndrome is correct?** (5)  
A. Protein excretion above 3.5 g/day always means all features of nephrotic syndrome are present.  
B. Protein excretion above 3.5 g/day can occur without the other features of nephrotic syndrome.  
C. Nephrotic syndrome is defined by hematuria alone.  
D. Protein excretion above 3.5 g/day excludes diabetes as a cause.  

**Answer: B.** Heavy protein excretion can occur without the other nephrotic-syndrome features, including in diabetes. (5)
