## Answer

 ### 1. A patient has enlarged cervical and inguinal lymph nodes. How is this pattern classified?

A. Localized lymphadenopathy  
B. Generalized lymphadenopathy  
C. Shotty lymphadenopathy  
D. Matted lymphadenopathy  

**Answer: B. Generalized lymphadenopathy.** Involvement of more than one anatomic region is generalized lymphadenopathy. (1)

### 2. Which finding is described as **always pathologic**?

A. A small, firm cervical node  
B. A tender inguinal node  
C. A palpable epitrochlear node  
D. A node that is less than 1 cm  

**Answer: C. A palpable epitrochlear node.** (1)

### 3. Which description best matches **“shotty”** lymph nodes?

A. Nodes that are connected and move together  
B. Small, firm nodes resembling buckshot pellets  
C. Soft nodes found in multiple anatomic regions  
D. Nodes that are tender on examination  

**Answer: B. Small, firm nodes resembling buckshot pellets.** “Matted” describes nodes that are connected and move together. (1)

### 4. During an examination for fever of unknown origin in an ICU patient, which approach is specifically emphasized?

A. Examine only visible skin lesions  
B. Examine the front and back of the skin, including areas where pressure ulcers may be overlooked  
C. Focus exclusively on lymph node regions  
D. Examine the skin only if the patient reports a rash  

**Answer: B.** A complete skin examination should include both front and back; unrecognized pressure ulcers have been mistaken for fever of unknown origin. (1)

### 5. Why is it useful to document a hospitalized patient’s lines, tubes, and drains when assessing possible infection?

A. They indicate whether lymphadenopathy is localized or generalized  
B. They can breach epithelial barriers and help identify body sites where microorganisms may localize  
C. They determine whether a skin rash is infectious  
D. They distinguish tender from nontender lymph nodes  

**Answer: B.** Lines, tubes, and drains can breach epithelial barriers, and knowing which are present helps identify potential infection sites. (1)
