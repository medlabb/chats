```
Changes in kidney with ageing
```

- - -

## Answer

 With normal ageing, the kidneys undergo **structural and functional changes**:

- **Structure:** Kidney mass decreases by about 25–30% between ages 30 and 80, with loss of functioning nephrons and glomeruli. Fibrosis and fat replace some tissue, and cortical volume declines. (rf_6d; 1)
- **Other anatomical changes:** Medullary pyramids become fewer and larger, and kidney cysts become more common and larger with age. (1; 2)
- **Filtering function:** Kidney filtration commonly declines with age, but the rate varies considerably; some healthy older adults show little or no decline. Creatinine clearance decreases by about 7.5–10 mL/min per decade on average, while serum creatinine may remain stable because creatinine production also falls and its tubular secretion increases. (rf_6d)
- **Water and acid handling:** The ageing kidney has less ability to dilute urine and excrete a water load, retain water during dehydration, and excrete an acid load. (3)
- **Greater vulnerability to injury:** Older kidneys are more susceptible to some medication-related toxicity and acute injury, and may recover less well after injury. (3; 4)
- **Hormonal changes:** Kidney production of active vitamin D and klotho decreases with age; the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system is also downregulated in older people. (3)

These are typical age-related changes; their extent varies between individuals. (rf_6d)
