 ## Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)

DKA is a serious complication of diabetes caused by insufficient insulin together with increased counterregulatory hormones. It leads to ketone production and metabolic acidosis. (1)

**Symptoms and signs** usually develop over about 24 hours and can include nausea and vomiting, thirst and frequent urination, abdominal pain, shortness of breath, dehydration, rapid pulse, low blood pressure, and deep, rapid Kussmaul breathing. Confusion or reduced alertness can occur in severe cases. Infection, inadequate insulin, or a heart or other tissue infarction may precipitate DKA. These manifestations and triggers are summarized in **Table 416-8**. (1)

**Diagnosis** is based on hyperglycemia—usually glucose above 250 mg/dL—ketosis, and metabolic acidosis, typically with bicarbonate below 15–18 mmol/L and an increased anion gap. DKA can sometimes occur with only mildly elevated or normal glucose, particularly with SGLT-2 inhibitor treatment. (2)

**Treatment requires hospital care** and includes IV fluids, insulin, assessment and correction of electrolyte abnormalities, and investigation and treatment of the precipitating cause. The management steps are summarized in **Table 416-9**. Potassium must be checked closely; if the initial potassium is below 3.3 mmol/L, insulin should be withheld until potassium is corrected. Frequent monitoring of glucose, electrolytes, anion gap, vital signs, and mental status is important. (3)
