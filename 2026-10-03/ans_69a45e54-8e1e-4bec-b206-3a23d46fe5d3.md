## Answer

 **Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM)** is glucose intolerance identified during pregnancy. It often resolves after delivery, but the risk of developing type 2 diabetes later is higher (1, 2).

- **Screening:** Screening is generally recommended at or beyond 24 weeks. A common approach is a nonfasting 50-g, 1-hour glucose challenge at 24–28 weeks; an elevated result is followed by a 3-hour glucose tolerance test. Two or more abnormal results establish the diagnosis (3).
- **Management:** Diet and glucose monitoring are the mainstays of care. Typical glucose goals are fasting below 95 mg/dL, 1-hour after meals below 140 mg/dL, or 2-hour after meals below 120 mg/dL. Insulin is used when glucose cannot be controlled with diet (4).
- **Why it matters:** Treatment is associated with lower rates of complications such as excessive fetal growth, preeclampsia, shoulder dystocia, and cesarean delivery (3).
- **After delivery:** Glucose screening is recommended 4–12 weeks postpartum, with repeat testing at least every 1–3 years if postpartum results are normal (2).
