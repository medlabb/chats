## Answer

 Treatment depends on the **specific type of small-vessel vasculitis** and whether disease is mild or threatens an organ or life. The available guidance discusses ANCA-associated vasculitis, eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA), hepatitis C–associated cryoglobulinemic vasculitis, and IgA vasculitis; it cites the ACR/Vasculitis Foundation 2021 guideline and the EULAR ANCA-associated vasculitis update (1, 2).

- **ANCA-associated vasculitis (GPA/MPA):** For severe disease, rituximab plus corticosteroids is a preferred induction approach, particularly for PR3-ANCA–positive GPA; cyclophosphamide plus corticosteroids is an alternative. Avacopan may be added to either regimen. For nonsevere disease, methotrexate plus prednisone is an option. Faster corticosteroid tapering has shown similar efficacy with fewer steroid-related complications. Cyclophosphamide is generally limited to 3–6 months, then replaced by a non-cyclophosphamide maintenance treatment. Provide Pneumocystis jirovecii prophylaxis when using rituximab or cyclophosphamide; plasma exchange is not routinely recommended (3, 4).
- **EGPA:** Corticosteroids are first-line; azathioprine or methotrexate may be used for mild-to-moderate disease. Mepolizumab is an option, but the source says it has not been studied for severe, organ- or life-threatening vasculitic disease, which generally requires cyclophosphamide. Rituximab may be considered for refractory disease (2).
- **Hepatitis C–associated cryoglobulinemic vasculitis:** For disease that is not life- or organ-threatening, antiviral therapy is first-line. Severe disease may require corticosteroids plus rituximab or cyclophosphamide alongside antiviral treatment; plasma exchange may help in selected cases (5).
- **IgA vasculitis:** The benefit of corticosteroids is controversial. Severe adult disease is sometimes treated with rituximab or mycophenolate, but there is no consensus on efficacy or the best regimen (6).

Prompt specialist assessment is important; the guidance recommends rheumatology referral for patients with ANCA-associated vasculitis and admission for organ- or life-threatening disease, such as alveolar hemorrhage or rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis (1). The selected references do not provide a complete treatment guideline for every small-vessel vasculitis subtype.
