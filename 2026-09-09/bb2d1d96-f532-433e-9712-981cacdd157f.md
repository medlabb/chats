For a **24-week neonate after arterial-line removal**, assess carefully for **vasospasm or arterial thrombosis**:

- Check and document distal **colour, temperature, capillary refill, pulses, limb perfusion, and—if available—distal oximetry/plethysmography**. Concerning findings include a cool, pale/mottled limb, delayed capillary refill, reduced/absent pulse, cyanotic digits, or a dampened/lost waveform. (1) (2)
- Consider **Doppler ultrasound** if perfusion remains abnormal or thrombosis is suspected. If there is focal distal circulatory compromise with thrombosis demonstrated on imaging, the reference recommends urgent vascular-surgical consultation. (3)
- Continue to monitor for **hematoma/bleeding, infection, embolic complications, and evolving ischemia** at the former cannulation site. (1)
- Topical **nitroglycerin (GTN)** has been described for arterial obstruction/vasospasm in neonates after warming failed, but transient hypotension was reported; blood pressure and limb perfusion therefore require close monitoring. (3)

The selected references do not provide further neonatal-specific GTN dosing or a complete management protocol.
