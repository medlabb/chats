For a **24-week neonate with suspected arterial thrombosis/ischemia after an arterial line**, the selected references support:

- **Immediate removal of the arterial catheter**—already done. (1)  
- Obtain **Doppler ultrasound** to confirm thrombosis and assess arterial flow. (1)  
- Consider **anticoagulation for 5–7 days** if arterial thrombosis is confirmed, with neonatal hematology/senior specialist input. (1)  
- If there is **progressive or limb-/organ-threatening ischemia**, thrombolysis or surgical thrombectomy may need consideration. (1)  
- Monitor closely for **absent or diminished pulses, coldness, duskiness/blanching, hypertension, acute renal injury, or mesenteric ischemia**. (1)  
- For peripheral arterial lines, assessment of collateral circulation is relevant; vasospasm and thromboembolism can both cause distal discoloration. (2)

The selected references do **not** provide guidance on GTN (nitroglycerin) cream, dosing, application, contraindications, or additional vasodilator treatment.
