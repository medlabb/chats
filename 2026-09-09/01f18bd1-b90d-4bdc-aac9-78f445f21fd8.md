- **Immediate local measures:** Warm the affected arterial site with a hot pack. If inadequate, intravenous lidocaine **0.5 mg/kg** may be beneficial. (1)  
- **Topical nitroglycerin:** If warming and lidocaine fail, apply a thin film of **2% nitroglycerin ointment** to the wrist, palm, and fingers—reported neonatal dosing was **4 mm/kg**. Rapid improvement in hand color and perfusion was observed, although transient blood-pressure reduction can occur. (1)  
- **Systemic vasodilators:** Specific systemic vasodilator therapy for this post–radial arterial-line complication is **not described in the selected references**.  
- **Monitoring:** Assess distal circulation for pulse, skin color, capillary refill, temperature, and perfusion; peripheral arterial thrombosis may present with decreased or absent pulses, prolonged capillary refill, and a cool, pale extremity. (2) Monitor blood pressure after topical nitroglycerin because transient hypotension has been reported. (1)  
- **Urgent consultation:** Remove the arterial catheter and promptly consult a **vascular surgeon** when there are signs of focal distal circulatory compromise together with thrombosis or embolism demonstrated on imaging such as Doppler ultrasound. (1) More generally, vascular insufficiency warrants emergency consultation with an appropriately trained pediatric orthopedic surgeon; a hand that remains cold, white, or cyanotic despite attempts to restore circulation requires operative exploration and vascular repair. (3)  

The selected references do not provide a specific neonatal monitoring schedule after radial-line removal or recommendations for systemic vasodilators beyond the measures described above.
