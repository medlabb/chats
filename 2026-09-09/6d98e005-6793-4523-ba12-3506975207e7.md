- **Assess perfusion promptly:** inspect color and temperature, check capillary refill and distal pulses, and obtain vascular imaging such as Doppler ultrasonography when thrombosis or embolism is suspected. Findings concerning for ischemia include a cold, pale or mottled extremity, delayed capillary refill, and decreased or absent pulses. (1) (2)

- **If arterial thrombosis/embolism is demonstrated with compromised distal circulation:** remove the arterial catheter and promptly consult a vascular surgeon. (2)

- **Suspected vasospasm/transient obstruction:** warming the arterial site with a hot pack may be beneficial. Intravenous lidocaine at **0.5 mg/kg** has also been reported as useful. (2)

- **Topical nitroglycerin:** if warming or lidocaine fails, a thin film of **2% nitroglycerin ointment** may be applied to the wrist, palm, and fingers at **4 mm/kg**. Rapid improvement in hand color and perfusion was reported in four neonates; transient blood-pressure reduction occurred, but serious hypotension was not reported. Blood pressure should therefore be monitored closely, particularly in an extremely preterm infant. (2)

The selected references do not provide a specific protocol for a **day-2, 24-week neonate**, management after line removal alone, or further anticoagulation/thrombolysis recommendations.
