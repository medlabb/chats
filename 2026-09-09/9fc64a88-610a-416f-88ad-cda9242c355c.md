After removal of a **peripheral arterial catheter**, closely assess and document the affected limb for:

- Distal pulses/Doppler signals  
- Capillary refill and perfusion  
- Colour and temperature  
- Swelling or skin changes  

Concerning findings include **absent or diminished pulses, prolonged capillary refill, coolness, pallor/discoloration, or poor distal perfusion**, which may indicate arterial thrombosis or acute ischemia (1). Acute ischemia is classified by sensory, motor, and Doppler findings; an immediately threatened limb requires urgent revascularization (2). Promptly involve the neonatal team and appropriate vascular/surgical specialists if perfusion is abnormal or deteriorating.

The selected references do **not** provide guidance on GTN cream, alternative topical treatments, anticoagulation, imaging, or specific management after line removal.
