- **Limb/site warming:** For transient arterial obstruction (vasospasm), warming the arterial site with a hot pack may be beneficial. (1)
- **Systemic therapy:** Intravenous lidocaine at **0.5 mg/kg** is also described as potentially beneficial. The selected reference does not provide recommendations for other systemic vasodilators. (1)
- **Topical nitroglycerin:** If warming or intravenous lidocaine fails, topical nitroglycerin ointment may be useful. In four neonates with radial artery catheters, a thin film of **2% nitroglycerin ointment**, applied to the wrist, palm, and fingers at **4 mm/kg**, was associated with rapid improvement in hand color and perfusion. Transient blood-pressure reduction occurred, but no serious hypotension was reported. (1)
- **Suspected thrombosis or embolism:** Remove the arterial catheter and promptly consult a vascular surgeon when there are signs of compromised distal circulation—such as an absent or decreased pulse, blanched or mottled skin, delayed capillary refill, or a cold distal extremity—and imaging confirms arterial thrombosis or embolism. (1)

The selected reference does not provide further guidance specific to management after arterial-line **removal** or on systemic vasodilators beyond intravenous lidocaine.
