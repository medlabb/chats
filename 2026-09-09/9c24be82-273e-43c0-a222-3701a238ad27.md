- **Assess promptly for ischemia/vasospasm:** monitor distal pulses, capillary refill, temperature, color, and waveform/oximetry changes. Concerning findings include a cool, pale or mottled extremity, diminished/absent pulses, delayed capillary refill, or cyanotic digits. Doppler ultrasound can evaluate suspected thrombosis. (1) (2)

- **Limb/site warming:** for transient arterial obstruction or vasospasm, warming the arterial site with a hot pack may be beneficial. (3)

- **Topical nitroglycerin:** if warming is unsuccessful, topical nitroglycerin ointment may be useful. In four neonates with radial artery catheters, a thin film of **2% nitroglycerin ointment** applied to the wrist, palm, and fingers at **4 mm/kg** was associated with rapid improvement in hand color and perfusion; transient blood-pressure reduction occurred, but serious hypotension was not reported. (3)

- **Other reported treatment:** intravenous lidocaine at **0.5 mg/kg** was also reported as potentially beneficial for transient arterial obstruction. (3)

- **Suspected thrombosis or embolism:** when focal distal circulatory compromise is accompanied by thrombosis or embolism on imaging, the arterial catheter should be removed and a vascular surgeon consulted promptly. (3)

- **Systemic vasodilators:** the selected references do not provide a recommended systemic vasodilator regimen for a preterm neonate. They mention studies of intra-arterial nitroglycerin, nitroprusside, verapamil, and phentolamine, but results were inconsistent; the cited source states that it does not use intra-arterial agents routinely to prevent vasospasm. (1)

The selected references do not specifically describe management differences after **line removal** or provide a complete neonatal protocol for systemic vasodilator therapy.
