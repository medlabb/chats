- **Immediately assess distal perfusion:** color, temperature, capillary refill, pulses, arterial waveform, and distal oximetry/plethysmography. Concerning findings include a pale, mottled, or cool extremity; delayed capillary refill; diminished/absent pulse; dampened waveform; or reduced distal pleth signal. (1) (2)

- **Evaluate for thrombosis or embolism**, typically with vascular imaging such as Doppler ultrasound when ischemia persists or thrombosis is suspected. If there is focal distal circulatory compromise **and imaging confirms thrombosis or embolism**, the arterial catheter should be removed if still present and a vascular surgeon consulted promptly. (1)

- For suspected **arterial spasm**, warming the affected arterial site with a **hot pack** may be beneficial. (1)

- If warming is unsuccessful, **topical nitroglycerin ointment** may be considered. In four neonates with radial arterial catheters, a thin film of **2% nitroglycerin ointment**, applied to the wrist, palm, and fingers at **4 mm/kg**, was associated with rapid improvement in hand color and perfusion. A transient decrease in blood pressure was reported, so blood pressure should be monitored closely; the selected reference does not establish safety or dosing specifically for a 24-week, day-2 infant. (1)

- The selected references state that intra-arterial administration of vasodilator agents is not routinely used to prevent vasospasm. (2)

Specific recommendations regarding anticoagulation, thrombolysis, surgical intervention, or a detailed protocol tailored to an extremely preterm 24-week neonate cannot be found in the selected references.
