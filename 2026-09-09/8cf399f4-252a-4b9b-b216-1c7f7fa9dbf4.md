- **Assess and monitor perfusion:** Check limb color, temperature, capillary refill, and distal pulses regularly. Findings concerning for ischemia include a cold or pale/mottled extremity, delayed capillary refill, and decreased or absent distal pulses. (1) (2)

- **Suspected vasospasm/ transient arterial obstruction:** Warm the arterial site with a **hot pack**. Intravenous lidocaine **0.5 mg/kg** has also been reported as beneficial. (3)

- **Topical nitroglycerin:** If warming and/or lidocaine fails, topical nitroglycerin ointment may be useful. In four neonates with radial arterial catheters, a thin film of **2% nitroglycerin ointment, 4 mm/kg**, applied to the wrist, palm, and fingers was associated with rapid improvement in hand color and perfusion. Transient blood-pressure reduction occurred, but serious hypotension was not reported. (3) Blood pressure should therefore be monitored closely when it is used.

- **Suspected thrombosis or embolism:** Obtain vascular imaging, such as Doppler ultrasound. If there are signs of focal distal circulatory compromise **and** imaging demonstrates arterial thrombosis or embolism, the arterial catheter should be removed and a vascular surgeon consulted promptly. (3)

The selected references do not provide a more specific neonatal protocol for ischemia occurring specifically after arterial-line **removal**, nor do they specify additional anticoagulation or thrombolytic treatment.
