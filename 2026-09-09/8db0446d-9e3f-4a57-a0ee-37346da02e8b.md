For a **day-2, 24-week neonate with suspected post–arterial-line limb hypoperfusion**, assess and document the affected limb frequently: **colour, temperature, capillary refill, distal pulses, waveform/oximetry**, and consider **Doppler ultrasound** if perfusion remains abnormal. Peripheral arterial thrombosis may present with absent/decreased pulses, prolonged capillary refill, and a cool, pale extremity. (1)

**Topical nitroglycerin (GTN)** is described for transient arterial obstruction/vasospasm in neonates; a thin film of 2% ointment was associated with improved hand perfusion, but transient blood-pressure reduction occurred. (2) Monitor blood pressure closely and avoid further dosing/escalate if hypotension develops.

Other measures described include **warming the arterial site**, with IV lidocaine reported if warming fails. (2) If there are persistent signs of compromised distal circulation and imaging confirms thrombosis/embolism, the reference recommends catheter removal and prompt vascular-surgical consultation. (2)

The selected references do not provide further 24-week-specific guidance on GTN dosing, duration, or alternative therapy.
