## Answer

 ## **Guillain–Barré syndrome (GBS)**

GBS is an **acute or subacute inflammatory polyneuropathy** that may follow a minor infection, inoculation, or surgery, although no trigger is sometimes identified. It may be associated with preceding *Campylobacter jejuni* or Zika virus infection. Both **demyelinating and axonal** forms occur. (1)

### Clinical features
- Typically causes **symmetric ascending weakness**, beginning in the legs; respiratory and swallowing muscles may become involved.
- **Deep-tendon reflexes are usually absent.**
- Sensory symptoms may occur but are generally less prominent than weakness.
- Autonomic dysfunction can include tachycardia, arrhythmias, labile blood pressure, abnormal sweating, impaired pulmonary function, sphincter disturbances, and paralytic ileus.
- The **Miller Fisher variant** is characterized by ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, and areflexia. (1)

### Diagnosis and investigations
Diagnostic criteria include:
- Progressive weakness involving more than one limb
- Distal areflexia with proximal areflexia or hyporeflexia

Supportive findings include relatively symmetric deficits, mild sensory involvement, cranial-nerve involvement, autonomic dysfunction, progression for up to 4 weeks, increased CSF protein after the first week, and CSF white-cell count ≤10/μL. These criteria are summarized in **Table 10-3: Diagnostic Criteria for Guillain–Barré Syndrome**. (2)

CSF may show **increased protein with a normal cell count**—cytoalbuminologic dissociation—although this may be absent during the first week. Nerve-conduction studies may demonstrate slowed conduction, conduction block, denervation, or axonal loss. (2)

### Treatment
- **Plasmapheresis** shortens recovery time and may reduce residual deficits, particularly when started early in severe, rapidly progressive, or respiratory-compromising disease.
- **Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG)** is equally effective; the cited regimen is **400 mg/kg/day for 5 days**. IVIG is preferred in children and in adults with cardiovascular instability. Plasmapheresis and IVIG should not be combined. (2)
- Severe cases require intensive-care monitoring for respiratory failure, vascular instability, and arrhythmias. Physical therapy and rehabilitation are important. Glucocorticoids are not indicated. (3)

### Prognosis
Progression usually stops by approximately 4 weeks, followed by improvement over weeks to months. The cited outcomes are approximately **70% complete recovery, 25% mild residual neurological deficits, and 5% death**, usually from respiratory failure. (3)
