## Answer

 ## Hypoglycemia

**Hypoglycemia** can cause:

- **Autonomic symptoms:** tremor, palpitations, anxiety, sweating, hunger, and paresthesias.  
- **Neuroglycopenic symptoms:** dizziness, weakness, drowsiness, confusion, delirium, seizures, and coma. (1)
- Before syncope, patients may feel weak, hungry, and sweaty; worsening hypoglycemia can cause agitation, confusion, and altered mental status. (2)

### Causes

Potential causes include insulin or hypoglycemic medications, alcohol use, liver disease, kidney disease, critical illness, malnutrition, hypothyroidism with adrenal insufficiency, and certain medications. (3) (4) (5) (6)

### Immediate management

- In **acute severe hypoglycemia**, administer **25–50 g of intravenous dextrose**, followed by continuous dextrose infusion, with hourly blood-glucose monitoring. Neurologic recovery may lag behind glucose normalization. (3)
- In pregnancy, symptomatic hypoglycemia may be treated with **15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate**, followed by glucose rechecking after 15 minutes; glucagon may be used when oral carbohydrate cannot be taken. (7)

Hypoglycemia can be life-threatening, particularly when prolonged or associated with seizures, coma, or altered mental status. (2) (1)
