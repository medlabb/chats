## Answer

 **Gluteal abscess—antibiotics (ABX):**

- **Incision and drainage is generally essential** for an abscess; antibiotics are typically added afterward. For skin abscesses, empiric therapy should cover **S. aureus, including MRSA**. (1)
- Usual oral options for an uncomplicated skin abscess include:
  - **Trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole:** 1–2 double-strength tablets orally twice daily  
  - **Doxycycline:** 100 mg orally twice daily  
  - **Minocycline:** 200 mg once, then 100 mg twice daily  
  - **Clindamycin:** 450 mg orally every 8 hours, though it is generally avoided when possible because of *C. difficile* risk and resistance. (1)
- Consider **parenteral therapy/admission** for systemic toxicity, rapid progression, extensive abscess or cellulitis, immunocompromise, or inability to take oral medication. Suggested agents include IV vancomycin or daptomycin. (1)
- If this is specifically **perianal/anorectal**, suggested empiric options after drainage are **amoxicillin–clavulanate 875/125 mg every 8–12 hours for 4–5 days**, or **ciprofloxacin 500 mg every 12 hours plus metronidazole 500 mg every 8 hours**. (2)
- If this is a **pilonidal abscess**, antibiotics are not required after drainage unless there are fever/systemic symptoms, extensive cellulitis, or immunocompromise; a listed option is a first-generation cephalosporin such as cefazolin plus metronidazole. (3)
