## Answer

 ## Treatment of hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS)

**Initial management is supportive:**

- **Red blood cell transfusion** for clinically significant anemia, such as hemoglobin around **6–7 g/dL** or hematocrit **<18%**.
- **Platelet transfusion** only for significant clinical bleeding or when an invasive procedure is required.
- Careful **fluid and electrolyte management**.
- Stop **nephrotoxic medications** and adjust renally excreted drug doses; avoid NSAIDs.
- Start **dialysis/kidney replacement therapy** for symptomatic uremia, BUN generally **>80 mg/dL**, severe refractory fluid overload, refractory hyperkalemia/acidosis or other electrolyte abnormalities, or nutritional needs in oliguria/anuria.
- Provide adequate nutrition. (1) (2) (3)

### Cause-specific therapy

- **Complement-mediated HUS:** treatment may include **eculizumab**; plasma therapy is an alternative if unavailable. Plasma exchange followed by immunosuppression is suggested for HUS associated with **complement factor H antibodies**. (4) (5)
- **Pneumococcal-associated HUS:** empiric **vancomycin plus a broad-spectrum cephalosporin** such as cefotaxime or ceftriaxone while awaiting cultures and sensitivities. (2)
- **STEC-HUS:** additional therapies such as antithrombotic agents and oral Shiga-toxin binding agents are **not recommended**; antithrombotic therapy increases hemorrhagic risk and has no demonstrated benefit. (6)

Specific treatment details for other less common causes of HUS cannot be found in the selected references.
