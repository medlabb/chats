## Answer

 ## **Tardive dyskinesia (TD)**

**Tardive dyskinesia** is a movement disorder that may develop after chronic treatment with antipsychotic or other dopamine-blocking medications. It commonly causes involuntary movements of the **tongue, jaw, lips, face, trunk, or limbs**—for example, tongue twisting or protrusion, chewing, lip smacking or puckering, facial grimacing, choreiform movements, or slow writhing movements. (1) (2)

### Timing and risk factors
Symptoms generally develop after at least **2 months of exposure** to a dopamine-blocking agent—or 1 month in people aged 60 or older—and may occur during treatment or after withdrawal. Movements should persist for at least 4 weeks for the clinical diagnosis described in the reference. (1)

Risk factors include **older age, female sex, pre-existing parkinsonism, previous brain damage, longer antipsychotic exposure, higher dosage, first-generation antipsychotics, mood disorders, and prior acute extrapyramidal symptoms**. (2) (1)

### Course and management
TD may stabilize or improve over time, although it can persist and occasionally become disabling, affecting walking, breathing, eating, or speaking. (3) (2)

Management may include:

- Regular assessment, such as with the **Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale (AIMS)**; patients receiving dopamine blockers for more than 6 months are described as needing systematic evaluation every 6 months. (3)
- Reassessing whether antipsychotic treatment is still needed.
- Considering gradual dose reduction, discontinuation, or switching medication when clinically appropriate; abrupt changes may temporarily worsen the movements. (4) (2)
- Considering clozapine or another dopamine–serotonin antagonist in selected patients, particularly when TD is severe. (3) (1)
- Considering the approved **VMAT2 inhibitors valbenazine and deutetrabenazine** when symptoms are not adequately controlled by dose reduction or medication changes. (5) (4)

Antiparkinsonian medications may worsen TD, although they may sometimes be unavoidable when acute extrapyramidal symptoms occur concurrently. (2)
