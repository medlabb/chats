## Answer

 **Treatment for Guillain–Barré syndrome** includes:

- **Plasmapheresis or IVIG**—IVIG is given at **400 mg/kg/day for 5 days**. Treatment is most useful when started within the first few days, particularly in severe or rapidly progressive illness or when ventilation is impaired. (1)
- **Prednisone is ineffective** and may prolong recovery. (1)
- **Respiratory monitoring and support:** ICU admission is recommended if forced vital capacity (FVC) is declining. Intubation should be considered when FVC reaches **15 mL/kg**, maximum inspiratory pressure reaches **−30 mm Hg**, or dyspnea develops. Declining oxygen saturation is a late sign of neuromuscular respiratory failure. (1)
- **Supportive care:** respiratory toilet and chest physical therapy help prevent atelectasis; hypotension may respond to fluids or pressor agents; thromboprophylaxis is important. (1)
