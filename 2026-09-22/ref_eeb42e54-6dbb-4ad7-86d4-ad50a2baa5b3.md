#### TREATMENT

If treatment is warranted to reduce tremor amplitude because of disability or social limitations, **[propranolol,](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660185)** 40-160 mg orally twice daily, can be prescribed but will need to be taken indefinitely. Adverse effects include bradycardia, depression, and bronchospasm. Other beta-blockers, such as [atenolol](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660376) and [sotalol](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660513), have also been used. If tremor is particularly disabling under certain predictable circumstances, a single oral dose of 40-120 mg of [propranolol](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660185) can be taken in anticipation of the precipitating circumstances.

**[Primidone](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659867)** is also effective, but patients are often particularly sensitive to it; it is therefore introduced more gradually than when used for epilepsy. Patients are started on 50 mg/d, and the daily dose is increased by 50 mg every 2 weeks until benefit occurs or side effects (eg, dizziness, fatigue, sedation, lassitude) limit further increments. A dose of 100 or 250 mg three times a day is often effective. There is no evidence that high doses (exceeding 750 mg daily) provide any added benefit.

If neither of these drugs is effective, combination therapy is sometimes worthwhile. Occasional patients respond to [alprazolam](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660360), up to 3 mg/d in divided doses. Some patients reportedly benefit from [gabapentin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660534) (up to 1,200 mg/d; started at 100 mg three times daily and increased by 100 mg/d each week to a maximum of 400 mg three times daily), [topiramate](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659464) (400 mg/d; started at 25 mg twice daily and increased by 25 mg/d each week up to maximum of 200 mg twice daily), [zonisamide](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658791) (up to 200 mg/d), or intramuscular injections of botulinum toxin. When tremor is disabling and unresponsive to pharmacologic measures, surgical measures may be necessary. High-frequency **thalamic stimulation** by an implanted electrode is effective and has a low morbidity. Benefit is maintained over the years in most patients with severe disability. Thalamotomy may be helpful but has a significantly higher morbidity than thalamic stimulation. Transcranial focused ultrasound thalamotomy may also reduce the amplitude of tremor and improve quality of life, and remains an option for those who are refractory to medical treatment and prefer to avoid a surgical procedure. Stereotactic radiosurgery is another option for intractable upper extremity tremor.
