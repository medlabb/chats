 ## Pediatric ITP: treatment approach

Treatment is primarily determined by **bleeding severity**, not platelet count alone. The modified Buchanan and Adix bleeding score is referenced in **Table 1**; dosing regimens are referenced in **Table 2**. (1) (2)

### 1. No bleeding, mild bleeding, or low-risk moderate bleeding

For bleeding grades **0–3a**, **watchful waiting** is generally recommended rather than pharmacologic treatment, provided reliable follow-up is available. Management includes activity restriction, avoidance of antiplatelet/anticoagulant drugs, serial platelet counts, and clinical monitoring for bleeding. Treatment should begin if bleeding progresses to grade **3b or higher**. (1) (2)

Pharmacologic treatment may reasonably be chosen if quality of life is substantially impaired or reliable follow-up cannot be assured. (1)

---

### 2. Moderate-to-high bleeding risk: grade 3b or equivalent

Examples include grade 3b bleeding, planned invasive procedures likely to cause blood loss, or platelet count <30,000/microL with additional bleeding-risk factors. (3)

#### If an urgent platelet rise is not required

Use an oral glucocorticoid as initial therapy:

- **Prednisone:** **2–4 mg/kg/day orally**, maximum **120 mg/day**, for **5–7 days**; or  
- **Dexamethasone:** **0.6 mg/kg/day**, maximum **40 mg/day**, orally or IV, for **4 days**. (4)

For pediatric ITP, therapy should generally be a short course. A platelet fall after discontinuation is common; additional treatment may be needed if clinically significant bleeding persists or recurs. **Ongoing or intermittent glucocorticoid therapy beyond 2–4 weeks is strongly discouraged.** (4)

#### If a rapid platelet rise is needed

Examples include surgery/procedures, recent head trauma without evidence of intracranial hemorrhage, or severe unexplained headache. Preferred options are:

- **IVIG:** **1 g/kg IV once**; if the platelet response is inadequate and serious bleeding remains a concern, it may be repeated once daily for a total of **up to 3 days**. (2)
- **IV anti-D:** **75 micrograms/kg IV once**, only in eligible patients. Anti-D should not be used in Rh-negative patients, patients with a positive DAT, hemolytic anemia, or prior splenectomy. (1) (2)

An increase in platelet count is usually observed within approximately **24 hours** after IVIG or anti-D. (3)

---

### 3. Severe or life-threatening bleeding: grade 4–5

Examples include intracranial hemorrhage, major trauma, gastrointestinal bleeding with hemodynamic instability, or pulmonary hemorrhage with cardiopulmonary compromise. Immediate combination therapy is recommended. (2)

#### Recommended regimen

- **Methylprednisolone:** **30 mg/kg/day IV**, maximum **1,000 mg/day**, as a single daily dose for **2–3 days**. (2)
- **IVIG:** **1 g/kg IV once**; if necessary, repeat once daily for a total of **up to 3 days**. (2)
- **Platelet transfusion:** **10–30 mL/kg bolus**, followed by continuous infusion. Check the platelet count approximately **15 minutes after the bolus**, then frequently thereafter. The stated target is generally **>50,000/microL**; some clinicians aim for **>100,000/microL** in intracranial hemorrhage, although this may be difficult to achieve in ITP. (2)
- **IV anti-D, if eligible:** **75 micrograms/kg IV once** in an Rh-positive, DAT-negative patient; its additional benefit in life-threatening bleeding is uncertain. (2)

Platelet transfusions are used because of the immediate bleeding risk, despite rapid immune-mediated platelet destruction; children with ITP may require larger-than-usual platelet doses. (1) (2)

## Glucocorticoid tapering strategy

The pediatric references provided specify:

- **Prednisone:** 5–7 days at **2–4 mg/kg/day** for children without severe bleeding. (4)

## Persistent or chronic ITP requiring second-line therapy

For children with persistent or chronic ITP requiring second-line treatment, **thrombopoietin-receptor agonists (TPO-RAs)** are described as preferred agents for most patients. They are titrated to maintain a safe platelet count, typically **≥50,000/microL**, while avoiding thrombocytosis. (rf_42)

### Eltrombopag

- **Age 1–5 years:** **25 mg orally once daily**
- **Age ≥6 years:** **50 mg orally once daily**
- Adjust by **12.5 mg every 2 weeks**
- Maximum: **75 mg/day**
- Dose reduction is required with hepatic impairment and may be required in some children of Eastern Asian ancestry aged ≥6 years.
- Administer on an empty stomach, avoiding intake for 2 hours before and after dosing and avoiding dairy/high-calcium or iron intake for 4 hours before and after dosing. (rf_42)

### Romiplostim

- Usual starting dose: **3 micrograms/kg subcutaneously once weekly**
- Manufacturer-suggested starting dose: **1 microgram/kg**
- Adjust by **1 microgram/kg per week**
- Maximum: **10 micrograms/kg/week** (rf_42)

### Avatrombopag

- **Age 1 to <6 years:** **10 mg orally once daily**
- **Age ≥6 years and adolescents:** **20 mg orally once daily**
- Adjust by **10 mg every 2 weeks**
- Maximum:
  - **20 mg/day** for children <6 years
  - **40 mg/day** for children ≥6 years (5)

### TPO-RA monitoring

For a child responding to a TPO-RA, platelet counts generally rise within **7–14 days**. Suggested monitoring is:

- **5–7 days after initiation**
- **1 week after each dose change**
- **Monthly once stable**
- Any time there is clinical concern for worsening thrombocytopenia (5)

For eltrombopag, liver enzymes should be checked before treatment, every 2 weeks during dose adjustment, and monthly thereafter. (5)
