 # MIS-C and Kawasaki Disease: Clinical Similarities and Differences

## Overview

**Multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children (MIS-C)** is an uncommon, postinfectious complication of COVID-19 caused by SARS-CoV-2. It is characterized by persistent fever with prominent **cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, and mucocutaneous** manifestations. Its clinical presentation may resemble incomplete Kawasaki disease (KD) or toxic shock syndrome. (1) (2)

**Kawasaki disease** is a multisystem vasculitis of uncertain, possibly infectious, etiology and is an important cause of prolonged fever in childhood. Diagnosis is primarily based on clinical findings, including conjunctivitis, oral changes, rash, changes in the hands and feet, and cervical lymphadenopathy. (3)

## Clinical similarities

MIS-C and KD may both present with:

- Persistent fever  
- Rash and conjunctivitis  
- Mucocutaneous abnormalities  
- Cervical lymphadenopathy  
- Cardiovascular involvement  
- Shock in severe cases  

Approximately **40–50% of children with MIS-C** in available case series met criteria for complete or incomplete KD. There is particular overlap between MIS-C and **Kawasaki disease shock syndrome**, which is characterized by prominent cardiovascular involvement. (4)

Children with persistent fever, ill appearance, shock, myocardial dysfunction, gastrointestinal symptoms, or acute cervical lymphadenitis should be evaluated for both KD and MIS-C. Cardiac testing, such as echocardiography, is usually included in the initial evaluation. (5)

## Key differences

| Feature | MIS-C | Classic Kawasaki disease |
|---|---|---|
| **Typical age** | More commonly affects older children and adolescents | More commonly affects infants and young children |
| **Gastrointestinal symptoms** | Particularly abdominal pain, are very common | Less prominent |
| **Cardiovascular disease** | Myocardial dysfunction and shock occur more often | Less common in classic KD, although characteristic of KD shock syndrome |
| **Inflammatory markers** | CRP, ferritin, and D-dimer tend to be more elevated | Generally less elevated than in MIS-C |
| **Blood counts** | Absolute lymphocyte and platelet counts tend to be lower | Generally higher than in MIS-C |
| **Coronary artery aneurysms** | Appear less frequent and resolve more quickly | More characteristic and may persist longer |
| **Epidemiology** | Black and Hispanic children appear disproportionately affected | Higher incidence in East Asia and among children of Asian descent |

(4)

## Typical manifestations of MIS-C

MIS-C may include:

- Persistent fever  
- Abdominal pain, vomiting, or diarrhea  
- Rash  
- Conjunctivitis  
- Shock or myocardial dysfunction  
- Lymphocytopenia  
- Elevated CRP, ESR, and D-dimer  
- Elevated cardiac markers, including troponin and BNP  

Patients commonly present after **three to five days of fever**, followed by shock and/or involvement of multiple organ systems. The clinical findings are summarized in **Table 1**, and the evaluation approach is presented in **Algorithm 1**. (6)

## Typical manifestations of Kawasaki disease

The principal clinical features of KD include:

- Fever  
- Redness of the whites of the eyes  
- Cracked, red lips  
- Red, swollen tongue  
- Rash  
- Swollen cervical lymph nodes  
- Redness and swelling of the palms and soles, followed by peeling of the fingers and toes  

These findings may appear at different times during the illness, and some may have resolved by the time the child is examined. (7) (3)

## Diagnostic challenge

Distinguishing MIS-C from KD can be difficult, particularly because many children have antibodies to SARS-CoV-2. Positive antibodies alone may therefore not reliably distinguish the two conditions. The distinction is especially challenging in children with KD-like MIS-C and positive SARS-CoV-2 antibodies. (8) (4)

MIS-C is thought to involve an abnormal immune response after SARS-CoV-2 infection. Most affected children have positive SARS-CoV-2 serology with a negative PCR test, although some have positive PCR results. Its immunophenotype appears distinct from that of KD and macrophage activation syndrome. (6)

## Clinical importance

The differential diagnosis of MIS-C includes KD unrelated to SARS-CoV-2, bacterial sepsis, severe acute COVID-19, toxic shock syndrome, and appendicitis. (6)

Importantly, any child who meets criteria for KD should receive **intravenous immune globulin (IVIG)** to provide the best protection against coronary artery aneurysms. (4)

## Conclusion

MIS-C and KD share fever, mucocutaneous findings, inflammation, and potential cardiovascular complications. MIS-C is more often associated with older age, prominent gastrointestinal symptoms, myocardial dysfunction, shock, greater inflammatory marker elevation, lymphocytopenia, thrombocytopenia, and less frequent coronary artery aneurysms. KD is more typical in infants and young children and has a stronger association with coronary artery complications. Despite these distinctions, substantial overlap remains, and early cardiac evaluation and specialist consultation are important. (4) (5)
