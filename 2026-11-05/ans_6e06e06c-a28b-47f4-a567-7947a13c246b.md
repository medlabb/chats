## Answer

 ## Hepatic encephalopathy

**Hepatic encephalopathy** is an alteration in mental status and cognitive function caused by liver failure. It is especially common in chronic liver disease; gut-derived neurotoxins, including ammonia and other metabolites, reach the brain because of reduced hepatic clearance and vascular shunting. Ammonia is often elevated, but its level correlates poorly with disease severity and is generally not relied upon alone for diagnosis or monitoring. (1)

### Clinical features

Symptoms may include:

- Sleep-pattern or personality changes, irritability, and mental dullness  
- Confusion and disorientation  
- Agitation, violence, lethargy, or difficulty arousing the patient  
- Stupor and coma in advanced cases  
- **Asterixis** (“liver flap”), elicited by extending the arms and dorsiflexing the wrists (1) (2)

In acute liver failure, encephalopathy can develop rapidly and may be associated with **brain edema and cerebral herniation**. In cirrhosis, common precipitating factors include gastrointestinal bleeding, infection—including spontaneous bacterial peritonitis—constipation, dehydration or volume depletion, electrolyte abnormalities, hyponatremia, overdiuresis, uremia, and narcotic analgesics. (1) (2)

The diagnosis is **clinical**. Alternative causes of altered mental status—particularly electrolyte disturbances, sedative use, and renal or respiratory failure—should be excluded. Mental-status examination and the trail-making test may help detect early disease. (1) (2)

### Management

1. **Identify and treat precipitating factors**
   - Evaluate for infection; if ascites is present, perform diagnostic tapping to assess for infection.
   - Assess for gastrointestinal bleeding.
   - Correct dehydration and electrolyte abnormalities. (1)

2. **Lactulose**
   - This is the mainstay of treatment.
   - It promotes colonic acidification and catharsis, reducing nitrogenous products in the gut.
   - The dose is titrated to achieve **two to three soft stools daily** and is usually continued after the initial episode. (3)

3. **Rifaximin**
   - Used as an adjunct when lactulose is poorly tolerated or for recurrent episodes.
   - The cited dose is **550 mg twice daily**. (3)

4. **Avoid routine protein restriction**
   - Dietary protein restriction is strongly discouraged because its nutritional harms are thought to outweigh its benefits. (3)

Recurrent encephalopathy despite treatment may improve after closure of large portosystemic shunts. Zinc supplementation may sometimes help. Hepatic encephalopathy in chronic liver disease is a poor prognostic sign, although it can be managed in most patients. (3)
