 ## Anemia

**Anemia** is a common medical problem. Its clinical effects depend on how rapidly it develops and the patient’s overall health. Common manifestations include:

- **Tiredness and shortness of breath**
- **Pallor** of the mucosa or conjunctiva
- **Resting tachycardia**
- Possible ischemic symptoms, such as **angina** or transient ischemic attacks/strokes, particularly in patients with atherosclerosis
- **Pica** in iron deficiency and **neuropathy** in vitamin B₁₂ deficiency (1)

### Initial diagnostic approach

Evaluation should include:

1. A detailed history, including prior anemia, iron therapy or transfusions, family history, blood donation, symptoms suggesting underlying disease, gastrointestinal symptoms, and menstrual blood loss.
2. Physical examination for consequences of anemia, such as a cardiac flow murmur, and possible causes, such as splenomegaly or blood in the stool.
3. **Peripheral blood-smear review**, which may identify characteristic red-cell morphology.
4. **Reticulocyte count**, which assesses bone-marrow red-cell production (2).

At least 75% of anemia cases are **hypoproliferative**; common causes include iron deficiency and inflammation (3).

### Important causes and patterns

- **Iron-deficiency anemia:** Typically microcytic, although microcytosis may be absent early or with confounding conditions. Evaluation includes serum iron, total iron-binding capacity, transferrin saturation, and ferritin. Ferritin below 30 μg/L indicates depleted body iron stores, although inflammation can increase ferritin and obscure iron deficiency (3).
- **Vitamin B₁₂ or folate deficiency:** Causes impaired DNA synthesis and typically produces **macrocytosis**; B₁₂ deficiency may also cause neutrophil hypersegmentation and neuropathy (3).
- **Anemia of inflammation:** Usually mild to moderate, normocytic and normochromic, with a low reticulocyte count; it may become microcytic when longstanding. Hypoferremia with hyperferritinemia indicates functional iron deficiency. Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant and is therefore difficult to interpret during inflammation. Table 102-3 is referenced for this assessment, but its contents are not provided (4).
- **Chronic kidney disease:** Anemia is associated with erythropoietin deficiency, inflammation, and iron imbalance; folate or vitamin B₁₂ deficiency and hemolysis may also contribute (4).
- **Bone-marrow disorders:** Aplastic anemia causes marked reduction of marrow precursors along with reduced platelets and neutrophils. Pure red-cell aplasia causes a very low reticulocyte count and absent or markedly reduced erythroid cells; parvovirus B19 is one possible cause (5).
