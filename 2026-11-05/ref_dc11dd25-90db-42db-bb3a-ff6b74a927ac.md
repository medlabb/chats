## ACETAMINOPHEN & NSAIDS

[Table 5–4](#papacmdt26_ch5tb4) provides comparison information for [acetaminophen](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659831), [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322), the COX-2 inhibitor [celecoxib](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660662), and other NSAIDs. An appropriate dose of [acetaminophen](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659831) may be just as effective an analgesic and antipyretic as an NSAID but without the risk of GI bleeding or ulceration. [Acetaminophen](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659831) can be given at a dosage of 500–1000 mg orally every 6 hours, not to exceed 4000 mg/day maximum for short-term use. Total [acetaminophen](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659831) doses should not exceed 3000 mg/day for long-term use or 2000 mg/day for older patients and for those with liver disease. Hepatotoxicity is of particular concern because of how commonly [acetaminophen](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659831) is also an ingredient in various over-the-counter medications and because of failure to account for the [acetaminophen](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659831) dose in combination acetaminophen-opioid medications such as Vicodin or Norco. The FDA has limited the amount of [acetaminophen](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659831) available in some combination analgesics (eg, in [acetaminophen](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659831) plus [codeine](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660118) preparations).
