## Answer

 **Vitiligo treatment** aims to stop or slow depigmentation, restore pigment, and maintain repigmentation. Treatment depends on the extent, location, activity of the disease, age, skin type, and treatment goals. It may take **at least six months** to judge effectiveness. (1)

Main options described in the references include:

- **Narrowband UVB phototherapy (NB-UVB):** First-line therapy for adults and children aged 6 years or older with generalized vitiligo, particularly when larger areas or cosmetically sensitive areas are involved. It is usually given **two to three times weekly**. (2)
- **Excimer laser or lamp:** Uses 308-nm UVB and is mainly indicated for segmental vitiligo or disease affecting less than 10% of the body surface area. (2)
- **PUVA:** Psoralen combined with UVA; topical PUVA may be useful for localized lesions, but oral PUVA is no longer generally recommended in favor of NB-UVB. (2)
- **Depigmentation:** For carefully selected adults with widespread, treatment-resistant vitiligo and little remaining normally pigmented skin. It requires lifelong strict photoprotection and may be permanent. (3)
- **Cosmetic camouflage and psychological support:** Concealing the patches, support groups, and counseling may help address the condition’s psychological impact. (4)

The face, neck, trunk, and mid-extremities generally respond better than the lips and distal extremities. Repigmentation commonly begins around hair follicles or lesion edges, and recurrence of depigmentation after repigmentation is reported at approximately 40%. (1)

The selected references do not provide a complete individualized treatment plan or cover every available treatment option.
