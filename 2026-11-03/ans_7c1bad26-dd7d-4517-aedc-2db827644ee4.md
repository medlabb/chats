## Answer

 **Crohn disease** is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease in which **transmural inflammation** can affect any part of the gastrointestinal tract, commonly the terminal ileum and/or colon. It can cause ulceration, strictures, fistulas, and abscesses. (1) (2)

**Common features include:**
- Intermittent diarrhea, fever, weight loss, fatigue, and abdominal pain—often in the right lower quadrant. (1) (3)
- Tenderness or a palpable abdominal mass. (1) (3)
- Perianal fissures, fistulas, or abscesses. (2)
- Complications such as intestinal narrowing, obstruction, penetrating disease, fistulas, or abscesses. (3) (4)

Diagnosis is supported by **radiographic or endoscopic evidence** of ulceration, stricturing, or fistulas; symptoms alone are not sufficient. (1)

Treatment depends on disease location, severity, and complications. Mild low-risk disease may involve dietary support, symptom treatment, and location-specific medication such as budesonide; moderate-to-severe or high-risk disease generally warrants early biologic therapy, with corticosteroids often used for short-term symptom control rather than long-term maintenance. (5) (6) (7)

A tender abdominal mass with fever and leukocytosis suggests an abscess and requires urgent abdominal CT or MRI; suspected obstruction may require intravenous fluids, nasogastric suction, medication, or surgery depending on the cause and response. (4)
