 ## Principles of Surgery

- **First, do no harm (*primum non nocere*):** Surgery should be recommended only when it offers a clinically significant benefit. Nonsurgical alternatives should be considered when operative risks outweigh expected benefits. (1)

- **Confirm feasibility:** Assess whether the procedure is technically possible based on the patient’s condition, disease complexity, surgeon expertise, and available resources such as the operating room, blood bank, intensive care, life-support equipment, and specialist support. Transfer may be necessary when appropriate resources are unavailable. (1)

- **Assess and optimize risk:** Evaluate the patient’s ability to tolerate surgical and anesthetic stress. Relevant testing may include blood counts, coagulation studies, metabolic and liver panels, cardiac biomarkers, HbA1c, blood typing and crossmatching, urinalysis, and procedure-specific imaging. (2)

- **Use informed consent and shared decision-making:** Discuss the diagnosis, reasonable treatment options—including not operating—their consequences, and the patient’s values and preferences in understandable terms. Consent is an ongoing process rather than merely a signed form. Surrogate decision-makers and advance directives apply when patients cannot speak for themselves. (rf_88)

- **Distinguish elective from emergency surgery:** Emergency surgery generally allows less preparation, requires rapid resource allocation and assessment, carries higher complication and mortality risks, and may involve limited consent. Elective surgery permits comprehensive evaluation and preoperative optimization. These differences are summarized in **Table 1-1**. (rf_f4)

- **Stratify anesthetic risk:** The **ASA classification** describes the severity of systemic disease and helps guide anesthetic planning and postoperative monitoring. Emergency procedures receive an “E” designation, such as ASA III-E. The classification and recommendations are shown in **Table 1-2**. (rf_f4)

- **Prevent infection and promote healing:** Strategies include appropriate, targeted antibiotic selection, bowel preparation when indicated, gentle tissue handling, separate instrument trays when needed, staged procedures or delayed closure in selected cases, and prompt removal of unnecessary urinary catheters, endotracheal tubes, and central lines. (rf_48)

- **Handle tissues carefully:** Wounds should be cleansed of clots, foreign material, and necrotic tissue; irrigated with physiologic solution; and closed atraumatically with accurate approximation of wound margins. (3)

- **Communicate effectively:** Consultations should clearly state the clinical question, the requested scope of management, and the expected response time. Direct communication among providers and involvement of the patient support coordinated perioperative decision-making. (4)
