## Answer

 **Raised LFTs** may involve elevations in AST, ALT, alkaline phosphatase, GGT, bilirubin, or abnormalities in albumin/prothrombin time. (1)

- **AST/ALT predominance** suggests hepatocellular injury; alkaline-phosphatase predominance suggests cholestasis. (1)  
- In **alcohol-related liver disease**, AST is often higher than ALT; an AST:ALT ratio of **≥2:1**, particularly with GGT at least twice normal, strongly suggests this pattern. (1)  
- **Viral hepatitis** can cause elevated liver enzymes; striking AST/ALT elevations may occur early, followed by bilirubin and alkaline-phosphatase elevations. (2)  
- Several **psychotropic medications** can cause transient transaminitis, including **valproate**. Valproate may also cause asymptomatic transaminase elevations, so baseline and periodic LFT monitoring are advised. (1) (3)  
- In hepatic impairment, doses of some antipsychotics and antidepressants may need reduction. (1)
