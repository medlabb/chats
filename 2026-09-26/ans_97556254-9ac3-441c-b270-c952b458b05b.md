## Answer

 **Treatment of bipolar disorder** is organized into three phases: **acute mania**, **acute depression**, and **maintenance treatment** to control symptoms between episodes and prevent relapse. (1)

- **Urgent hospitalization:** Emergency admission should be considered when there is serious risk to the patient or others, including severe aggression, suicidal or homicidal thoughts or actions, dangerous behavior, refusal of treatment, psychosis, or catatonia. (1)

- **Acute mania:** First-line options include **lithium, sodium valproate/divalproex, quetiapine, or risperidone**, either alone or in selected combinations. Other options include aripiprazole, olanzapine, carbamazepine, and haloperidol. **ECT** is generally a second-line treatment but may be used first-line when rapid improvement is needed, such as with high suicide risk, catatonia, or medical stabilization. (2) (3)

- **Acute bipolar depression:** For bipolar I depression, first-line options include **lithium**, **quetiapine**, or **lamotrigine combined with lithium**. Lurasidone and cariprazine may also be effective where available. (2) (4)

- **Maintenance treatment:** Recommended options for bipolar I disorder include **lithium, valproate, or quetiapine** alone. First-line combinations include aripiprazole with lithium or valproate, and quetiapine with lithium or valproate. For bipolar II disorder, quetiapine or lithium monotherapy is recommended as a first-line option for acute depression and maintenance. (2)

- **Psychological and social interventions:** **Psychoeducation** for the patient and family is important, covering relapse warning signs, treatment adherence, medication adverse effects, lithium-related care, and follow-up. **Interpersonal and social rhythm therapy** may be used alongside other treatments to improve regular biological and social rhythms, adherence, stress management, and relapse prevention. (5) (6)
