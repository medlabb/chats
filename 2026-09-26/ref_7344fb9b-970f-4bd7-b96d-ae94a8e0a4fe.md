<small>WHAT IF A PATIENT DOES NOT RESPOND TO A FIRST-LINE TREATMENT?</small>

If optimization of monotherapy with a first-line agent is ineffective, a switch to another first-line monotherapy therapy (e.g., a switch from lithium to quetiapine or vice versa or to cariprazine or lurasidone) or combination therapy (e.g., lamotrigine plus lithium or lurasidone or cariprazine plus a mood stabilizer) is appropriate.

Second-Line Treatments. Lumaterperone has excellent safety and tolerability profile and hence is a good option for patients that have trouble tolerating first-line agents for bipolar depression. The addition of an SSRI or bupropion could be considered for bipolar depressed patients who were unresponsive to optimization of lithium or valproate monotherapy, especially if there is a previous history of response to adjunctive antidepressant therapy. However, antidepressants are not appropriate for patients with a history of rapid cycling or those with mixed features. If antidepressants are used for treating acute bipolar depression, an attempt should be made to taper and discontinue them within 8 weeks of remission of depression.

ECT should be considered for patients who have failed several first-line treatments or for whom the rapid onset of response is paramount (e.g., in the presence of catatonia). ECT may be considered earlier for bipolar depressed patients with psychotic features, those at high risk of suicide, the older adults, and those with rapidly deteriorating physical status due to poor oral intake because of depression.

Valproate monotherapy is appropriate for bipolar depressed patients who had tolerability issues with other first-line monotherapy options. While no data exists, it may also be useful for bipolar depression with mixed features. Olanzapine-fluoxetine combination would also be a reasonable option for patients who remain unresponsive to treatment. Antidepressant adjunctive therapy to other atypical antipsychotics such as aripiprazole or quetiapine may be helpful for some patients.
