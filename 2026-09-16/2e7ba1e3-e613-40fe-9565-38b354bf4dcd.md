## Answer

 **Indications for neonatal exchange transfusion** include:

- **Clinical signs of kernicterus**—an absolute indication at any serum bilirubin level. (1)
- **Failure of intensive phototherapy** to reduce bilirubin to a safe range, especially when the risk of kernicterus exceeds procedural risk. (1)
- **Total serum bilirubin reaching the exchange-transfusion threshold**, based on gestational age and the presence of neurotoxicity risk factors. Thresholds are shown in **Figures 137.9 and 137.10**; risk factors include low albumin, isoimmune hemolysis, G6PD deficiency or other hemolysis, sepsis, and recent clinical instability. (1)
- **Rapidly rising indirect bilirubin**, particularly when levels rise above **20 mg/dL**. (2)
- **Hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN)** with clinically significant anemia and/or hyperbilirubinemia, based on clinical status and the trajectory of hemoglobin or bilirubin. Additional considerations include previous kernicterus, severe HDFN in a sibling, reticulocytosis >15%, and prematurity. (3)
- A **hydropic, severely affected neonate with Rh disease**, who may require exchange at bilirubin levels lower than standard thresholds. (1)

For preterm infants, exchange transfusion is indicated if bilirubin greatly exceeds the suggested maximal level, intensive phototherapy is unsuccessful, or signs of kernicterus are present (**Table 137.6**). (4)

**Partial exchange transfusion** may be considered separately for symptomatic neonatal polycythemia, particularly when hematocrit is **≥75%** and symptoms persist despite aggressive IV hydration. (5)
