## Answer

 **Hepatitis B treatment—supported update from the selected references:**

- **Acute hepatitis B:** Usually managed with supportive care—rest, hydration, adequate nutrition, and avoidance of alcohol. Antiviral studies have not shown clear benefit, although some experts use **nucleos(t)ide analogues** in acute liver failure caused by HBV. (1)
- **Chronic hepatitis B:** Treatment aims to eliminate or permanently suppress HBV, reduce hepatic inflammation, and slow progression to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma. Patients with high viral replication, elevated aminotransferases, or early cirrhosis are the principal candidates for antiviral therapy. (2)
- **Preferred first-line therapy:** Start a nucleos(t)ide analogue with a high genetic barrier to resistance, particularly **entecavir or tenofovir**. The references also list pegylated interferon-α and several other nucleos(t)ide analogues as approved therapies. (2)
- **Treatment response:** Interferon-based therapy is associated with increasing rates of **HBsAg seroclearance** over time; this is less common with nucleos(t)ide analogue therapy. (2)
- **HBV–HDV coinfection:** Nucleos(t)ide analogues do not effectively reduce HDV replication. The cited treatment approach for chronic HDV is weekly **pegylated interferon for at least 48 weeks**; adding a potent nucleos(t)ide analogue when HBV DNA is high is considered logical, although long-term effectiveness remains undefined. (2)
- **Monitoring:** All patients with chronic HBV remain at risk for hepatocellular carcinoma and should undergo risk-based screening; risk decreases as viral load decreases. (2)

The selected references do not provide more recent guideline changes, newer agents, treatment thresholds, or detailed recommendations for special populations.
