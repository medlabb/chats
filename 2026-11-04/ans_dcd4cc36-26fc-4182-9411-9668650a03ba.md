## Answer

 ## Grading and severity of mitral stenosis

Severity is assessed by **mitral valve area (MVA)** together with the **transvalvular pressure gradient and flow rate**. The gradient is flow- and heart-rate-dependent; tachycardia, including rapid atrial fibrillation, increases the gradient and left-atrial pressure. (1)

| Severity | Mitral valve area |
|---|---:|
| Normal | **4–6 cm²** |
| Significant obstruction | **<~2 cm²** |
| Severe MS | **<1.5 cm²** |
| Very severe MS | **<1.0 cm²** |

In severe MS, an elevated left-atrial pressure—approximately **25 mmHg**—may be required to maintain normal cardiac output. With very severe MS, cardiac output may be reduced even at rest. (1)

### Echocardiographic assessment

TTE with Doppler should assess:

- Mitral inflow velocities  
- **Peak and mean transmitral gradients**
- Mitral valve area
- Leaflet mobility, thickening, calcification, and subvalvular disease
- Associated mitral regurgitation
- Left- and right-sided chamber size and function
- Estimated pulmonary artery systolic pressure
- Suitability for percutaneous mitral balloon commissurotomy (PMBC) (2)

When resting findings and symptoms are discordant, exercise TTE can assess the exercise mean mitral gradient, pulmonary artery pressure, and right-ventricular function. (2)

## Criteria for intervention

### Mitral commissurotomy

Mitral commissurotomy is indicated, unless contraindicated, for patients with:

- **Symptoms corresponding to NYHA class II–IV**
- **Isolated severe MS**
- MVA **<1.5 cm²** in normal-sized adults, or approximately **<1 cm²/m² body-surface area** (3)

Commissurotomy is generally **not recommended** for asymptomatic patients or those with mild-to-moderate MS—defined here as MVA **>1.5 cm²**—unless there is:

- Recurrent systemic embolization, or
- Severe pulmonary hypertension: pulmonary artery systolic pressure **>50 mmHg at rest** or **>60 mmHg with exercise**. (4)

In pregnancy, commissurotomy should be performed if pulmonary congestion persists despite intensive medical treatment; PMBC is preferred. (4)

### Choice of PMBC

PMBC is preferred when the valve anatomy is favorable, including:

- Relatively pliable leaflets
- Little or no commissural calcium
- No major subvalvular scarring or thickening
- No left-atrial thrombus
- Associated MR no greater than **2+/4+** (3)

TEE is routinely used to exclude left-atrial thrombus and evaluate MR before PMBC. A lower echocardiographic score—based on leaflet thickening, calcification, mobility, and subvalvular thickening—predicts a greater likelihood of procedural success. (3)

### Surgical commissurotomy

Surgical commissurotomy is considered when PMBC is not feasible or is unsuccessful, including in many patients with restenosis after prior surgery. (3)

Successful commissurotomy is defined as:

- **50% reduction in mean mitral valve gradient**, and
- **Doubling of the mitral valve area**. (4)

### Mitral valve replacement

Mitral valve replacement is necessary when there is:

- Significant associated MR
- Severe distortion of the valve after prior transcatheter or surgical manipulation
- Inability to improve valve function adequately with commissurotomy (4)

When replacement may be required, surgery should generally be reserved for patients with **severe MS (MVA ≤1.5 cm²)** who remain **NYHA class III—symptomatic with ordinary activity—despite optimal medical therapy**. (4)
