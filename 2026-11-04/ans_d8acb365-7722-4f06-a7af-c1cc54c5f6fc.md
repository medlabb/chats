## Answer

 ## Pediatric fever

**Fever** is an abnormal increase in body temperature caused by elevation of the hypothalamic set point. In otherwise healthy children, most fevers are self-limited and benign, and fever itself does not cause brain damage. (1) (2)

### When medical evaluation is recommended

- **Infants younger than 3 months:** rectal temperature **≥38°C (100.4°F)** requires evaluation, even if the infant appears well. Do not give fever medication before consulting a health care provider. (3)
- **Children 3–36 months:** evaluation is recommended for rectal temperature **≥38.9°C (102°F)**, fever lasting more than three days, or an ill appearance such as refusing fluids or marked fussiness. (3)
- **Any age:** temperature **≥40°C (104°F)** by oral, rectal, tympanic, or forehead measurement—or **≥39.4°C (103°F)** axillary—requires evaluation. (3)
- Seek evaluation for **febrile seizure, recurrent fever lasting over seven days, chronic medical conditions, or a new rash**. (3)
- In children aged 3–36 months without an obvious source, rectal temperature **≥39°C (102.2°F)** may warrant evaluation for an occult infection, including urinary tract infection. (4)

### Home care and treatment

- Fever does not always require treatment. A healthy child older than 3 months with a rectal temperature below **38.9°C (102°F)** who is acting normally generally does not require fever medication. (5)
- Encourage **fluids** and reduce activity. Treat primarily when the child is uncomfortable or when specific medical circumstances warrant it. (2) (1)
- If an antipyretic is chosen for a child without a condition affecting medication choice, acetaminophen may be given at **10–15 mg/kg per dose orally every 4–6 hours**, with no more than five doses in 24 hours; maximum daily dose is **75 mg/kg/day, up to 4 g/day**. Dose medications by weight. (1) (2)
- Ibuprofen is an alternative and should be dosed by weight according to the manufacturer’s label. Do **not routinely combine or alternate** acetaminophen and ibuprofen. (1)
- External cooling is generally not recommended for previously well children with a febrile illness. (1)
- Fever lasting beyond **4–5 days**, becoming markedly higher, or developing new localized symptoms should prompt evaluation. (1)
