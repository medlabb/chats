## Answer

 **Plasil (metoclopramide) — older adults**

- **Common effects:** drowsiness, fatigue, dizziness, confusion, restlessness, nausea/diarrhea, and headache. CNS effects are more likely in people **over 65**, those with kidney impairment, or those taking CNS depressants. (1)
- **Movement-related effects:** involuntary movements, facial grimacing, neck spasms, inability to sit still, tremor, rigidity, and Parkinson-like symptoms. **Tardive dyskinesia** may be irreversible and the risk increases with longer treatment and cumulative dose. (2)
- It is listed as a potentially inappropriate medicine in people **65 years and older**, unless used for gastroparesis for less than 12 weeks. Treatment should generally **not continue for 12 weeks or longer**. (3) (4)
- It should be avoided or carefully reviewed in patients with **Parkinson disease, seizures, previous tardive dyskinesia/dystonic reaction, or suspected bowel obstruction, perforation, or bleeding**. (5)

**How to give in an elderly person**

The selected references do **not provide one standard elderly dose**; the dose depends on the indication, formulation, kidney function, and interacting medicines. For reduced kidney function, adult guidance states:  
- CrCl >60 mL/min: no adjustment  
- CrCl >10–60 mL/min: approximately **50% of the usual total daily dose**  
- CrCl ≤10 mL/min: approximately **33% or less** of the usual total daily dose. (6)

Do not use a dose or injection route without the prescriber confirming the indication and kidney function. Rapid IV administration is associated with increased risk of akathisia/dystonia and cardiovascular reactions. (2) (7)
