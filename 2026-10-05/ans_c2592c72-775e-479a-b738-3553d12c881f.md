## Answer

 **Drug interactions relevant to the ER include:**

- **Warfarin + many antibiotics**
- **ACE inhibitors + NSAIDs**
- **Opioids + sedative-hypnotics**  
These combinations can cause clinically significant drug–drug interactions and require careful monitoring. (1)

In older adults, polypharmacy increases adverse drug events because of altered kidney/liver metabolism, interactions with comorbidities, and interactions among multiple medications. Common emergency-hospitalization medications include **sedative-hypnotics, anticoagulants, opioids, and hypoglycemic agents**. (2)

Drug-related cardiac interactions or toxicities may produce:

- **QT prolongation/torsades:** citalopram, droperidol, methadone, sertraline, sotalol, and class Ia/III antiarrhythmics
- **Bradycardia or AV block:** β-blockers, calcium-channel blockers, clonidine, and digitalis glycosides
- **Wide QRS:** tricyclic antidepressants, bupropion, lamotrigine, diphenhydramine, and some antiarrhythmics (3)

Accurate **medication reconciliation** should include prescription drugs, over-the-counter medications, supplements, analgesics, and sleep aids. (1)
