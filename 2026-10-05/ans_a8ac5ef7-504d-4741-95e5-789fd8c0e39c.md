## Answer

 **Hypothyroidism** is caused by inadequate thyroid hormone production. (1)

### Hypothyroidism in pregnancy
- Routine screening of asymptomatic pregnant women is not supported by sufficient evidence. Testing is recommended for women with a personal or family history of thyroid disease, type 1 diabetes, or symptoms/signs of thyroid disease. (2)
- Evaluation includes **TSH**; in suspected hypothyroidism, **free T4 (FT4)** and/or the free thyroxine index should also be measured. (2)
- Treatment is with **levothyroxine**, adjusted to normalize TSH. Thyroxine requirements generally increase during pregnancy. The dose may be adjusted every **4–6 weeks** in **25–50 μg increments** until TSH normalizes, then thyroid levels should be checked once per trimester. (3)
- The pediatrician should be informed of the maternal thyroid diagnosis because neonates may be at risk for thyroid dysfunction. (1)

**Postpartum thyroiditis** may cause hypothyroidism during the first year after childbirth. It occurs in approximately 5%–10% of women without a prior history of thyroid disease; about 40% of those with hypothyroidism require treatment, and approximately 11% develop permanent hypothyroidism. (4)
