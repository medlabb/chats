## Answer

 **Postoperative care in neonates** supported by the selected references includes:

- **Supportive medical management:** Continue parenteral nutrition and intravenous antibiotics after surgery for spontaneous intestinal perforation (SIP). (rf_26)
- **Drain or ostomy monitoring:** Monitor output from the peritoneal drain or ostomy. (rf_26)
- **Nutrition:** Introduce small enteral “trophic” feeds gradually. Increase feeds when bowel function is evident, such as stooling or ostomy output. A dietitian may help optimize nutrition and manage complications of parenteral nutrition. (rf_26)
- **Surgical follow-up:** Continue pediatric surgical care to assess recovery and manage complications such as strictures, incisional hernias, and intestinal adhesions that may cause obstruction. (rf_26)
- **Respiratory and cardiorespiratory monitoring:** For ex-premature infants with postmenstrual age (PMA) ≤60 weeks, one described approach is continuous pulse oximetry and ECG with recovery-room-level nursing care overnight. If apnea occurs during the first 12 hours, ICU admission for closer monitoring is recommended. Infants with PMA >60 weeks may receive standard recovery-room care. (1)
- **Discharge planning:** Parents should receive instructions on postoperative care, with coordination between NICU and surgical teams and arranged follow-up after discharge. (2)

The selected references do not provide a comprehensive general neonatal postoperative protocol covering all operations, including detailed pain control, wound care, fluid management, or temperature management.
