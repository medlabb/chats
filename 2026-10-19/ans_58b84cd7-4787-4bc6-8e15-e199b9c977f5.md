## Answer

 Tenderness over the **sternum and ribs**, especially when the pain is reproducible by pressing on the area, often suggests a **musculoskeletal cause**. However, this does **not completely exclude acute coronary syndrome**: up to 15% of patients with ACS may have reproducible chest-wall tenderness. (1) (2)

Other patterns may provide clues:

- Sharp pain worsened by deep breathing, coughing, sneezing, or movement may indicate **pleuritic pain**. (3)
- Pain worse when lying flat and improved when upright may occur with **pericarditis**. (4)
- Pain reproduced by pulling upward and forward beneath the costal margin may suggest **slipping rib syndrome**. (2)

Because chest pain can have serious causes and tenderness alone cannot rule them out, an evaluation may include vital signs, examination, and—when appropriate—an ECG and cardiac biomarkers. (5) (2)
