## Answer

 ## Cardiogenic shock

**Cardiogenic shock** is inadequate tissue and end-organ perfusion caused by the heart’s inability to pump sufficient blood, resulting in reduced cardiac output. It may result from left- and/or right-ventricular systolic dysfunction. (1) (2)

### Causes

- **Cardiomyopathic:** large myocardial infarction, severe right-ventricular infarction, acute decompensation of severe dilated cardiomyopathy, stunned myocardium, myocarditis, or myocardial depression associated with advanced septic or neurogenic shock. (3)
- **Arrhythmic:** sustained ventricular tachycardia, complete heart block, or other severe tachyarrhythmias and bradyarrhythmias that markedly reduce cardiac output. (3)
- **Mechanical:** acute severe mitral or aortic insufficiency, papillary muscle or chordal rupture, ventricular septal rupture, aortic dissection involving the aortic valve, atrial myxoma, or ventricular free-wall rupture. (3)

### Clinical and hemodynamic features

Patients commonly have **hypotension**, pulmonary edema or congestion, diffuse lung crackles, distended neck veins, poor ventricular function, and valvular or septal abnormalities on echocardiography. (4)

Typical hemodynamic findings include:

- Low cardiac index—generally **<2.2 L/min/m²**, with diagnostic thresholds of **<1.8 L/min/m² without support** or **<2.2 L/min/m² with support**
- Elevated pulmonary capillary wedge pressure, usually **>15 mmHg**
- Often elevated systemic vascular resistance
- Reduced mixed venous oxygen saturation (5) (6)

On pulmonary artery catheterization, cardiogenic shock typically shows low cardiac output, elevated PCWP, and high SVR; SvO₂ is often below 65–70 percent. (4)

### Etiologic clues

- **Myocardial infarction:** crushing substernal chest pain, acute dyspnea, elevated cardiac enzymes, and ECG evidence of infarction.
- **Arrhythmia:** sudden onset with palpitations or syncope and an abnormal telemetry or ECG finding.
- **Mechanical complication:** acute pulmonary edema and a new murmur after recent myocardial infarction.
- **Myocarditis:** pleuritic chest pain and a pericardial rub. (4)

Diagnosis may be supported by echocardiography and, when needed, pulmonary artery catheterization. Hemodynamic assessment can also help identify mechanical complications such as ventricular septal rupture, mitral regurgitation, and tamponade. (6)
