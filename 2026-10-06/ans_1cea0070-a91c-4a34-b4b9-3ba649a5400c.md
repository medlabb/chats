## Answer

 **Bleeding gastric ulcer requires urgent hospital management**, particularly with hematemesis, melena, hemodynamic instability, or heavy/rapid bleeding. Assess hemodynamic stability and provide IV fluids and blood transfusion when required; keep the patient **NPO** until an endoscopic plan is established. (1) (2) (3)

### Treatment

1. **IV proton-pump inhibitor (PPI)**
   - Give an IV PPI during initial management, especially in severe or active bleeding; an example is esomeprazole 80 mg IV bolus. If endoscopy is delayed beyond 12 hours, a further IV dose may be given. (4) (5)
   - After endoscopy, ulcers with active bleeding, a visible vessel, or an adherent clot should receive high-dose PPI infusion—for example, esomeprazole 8 mg/hour—for **72 hours**, followed by high-dose oral PPI if there is no recurrent bleeding. (5) (6)

2. **Urgent upper endoscopy**
   - Perform endoscopy after stabilization, generally within **24 hours**; it identifies the bleeding source, assesses risk, and allows treatment. (6)
   - Active bleeding or a visible vessel requires endoscopic hemostasis using **thermal coagulation or clips**, with or without epinephrine injection. **Epinephrine must not be used alone**. Hemostatic spray or over-the-scope clips may be considered when conventional methods fail or bleeding recurs. (6) (2)
   - Ulcers without high-risk stigmata can generally be managed with acid suppression alone. (1)

3. **If bleeding persists or recurs**
   - Repeat endoscopic treatment may be used; if unsuccessful, **angiographic embolization** or, rarely, surgery may be required. (2) (7)

4. **Prevent recurrence**
   - Treat the underlying cause, including eliminating precipitating factors such as **Helicobacter pylori** and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. (8)
