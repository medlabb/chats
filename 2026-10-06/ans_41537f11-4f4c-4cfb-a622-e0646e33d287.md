## Answer

 Management depends on the presentation:

- **Likely viral upper respiratory infection/viral rhinosinusitis:** During the first 10 days, the illness is generally presumed viral and is managed with **supportive care**, including nasal saline, humidification, and fluids. Nasal saline may improve congestion, rhinorrhea, and postnasal drip. (1) (2)
- **Decongestants:** Pseudoephedrine may be used as needed, but caution is advised with prostatic hypertrophy or poorly controlled hypertension. Oxymetazoline may be used for no more than **3–5 days**, since prolonged use can cause rhinitis medicamentosa. Antihistamines are recommended only when allergy exacerbation is present. (2)
- **Intranasal corticosteroids:** These may be used alone in mild-to-moderate acute rhinosinusitis or with antibiotics in severe cases. (2)
- **Suspected acute bacterial rhinosinusitis:** Consider this when symptoms persist beyond 10 days or worsen after initial improvement. Antibiotics are indicated when symptoms persist beyond 7–10 days; typical treatment lasts 5–10 days. First-line therapy is amoxicillin with or without clavulanate. (1) (3)
- **Failure of therapy:** If there is no improvement after 72 hours, or if antibiotics were used during the previous 4–6 weeks, the antibiotic should be changed; culture-directed treatment is preferred when possible. (3)
- **Children:** Antibiotics should generally not be given during the first 10 days of uncomplicated rhinosinusitis. Recommended agents and dosing are provided in the selected reference. (3)
- **Sore throat/adenotonsillitis:** Analgesics and antipyretics are used; antibiotics are reserved when bacterial infection is suspected. Throat culture or testing for group A β-hemolytic *Streptococcus* may be performed when clinically indicated. (4) (rf_11)

Blood tests are usually not helpful unless the patient is toxic or immunosuppressed, or complications such as orbital or intracranial extension are suspected. (3) The selected references do not provide a complete general management protocol for all upper respiratory tract infections.
