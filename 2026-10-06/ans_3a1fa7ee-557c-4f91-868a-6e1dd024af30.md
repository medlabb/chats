## Answer

 - **Vitamin B12:** Serum B12 is measured in patients with eosinophilia when there are features suggesting a hematologic, malignant, or immunologic disorder—such as an abnormal blood smear, anemia, thrombocytopenia, lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, or findings suggestive of systemic mastocytosis. The selected references do not specify how to interpret an abnormal B12 level or its diagnostic value. (1)

- **Tryptase:** Tryptase helps assess **mast-cell activation or an associated myeloid disorder**. Persistently elevated baseline tryptase may occur with systemic mastocytosis, hereditary alpha-tryptasemia, chronic eosinophilic leukemia, myelodysplastic syndromes, and other conditions. In patients with eosinophilia and suspected systemic mastocytosis, tryptase is therefore part of the evaluation. (2; 3; 4) Baseline tryptase above **20 ng/mL on at least two occasions** should raise strong suspicion for systemic mastocytosis, although the absolute level does not determine the disease category.
