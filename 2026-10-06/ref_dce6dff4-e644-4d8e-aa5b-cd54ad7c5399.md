## TREATMENT

### A. Candidal Esophagitis

Systemic therapy is required for esophageal candidiasis. An empiric trial of antifungal therapy is often administered without performing diagnostic endoscopy. Initial therapy is generally with [fluconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660475), 400 mg orally on day 1, then 200–400 mg/day for 14–21 days. Patients not responding to empiric therapy within 3–5 days should undergo endoscopy with brushings, biopsy, and culture to distinguish resistant fungal infection from other infections (eg, CMV, herpes). Esophageal candidiasis not responding to [fluconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660475) therapy may be treated with [itraconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660506) suspension (not capsules), 200 mg/day orally, or [voriconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658849), 200 mg orally twice daily. Refractory infection may be treated intravenously with [micafungin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658943) 150 mg daily or [caspofungin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658776) 50 mg daily for 28 days. Consultation with an infectious disease specialist is advised.

## TREATMENT

### A. Mucosal Candidiasis

#### 1. Vulvovaginal candidiasis

This condition can be treated with topical or oral azoles. A single 150-mg oral dose of [fluconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660475) is equivalent to topical treatments with better patient acceptance. Topical azole preparations include [clotrimazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660263), 100-mg vaginal tablet for 7 days, or [miconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660188), 200-mg vaginal suppository for 3 days. Disease recurrence is common but can be decreased with weekly oral [fluconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660475) therapy (150 mg weekly). Vulvovaginal candidiasis caused by non-*albicans* strains—eg, *C glabrata*—may require alternative therapies (such as intravaginal boric acid) in the setting of azole resistance. Oral ibrexafungerp (voluntarily recalled in 2023 due to potential contamination), a highly bioavailable glucan synthase inhibitor, may treat vulvovaginal candidiasis from any disease-causing strains, including azole-resistant pathogens. [Oteseconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659605) is an alternative treatment option for recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis.

#### 2. Esophageal candidiasis

If patients can swallow and take adequate amounts of fluid orally, [fluconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660475), 200–400 mg orally daily for 14–21 days, is recommended. Patients who are unable to tolerate oral therapy should receive intravenous [fluconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660475), 400 mg daily, or an echinocandin for 14–21 days. Options for patients with fluconazole-refractory disease include 14–21 days of oral [itraconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660506) solution, 200 mg daily; oral or intravenous [voriconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658849), 200 mg twice daily; oral [posaconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658980), 300 mg daily; or an intravenous echinocandin ([caspofungin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658776), 70-mg loading dose, then 50 mg/day; [anidulafungin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658968), 200 mg/day; or intravenous [micafungin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658943), 150 mg/day). Relapse is common with all agents in persons with HIV without adequate immune reconstitution.
