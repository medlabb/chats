## Answer

 ## **Vancomycin**

- **Class/mechanism:** Bactericidal glycopeptide that binds the terminal **D-Ala-D-Ala** sequence of nascent peptidoglycan, inhibiting transglycosylation, peptidoglycan-chain elongation, and cross-linking. Its killing is **time-dependent**. (1) (2)
- **Spectrum/uses:** Narrow activity against gram-positive organisms; used for serious infections caused by drug-resistant gram-positive bacteria, including **MRSA**. It may be combined with ceftriaxone for penicillin-resistant pneumococcal infections. Oral vancomycin is not absorbed systemically and is used for **Clostridioides difficile** infection. (1)
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Parenteral vancomycin penetrates most tissues and is eliminated unchanged in urine. **Dose modification is mandatory in renal impairment.** The provided drug summary table lists parenteral administration, oral use for *C. difficile* colitis, and renal elimination for IV therapy. (1) (3)
- **Adverse effects:** Chills, fever, phlebitis, **ototoxicity**, and **nephrotoxicity**. Rapid IV infusion may cause diffuse flushing due to histamine release. (1)
- **Resistance:** Vancomycin resistance in enterococci and staphylococci may result from replacement of terminal D-Ala with D-lactate, reducing drug binding. VRE and vancomycin-intermediate *S. aureus* are clinically important. (1)
- **Hemodialysis:** The reference states that vancomycin is not removed from blood by hemodialysis. (2)

The selected references do not provide specific dosing regimens or therapeutic drug-monitoring targets.
