## Answer

 **Ceftriaxone** is a **parenteral, third-generation cephalosporin**. It is bactericidal, acting by binding penicillin-binding proteins and inhibiting bacterial cell-wall synthesis. (1)

- **Spectrum:** Active against many gram-negative organisms and is among the most active cephalosporins against penicillin-resistant pneumococci, although resistance occurs. (1)  
- **CNS penetration:** Third-generation cephalosporins generally penetrate the blood–brain barrier. (1)  
- **Elimination:** Excreted mainly in the **bile**. (1)  
- **Clinical uses:** Pneumonia, meningitis, gonorrhea, and other serious infections; it is a drug of choice for gonorrhea. (2)  
- **Acute otitis media:** A single injection is usually as effective as a 10-day course of amoxicillin. (3)  
- **Adverse effects and precautions:** Cephalosporins may cause allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, injection-site pain, and phlebitis. Complete cross-hypersensitivity among cephalosporins should be assumed; patients with prior penicillin anaphylaxis should not receive a cephalosporin. (3)
