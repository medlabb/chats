Do we use a synchronized D.C shock if the patient is known to have AF, and now his heart rate is 180 and is shocked?

## Answer

 **Yes—if the patient with AF and a ventricular rate of 180/min is hemodynamically unstable**—for example, hypotension, myocardial ischemia/angina, pulmonary edema, or shock—use **immediate synchronized direct-current cardioversion**. (1) (2)

If the patient is **hemodynamically stable**, immediate synchronized cardioversion is usually not the first approach; rate control is generally appropriate, while considering the duration of AF and thromboembolic risk. (1) (3)

For AF, the suggested initial synchronized cardioversion energy is **120–200 J**. (4) Anticoagulation and/or assessment for atrial thrombus is important when cardioversion is not emergent, particularly if AF has lasted **≥48 hours**. (1)
