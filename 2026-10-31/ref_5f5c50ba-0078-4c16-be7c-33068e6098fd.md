## OTHER CELL WALL OR MEMBRANE-ACTIVE AGENTS

#### A. [Vancomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660261)

[Vancomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660261) is a bactericidal glycoprotein that binds to the <small class="uppercase">D</small>

-Ala-<small class="uppercase">D</small>

-Ala terminal of the nascent peptidoglycan pentapeptide side chain and inhibits transglycosylation. This action prevents elongation of the peptidoglycan chain and interferes with cross-linking. Resistance in strains of enterococci (vancomycin-resistant enterococci [VRE]) and staphylococci (vancomycin-resistant *S aureus* [VRSA]) involves a decreased affinity of [vancomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660261) for the binding site because of the replacement of the terminal <small class="uppercase">D</small>

-Ala by <small class="uppercase">D</small>

-lactate. [Vancomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660261) has a narrow spectrum of activity and is used for serious infections caused by drug-resistant gram-positive organisms, including methicillin-resistant staphylococci (MRSA), and in combination with a third-generation cephalosporin such as [ceftriaxone](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660417) for treatment of infections due to penicillin-resistant pneumococci (PRSP). Oral [vancomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660261) is not absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract and can be used in the treatment of infections caused by *Clostridioides* (formerly *Clostridium*) *difficile*, although [fidaxomicin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659102) reportedly provides better outcomes. When given parenterally, [vancomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660261) penetrates most tissues and is eliminated unchanged in the urine. Dosage modification is mandatory in patients with renal impairment. Toxic effects of [vancomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660261) include chills, fever, phlebitis, ototoxicity, and nephrotoxicity. Rapid intravenous infusion may cause diffuse flushing from histamine release. **Teicoplanin** and **[telavancin,](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659065)** other glycopeptide derivatives, have similar characteristics.

Vancomycin-resistant enterococci are increasing and pose a potentially serious clinical problem because such organisms usually exhibit multiple-drug resistance. Vancomycin-intermediate strains of *S aureus* resulting in treatment failures have also been reported.

#### B. [Daptomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658899)

[Daptomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658899) is a cyclic lipopeptide with spectrum similar to [vancomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660261) but active against vancomycin-resistant strains of enterococci and staphylococci. The drug inserts into the cytoplasmic membrane, causing potassium leak and cell death. [Daptomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658899) is eliminated via the kidney. Creatine phosphokinase should be monitored because [daptomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658899) may cause myopathy.

#### C. [Fosfomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660602)

[Fosfomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660602) is an antimetabolite inhibitor of cytosolic enolpyruvate transferase. This action prevents the formation of *N*-acetylmuramic acid, an essential precursor molecule for peptidoglycan chain formation. Resistance to [fosfomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660602) occurs via decreased intracellular accumulation of the drug.

[Fosfomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660602) is excreted by the kidney, with urinary levels exceeding the minimal inhibitory concentrations (MICs) for many urinary tract pathogens. However, with multiple dosing, resistance emerges rapidly and diarrhea is common. [Fosfomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660602) may be synergistic with beta-lactam and quinolone antibiotics in specific infections.

#### D. [Bacitracin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660020)

[Bacitracin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660020) is a peptide antibiotic that interferes with a late stage in cell wall synthesis in gram-positive organisms. Because of its marked nephrotoxicity, the drug is limited to topical use.

Some bactericidal agents (aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones) cause **concentration-dependent** killing. Maximizing peak blood levels of such drugs increases the rate and the extent of their bactericidal effects. This is one of the factors responsible for the clinical effectiveness of high-dose, once-daily administration of aminoglycosides. Other bactericidal agents (beta-lactams, [vancomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660261)) cause **time-dependent** killing. Their killing action is independent of drug concentration and continues only while blood levels are maintained above the MBC.

Inhibition of bacterial growth that continues after antibiotic blood concentrations have fallen to low levels is called the **postantibiotic effect (PAE).** The mechanisms of PAE are unclear but may reflect the lag time required by bacteria to synthesize new enzymes and cellular components, the possible persistence of antibiotic at the target site, or an enhanced susceptibility of bacteria to phagocytic and other defense mechanisms including postantibiotic leucocyte enhancement. PAE is another factor contributory to the effectiveness of once-daily administration of aminoglycosides and may also contribute to the clinical efficacy of the fluoroquinolones.

##### 2. Drug elimination mechanisms

Changes in hepatic and renal function—and the use of dialysis—can influence the pharmacokinetics of antimicrobials and may necessitate dosage modifications. The major mechanisms of elimination of commonly used antimicrobial drugs are shown in [Table 51–3](#katzrev14_ch51tb3). In anuria (creatinine clearance <5 mL/min), the elimination half-life of drugs that are eliminated by the kidney is markedly increased, usually necessitating major reductions in drug dosage. [Erythromycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660073), [clindamycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660079), [chloramphenicol](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659966), [rifampin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660083), and [ketoconazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660439) are notable exceptions, requiring no change in dosage in renal failure. Drugs contraindicated in renal impairment include [cidofovir](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660591), nalidixic acid, long-acting sulfonamides, and [tetracyclines](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660066). Dosage adjustment may be needed in patients with hepatic impairment for drugs including amprenavir, [chloramphenicol](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659966), [clindamycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660079), [erythromycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660073), indinavir, [metronidazole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659985), and [tigecycline](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658949). Dialysis, especially hemodialysis, may markedly decrease the plasma levels of many antimicrobials; supplementary doses of such drugs may be required to reestablish effective plasma levels after these procedures. Drugs that are *not* removed from the blood by hemodialysis include amphotericin B, cefonicid, cefoperazone, [ceftriaxone](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660417), [erythromycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660073), [nafcillin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660408), [tetracyclines](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660066), and [vancomycin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660261).
