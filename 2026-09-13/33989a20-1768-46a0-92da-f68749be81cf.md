## Answer

 For **extremely low-birth-weight (ELBW) infants**, the selected references do **not mention or support glibenclamide**, so its role cannot be determined from these references.

Management described is:

- **Frequent glucose monitoring** and avoidance of unnecessarily excessive glucose delivery. In ELBW infants, the GIR may be reduced if hyperglycaemia develops, but it should generally not be reduced below **4 mg/kg/min**. (1)
- Use **early amino-acid infusion** and consider intravenous lipids while maintaining adequate nutrition. (1)
- **Routine insulin is not recommended** because early insulin has little demonstrated clinical benefit and increases hypoglycaemia risk. (2)
- Insulin may be used occasionally for hyperglycaemia that does not respond to reducing the GIR; the cited dose is **0.05–0.1 units/kg/hour**, with frequent glucose monitoring. (2)

Therefore, based on the selected references: **do not use glibenclamide routinely; insulin is reserved for persistent, nonresponsive hyperglycaemia rather than used routinely.**
