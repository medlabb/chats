##### Interventional Treatment

Interventional treatment is an option for selected patients with acute ischemic stroke, but not TIA. Current guidelines regarding the timing of therapy in relation to stroke onset are summarized in [Table 13-5](#greenberg11_ch13tb5).

1. **Intravenous thrombolysis—**Intravenous administration of [alteplase](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660465) within 4.5 hours of the onset of symptoms (or within 9 hours if imaging studies demonstrate diffusion-perfusion mismatch) can reduce disability and mortality from acute ischemic stroke. Treatment should be started within 60 minutes of the patient’s arrival at the hospital, which provides time for diagnosis and evaluation of possible contraindications. ##### Statins

Treatment with a statin (eg, atorvastatin 80 mg orally daily) is recommended for all patients with prior TIA or ischemic stroke.


##### Blood Pressure Control

Angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitors (or perhaps angiotensin receptor blockers) and diuretics appear to be most effective in reducing the risk of recurrent stroke.


##### Antiplatelet Drugs

Patients with prior non-cardioembolic TIA or ischemic stroke should receive [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) (81-325 mg/d), with or without a 21-day course of [clopidogrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658818) (600 mg, then 75 mg/d for 20 days); [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322)/[dipyridamole](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660297) (25/200 mg twice daily); or [clopidogrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658818) (75 mg/d) alone. For patients whose TIA or stroke occurred while taking [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322), it is unclear if increasing the dose or substituting another antiplatelet drug confers additional benefit. There is no evidence that anticoagulation or the combination of antiplatelet therapy and anticoagulation is effective in this setting.


##### Anticoagulation

Patients with prior TIA or ischemic stroke and **valvular atrial fibrillation** (atrial fibrillation with moderate to severe mitral stenosis or a mechanical heart valve) or **mechanical aortic or mitral valve replacement** without atrial fibrillation should be given long-term [warfarin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660008) treatment targeted to an INR of 2.5 ± 0.5. Low-dose [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) is added for patients with mechanical valves who are at low risk for bleeding complications. **Nonvalvular atrial fibrillation** should be treated with [apixaban](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659135), [dabigatran](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659087), [rivaroxaban](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659108), [edoxaban](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659219), or [warfarin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660008) (INR 2.5 ± 0.5). Short-term (~3 months) anticoagulation with [warfarin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660008) is indicated for prior TIA or ischemic stroke with **acute myocardial infarction** or **cardiomyopathy** when either is complicated by mural thrombus. [Warfarin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660008) for 3-6 months followed by long-term low-dose [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) is recommended for recipients of **bioprosthetic valves**. Either antiplatelet drugs or anticoagulation with [warfarin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660008) can be used in **rheumatic valvular disease** without atrial fibrillation.


##### Surgical Treatment

**Carotid endarterectomy** or **stenting** for secondary prevention of TIA or stroke is as described earlier for treatment of TIA or acute ischemic stroke.

#### PRIMARY PREVENTION


##### Lifestyle

Moderate-intensity physical **activity** for ≥150 minutes per week, or vigorous-intensity physical activity for ≥75 minutes per week, is recommended. A **diet** low in salt, *trans* fats, processed meats, refined carbohydrates, and sweetened beverages and rich in vegetables, fruits, nuts, whole grains, legumes, lean meat, and fish may reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease, including stroke. **Weight** reduction in overweight or obese patients, cessation of **smoking**, and moderation of heavy **[alcohol](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659379)** use are advised.

**Obstructive sleep apnea** is associated with atrial fibrillation and increased stroke risk. Treatment with devices that provide continuous positive airway pressure during sleep may reduce this risk, but such an effect has not been proven.


##### Statins

Treatment with a statin (eg, atorvastatin 20 mg orally daily) is recommended for patients 45-70 years of age with LDL cholesterol ≥190 mg/dL, diabetes, or >10% 10-year risk for cardiovascular events (see [http://tools.acc.org/ASCVD-Risk-Estimator/](http://tools.acc.org/ASCVD-Risk-Estimator/)).


##### Blood Pressure Control

Blood pressure should be reduced by lifestyle modification, antihypertensive drugs, or both for patients with hypertension (>130-140 mm Hg systolic or >80-90 mm Hg diastolic pressure).


##### Glycemic Control

Diabetes increases the risk of stroke and should be treated by dietary modification, exercise, and pharmacologic measures (eg, [metformin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660576)) where necessary, although the relationship between intensity of glycemic control and stroke incidence is unclear. Stroke risk in diabetics can be reduced further by statins and antihypertensive treatment, where indicated.


##### Antiplatelet Drugs

Low-dose **[aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322)** (81-100 mg/d) may reduce the risk of stroke in patients with increased (>10%) 10-year risk for such events (see [http://tools.acc.org/ASCVD-Risk-Estimator/](http://tools.acc.org/ASCVD-Risk-Estimator/)), but its use should be balanced against the risk for bleeding complications in a given patient.


##### Anticoagulation

Patients with **valvular atrial fibrillation** (atrial fibrillation with moderate to severe mitral stenosis or a mechanical heart valve) should be anticoagulated with [warfarin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660008), targeted to an international normalized ratio (INR) of 2.5 ± 0.5. In **nonvalvular atrial fibrillation**, the **CHA<sub>2</sub>DS<sub>2</sub>-VASc score** is used to guide therapy. This score assigns 2 points each for age ≥75 years and a history of transient ischemic attack or stroke, and 1 point each for congestive heart failure, treated or untreated hypertension, diabetes, peripheral arterial disease or aortic plaque or history of myocardial infarction, age 65-74 years, and female sex. Patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation and CHA<sub>2</sub>DS<sub>2</sub>-VASc score ≥2 (for men) or ≥3 (for women) should receive anticoagulation, with [dabigatran](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659087), [rivaroxaban](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659108), [apixaban](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659135), or [edoxaban](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659219) preferred over [warfarin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660008) in this setting. Options for those with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation and lower CHA<sub>2</sub>DS<sub>2</sub>-VASc scores include anticoagulation, [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322), or no treatment, depending on individual circumstances. The role of anticoagulation in reducing stroke risk related to other cardiac disorders is less clear.

###### C. Chronic (Transformed) Migraine

Episodic migraine can change its clinical features over months to years, evolving into a chronic headache syndrome with nearly daily pain. The headache often loses its classic migrainous features. Obesity, prior headache frequency, and [caffeine](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659955) use are associated risk factors.


###### D. Medication Overuse Headache

Overuse of medication for acute headache may be associated with chronic migraine, but whether overmedication is the cause, or indicates worsening of the underlying disorder, is often unclear. Most patients diagnosed with medication overuse headache have migraine as their underlying problem and have overused simple analgesics or triptans, but patients with other types of headache and those using other classes of drugs are also susceptible. Headache is characteristically present at least 15 days per month for at least 3 months. Treatment involves tapering or withdrawing the offending medications and beginning prophylactic headache therapy. Glucocorticoids may be useful for bridge therapy to control headache during drug withdrawal.


###### E. Migraine Postdrome

Postdromal symptoms, including exhaustion, mood changes (eg, euphoria, elation), and cognitive impairment, occur in the majority of migraine patients and may persist for hours to days following a migraine episode.
