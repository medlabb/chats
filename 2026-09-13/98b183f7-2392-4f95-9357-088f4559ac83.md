### [ASPIRIN](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322)

The most widely used antiplatelet agent worldwide is [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322). As a cheap and effective antiplatelet drug, [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) serves as the foundation of most antiplatelet strategies.

#### Mechanism of Action

[Aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) produces its antithrombotic effect by irreversibly acetylating and inhibiting platelet cyclooxygenase (COX)-1 **([Fig. 123-3](#hpim22_ch123fg3))**, a critical enzyme in the biosynthesis of thromboxane A<sub>2</sub>. At high doses (~1 g/d), [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) also inhibits COX-2, an inducible COX isoform found in endothelial cells and inflammatory cells. In endothelial cells, COX-2 initiates the synthesis of prostacyclin, a potent vasodilator and inhibitor of platelet aggregation.

Vorapaxar inhibits thrombin-mediated platelet activation by targeting protease-activated receptor-1 (PAR-1), the major [thrombin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659045) receptor on human platelets.*

#### Indications

[Aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) is widely used for secondary prevention of cardiovascular events in patients with established coronary artery, cerebral artery, or peripheral artery disease. Compared with placebo in this setting, [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) produces a 25% reduction in the risk of cardiovascular death, MI, or stroke. [Aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) is no longer used routinely for primary prevention because recent studies suggest that the risk of gastrointestinal and intracerebral hemorrhage outweigh the benefits. Consequently, [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) is only recommended for primary cardiac prevention if the baseline cardiovascular risk is at least 1% per year over 10 years and patients are at low risk for bleeding.

#### Dosages

[Aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) is usually administered at doses of 75–325 mg once daily. Higher doses of [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) are not more effective than lower [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) doses, and some analyses suggest reduced efficacy with higher doses. Because the side effects of [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) are dose-related, daily [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) doses of 75–100 mg are recommended for most indications. When rapid platelet inhibition is required, an initial [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) dose of at least 160 mg should be given.

#### Side Effects

The most common side effects are gastrointestinal and range from dyspepsia to erosive gastritis or peptic ulcers with bleeding and perforation. These side effects are dose related. Use of enteric-coated or buffered [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) in place of plain [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) does not eliminate gastrointestinal side effects. The overall risk of major bleeding with [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) is 1–3% per year. The risk of bleeding is increased two- to threefold when [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) is given in conjunction with other antiplatelet drugs, such as [clopidogrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658818) or [ticagrelor](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659122), or with anticoagulants, such as [warfarin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660008). When dual or triple therapy is prescribed, low-dose [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) should be given (75–100 mg daily). Eradication of *Helicobacter pylori* infection and administration of proton pump inhibitors may reduce the risk of aspirin-induced upper gastrointestinal bleeding in patients with peptic ulcer disease.

##### Antiplatelet Drugs (See [Chap. 123](/content.aspx?legacysectionid=hpim22_ch123))

Initial treatment should begin with the cyclooxygenase inhibitor [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) with a dose of 150–325 mg of an oral non-enteric-coated preparation or 75–250 mg intravenously. Thereafter, lower oral doses (75–100 mg/d) are recommended since they maintain efficacy while causing less bleeding. Contraindications are severe active bleeding and [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) allergy.

In the absence of a high risk for bleeding, patients with NSTE-ACS, irrespective of whether an invasive or conservative strategy (see below) is selected, should also receive a platelet P2Y<sub>12</sub> receptor blocker to inhibit platelet activation. There are now three oral and one intravenous P2Y<sub>12</sub> inhibitors available. The thienopyridine [clopidogrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658818) is an inactive prodrug that is converted into an active metabolite that causes irreversible blockade of the platelet P2Y<sub>12</sub> receptor. The loading dose of [clopidogrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658818) is 300–600 mg, whereas the maintenance dose is 75 mg daily. When [clopidogrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658818) is added to [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322), so-called dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT), in patients with NSTE-ACS, it confers a 20% relative reduction in cardiovascular death, MI, or stroke, compared to [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) alone but is associated with a moderate (absolute 1%) increase in major bleeding.

Two other P2Y<sub>12</sub> inhibitors have been shown to be superior to [clopidogrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658818) in preventing recurrent cardiac ischemic events but both increase bleeding. [Prasugrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659056), also a thienopyridine, achieves a more rapid onset and higher level of irreversible platelet inhibition than [clopidogrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658818). Compared to [clopidogrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658818), [prasugrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659056) significantly reduced the combined risk of cardiovascular death, MI, stroke, and stent thrombosis, but increased bleeding, in patients with ACS with scheduled PCI. It has been approved for ACS patients following angiography when PCI is planned; it should be administered at a loading dose of 60 mg followed by 10 mg/d. [Prasugrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659056) is contraindicated in patients with prior stroke or transient ischemic attack or at high risk for bleeding, and should be administered once the coronary anatomy is known (i.e., post coronary angiography).

[Ticagrelor](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659122), a potent, *reversible* platelet P2Y<sub>12</sub> inhibitor, reduces the risk of cardiovascular death, total mortality, or MI compared to [clopidogrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=658818) across a broad spectrum of patients with ACS. After a loading dose of 180 mg, 90 mg bid is administered as maintenance. Like [prasugrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659056), [ticagrelor](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659122) increases the risk of bleeding. Unlike [prasugrel](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659056), [ticagrelor](/drug.aspx?gbosId=659122) demonstrated benefit whether patients were managed conservatively or with an early invasive strategy (see below). Some patients may develop dyspnea soon after administration, although the symptoms are often transient and infrequently serious.
