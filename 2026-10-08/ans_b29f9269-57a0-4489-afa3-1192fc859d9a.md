```
How to diagnose necrotizing pneumonia
```

## Answer

 **Necrotizing pneumonia is suspected** when a patient has a compatible clinical syndrome—fever, leukocytosis, tachypnea, hypoxemia, and purulent sputum—with a new or enlarging pulmonary infiltrate on chest radiography. In some cases, the infiltrate progresses to necrosis, and nodular densities or cavities may be seen. (1)

**Diagnostic evaluation:**

- Obtain **sputum Gram stain and culture** and **blood cultures**. A sputum Gram stain showing predominantly polymorphonuclear leukocytes together with a positive culture for *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, in the appropriate clinical setting, supports acute *Pseudomonas* pneumonia. (1)
- Use **chest CT** when cavitation or a complicated process is suspected; CT can provide better definition and earlier evidence of cavitation than chest radiography. (rf_8e, 2)
- Evaluate for other causes and complications, including loculated effusion, pleural infection, lung abscess, malignancy, and other cavitary diseases. (rf_8e, 2)
- Initial testing may include **respiratory viral PCR**, **urinary Legionella antigen**, and, in neutropenic patients, testing for invasive fungal disease such as serum galactomannan and β-D-glucan. (3)
- If noninvasive tests are negative and the patient fails to improve, pursue **early bronchoscopy with bronchoalveolar lavage** or **image-guided percutaneous lung biopsy**. (4, 3)
