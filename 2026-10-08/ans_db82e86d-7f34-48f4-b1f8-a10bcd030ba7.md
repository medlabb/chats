```
How to diagnose necrotizing pneumonia
```

- - -

## Answer

 **Necrotizing pneumonia is suspected** in a patient with a compatible pneumonia syndrome—such as fever, dyspnea, cough, and sputum production—plus an infiltrate or opacity on chest imaging. (1)

**Diagnostic approach:**

1. **Initial chest radiograph:** Look for consolidation and especially **cavitation or necrotizing processes**. Necrotizing radiographic changes suggest a typical bacterial cause, including *S. pneumoniae*, *S. aureus*, or *S. pyogenes*, although imaging alone cannot reliably establish the pathogen. (2)

2. **Chest CT:** If necrotizing pneumonia is seen or suspected on radiography, CT is generally the next step. It better characterizes cavitation and necrosis, identifies additional pulmonary or mediastinal lesions, and can detect associated complications such as lung abscess or empyema. (3) (4)

3. **Microbiologic evaluation:** Depending on severity, obtain blood cultures and respiratory specimens. Evaluation may include sputum Gram stain and culture, acid-fast stains when indicated, and testing for fungal or mycobacterial infection when the imaging pattern or clinical context warrants it. (3) (5)

4. **Bronchoscopy:** When cavitation is present, bronchoscopy with bronchoalveolar lavage is generally performed to obtain samples for culture and cytology, when clinically appropriate. (3)

The combination of a compatible clinical syndrome and imaging evidence establishes an initial clinical diagnosis, but the findings are nonspecific; the diagnosis and possible alternative causes should be reassessed as the clinical course evolves. (1)
