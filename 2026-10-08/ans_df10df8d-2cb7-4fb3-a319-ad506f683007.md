## Answer

 ## Treatment of necrotizing pneumonia

- **Urgent hospital/ICU assessment** is appropriate, particularly with respiratory failure, hemodynamic instability, or septic shock. Obtain sputum/BAL Gram stain and cultures when feasible to guide therapy. (1, 2)
- Start **prompt empiric IV antibiotics**. Because necrotizing pneumonia may involve **MRSA**, empiric **vancomycin or linezolid** should be included when MRSA is suspected—especially in severe or influenza-associated necrotizing pneumonia. (2, 1, 3)
- If **Pseudomonas or resistant gram-negative infection** is suspected, an antipseudomonal β-lactam such as **piperacillin–tazobactam, cefepime, ceftazidime, imipenem, or meropenem** may be combined with **ciprofloxacin or levofloxacin**, pending microbiology. (1)
- If aspiration, lung abscess, empyema, or dental infection is likely, add **anaerobic coverage**. Options include **ampicillin–sulbactam**, **piperacillin–tazobactam**, or a **carbapenem**; metronidazole should **not** be used alone. (4, 5)
- Once cultures and susceptibilities are available, **de-escalate to the narrowest active antibiotic**. For confirmed MRSA pneumonia, use **vancomycin or linezolid**; for MSSA, options include **cefazolin, nafcillin, or oxacillin**, with cefazolin generally preferred because of fewer adverse effects. (3, 6)
- If there is an associated abscess and the patient fails to improve, **drainage is indicated**—preferably transthoracic percutaneous drainage when accessible. Surgery is reserved for abscesses that cannot be drained or fail less-invasive treatment. (7)

The selected references do not provide a definitive treatment duration or a single regimen applicable to every case; these depend on the clinical setting, cultures, complications, and response to therapy. The ICU approach is summarized in **algorithm 4**. (1, 7)
