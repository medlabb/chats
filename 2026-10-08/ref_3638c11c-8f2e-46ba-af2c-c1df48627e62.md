#### Dosage and Administration

The immediate-release gabapentin USPI recommends that adjunctive therapy in adolescents and adults with epilepsy should start at 300 mg three times a day and be increased up to 1,800 mg/day in three divided doses. However, in adults with epilepsy, immediate-release gabapentin is generally well tolerated at doses as high as 3,600 and 2,400 mg/day in short- and long-term studies, respectively. Adjunctive immediate-release gabapentin in pediatric seizures is recommended to start at 10 to 15 mg/kg per day in three divided doses and to increase over three days to 25 to 40 mg/kg per day in three divided doses. A long-term epilepsy study suggested that immediate-release gabapentin doses up to 50 mg/kg per day may be well tolerated. Plasma gabapentin concentrations in patients with epilepsy commonly range between approximately 12 and 20 μg/mL (70 and 120 μM/L) but did not appear to be systematically related to antiseizure effects and thus were not routinely monitored.

Immediate-release gabapentin for adults with postherpetic neuralgia or neuropathic pain is recommended to start with 300 mg on day one, increase to 600 mg on day 2, 900 mg on day 3, and subsequently increase as needed to relieve pain up to 1,800 mg/day in three divided doses. Extended-release gabapentin for postherpetic neuralgia is recommended to start at 300 mg daily, increased to 600 mg on day 2, and then further increased to 300 mg every 4 days, with a maximum dose of 1,800 mg. Gabapentin enacarbil for postherpetic neuralgia is recommended to start at 600 mg daily with food for 3 days, then increase to 600 mg twice daily, and for restless legs syndrome is suggested to start and maintain at 600 mg per evening with food.

Off-label gabapentin is typically used adjunctively at similar doses. In some (nonepilepsy) applications, clinicians may administer more of the dosage at bedtime to enhance sleep and decrease daytime somnolence. However, individual doses of immediate-release gabapentin above 1,200 mg are divided due to saturable absorption. In psychiatric outpatients, immediate-release gabapentin may be started at 300 mg at bedtime and increased by 300 mg every 4 days as necessary. Starting on a dose of 1,200 mg at bedtime is typically well tolerated. Occasionally, older adult patients or patients on complex combination therapies may require doses as low as 100 to 200 mg due to sedation. The final total daily doses tend to be between 1,200 and 2,400 mg/day but may range as high as 4,800 mg/day.
