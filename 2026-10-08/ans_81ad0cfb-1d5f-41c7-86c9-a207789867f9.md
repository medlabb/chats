## Answer

 ## Secondary amenorrhea

**Secondary amenorrhea** is the absence of menstruation for **3–6 months**, or for three usual menstrual cycles in a woman with oligomenorrhea. (1)

### Causes
Causes are categorized as:

- **Pregnancy**—the most common cause and must be excluded first with a **β-hCG** test. (2)
- **Hypothalamic-pituitary dysfunction**, associated with factors such as weight loss, excessive exercise, obesity, medications, chronic illness, anxiety, anorexia nervosa, and pituitary tumors. (3)
- **Ovarian dysfunction**, including primary ovarian insufficiency, in which FSH and LH are increased and estradiol is decreased. (3)
- **Genital outflow tract abnormalities**, including uterine obstruction or endometrial scarring. **Asherman syndrome**—intrauterine scarring—is the most frequent anatomic cause of secondary amenorrhea and may follow dilation and curettage, particularly when infection is present. (3; 4)

### Evaluation
Evaluation includes:

- Detailed history of the last menstrual period, exercise, diet, weight changes, eating disorders, medications, illicit drug use, psychiatric history, hirsutism, acne, and galactorrhea. (5)
- Physical examination, including BMI, Tanner staging, genital anatomy, and signs of androgen excess. (5)
- Laboratory testing with **pregnancy testing, TSH, prolactin, estradiol, and FSH**. (5)
- A **progesterone challenge test**, which may help assess estrogen effect, endometrial responsiveness, and outflow tract patency. Withdrawal bleeding suggests estrogen effect with likely anovulation or oligo-ovulation; absence of bleeding may suggest hypoestrogenism, Asherman syndrome, or outflow obstruction. (5)

Relevant imaging may include ultrasonography, MRI, or CT, depending on the clinical history. Hysteroscopy is used to definitively diagnose and treat Asherman syndrome. (5; 6)
