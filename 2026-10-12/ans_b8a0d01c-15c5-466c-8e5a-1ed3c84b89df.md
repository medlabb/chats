```
Treatment for aortic trombosis in newborns
```

- - -

## Answer

 **Aortic thrombosis in a newborn is a medical emergency requiring urgent consultation with neonatology, pediatric hematology, and vascular/cardiothoracic surgery.** Treatment depends on the degree of obstruction and whether blood flow to vital organs or limbs is impaired.

- **Remove any associated arterial catheter**, if possible. (1)
- If the thrombosis causes **clinically significant impairment of blood flow to a vital organ or extremity**, anticoagulation is recommended. (1)
- **Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH)** is generally preferred for neonatal anticoagulation; dosing is adjusted using anti-factor Xa levels, with a therapeutic target of **0.5–1 units/mL**. (2)
- **Unfractionated heparin (UFH)** may be preferred when rapid adjustment or reversal is needed, in kidney failure, with bleeding risk, or when thrombolysis may be used. (3)
- **Thrombolysis with recombinant tissue-type plasminogen activator (tPA)** may be warranted when the thrombosis is life-threatening or threatens organ/limb viability. This should be decided by a multidisciplinary team, with surgical consultation. (1) Thrombolysis is generally reserved for major-vessel occlusion causing critical compromise because of the substantial bleeding risk. (4)
- Before thrombolysis, evaluation includes coagulation studies, blood count and platelets, kidney and liver function, cranial ultrasound, and D-dimer; thrombocytopenia, low fibrinogen, and severe coagulopathy should be corrected. Fresh frozen plasma is given before thrombolytic therapy according to the selected reference. (2) (4)
- If tPA is used, the reference describes continuous IV infusion without a loading dose, commonly starting at **0.1–0.2 mg/kg/hour for six hours**, with concurrent UFH at **10 units/kg/hour**; dosing and monitoring must be directed by specialists. (4)
- Anticoagulation is commonly continued for **six weeks to three months**, with serial ultrasound monitoring and treatment continued until thrombus resolution or three months, whichever is shorter. (2) (5)

The selected references do not provide a separate, aorta-specific treatment algorithm or outcome guidance beyond these recommendations.
