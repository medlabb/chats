## Answer

 ## **EXIT procedure**

### **Definition**
Ex utero intrapartum therapy (**EXIT**) is a procedure in which the fetus is **partially delivered and intubated before the umbilical cord is clamped**. Uteroplacental blood flow and gas exchange are maintained using inhalational agents for uterine relaxation and amnioinfusion to preserve uterine volume and prevent placental separation or cord compression. (1)

This provides time for **airway management, ECMO cannulation, and chest decompression** before complete delivery. It may also permit delayed removal of a congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM); rarely, the lung mass is excised before delivery is completed. (1)

### **Indications**
EXIT has been used for **exceptionally large lung lesions**, particularly a large **CPAM connected to the bronchial airway**. After birth, initial respiration may fill the lesion with air without decompression, producing an expanding intrathoracic mass that can compress the heart and behave like a tension pneumothorax. (1)

### **History and outcomes**
The selected reference states that EXIT **has been used** to improve outcomes in exceptionally large lung lesions, but it does not provide a historical account of the procedure’s development or origin. Reported overall fetal/newborn survival is **90%**; however, maternal bleeding risk is increased, and **13% of mothers require transfusion**. (1)
