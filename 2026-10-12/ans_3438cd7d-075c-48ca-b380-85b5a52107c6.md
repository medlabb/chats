## Answer

 - **Hypernatremia is defined as serum Na⁺ ≥150 mEq/L in neonates** (and >150 mEq/L in children). (1; 2)
- If there is **moderate/severe hypovolemia or poor perfusion**, restore intravascular volume first with an **isotonic fluid**; correction of hypernatremia follows. (3)
- Once circulation is restored, use **hypotonic/free-water-containing fluid** to correct the water deficit. **0.9% saline may be used initially** in hypernatremic hypovolemia because it is hypotonic relative to the infant’s hypernatremic plasma; **0.45% saline** is an example of a fluid that provides additional free water. (4)
- **Dextrose in water** may be used when there is isolated water loss, such as in an AVP disorder, but it will not adequately correct hypovolemia when salt and water have both been lost. (5)
- Correct slowly: neonatal correction faster than approximately **0.5 mEq/L/hour** should generally be avoided because of the risk of cerebral edema and seizures. Monitor serial serum sodium and adjust the fluid prescription. (1; 5)

For infants, including low-birth-weight preterm infants, total body water is higher—approximately **80% of body mass**—which should be considered when estimating the water deficit. (2)

The selected references do **not provide separate fluid-choice thresholds or protocols for each sodium range in preterm versus term infants**.
