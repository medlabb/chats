## Answer

 ## **Transverse myelitis**

**Acute transverse myelitis** is a syndrome caused by infectious or noninfectious inflammatory disorders that disrupt spinal-cord function. Causes include bacterial, viral, fungal, or parasitic infections; multiple sclerosis; neuromyelitis optica; acute disseminated encephalomyelitis; systemic autoimmune disease; and idiopathic inflammation. (1)

### Clinical features
- Bilateral **sensory, motor, and autonomic deficits** involving the limbs and trunk  
- A distinct **sensory level** corresponding to the spinal-cord lesion  
- Progression over **hours to days**  
- Possible asymmetric or unilateral involvement  
- CSF inflammation, with **pleocytosis and/or increased IgG index**; low CSF glucose may suggest infection  
- MRI evidence of an intrinsic cord lesion, usually enhancing with gadolinium (1, 2)

### Important differential diagnoses
Neoplastic, compressive, and vascular disorders can mimic inflammatory myelitis. Onset over a few hours suggests vascular disease, progression over several days supports inflammation, and a course over several weeks raises consideration of dural arteriovenous fistula or other chronic myelopathies. MRI helps identify compression from tumor, hemorrhage, or epidural abscess, as well as vascular-distribution lesions and spondylotic myelopathy. (2)

### Treatment
Acute treatment typically uses intravenous **methylprednisolone**, commonly 1 g daily for 3–5 days. In glucocorticoid-unresponsive cases, plasma exchange, intravenous immunoglobulin, or cyclophosphamide may be considered, although their benefit is not firmly established. (2)

Patients often improve over several months, but residual neurological deficits may remain, and recurrence depends on the underlying cause. (2)

### Relationship to neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder
Myelitis extending over at least three spinal segments, particularly when associated with optic neuritis, may occur in **neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder**. Acute attacks are treated with intravenous methylprednisolone for 5 days followed by an oral prednisone taper; plasmapheresis is used when response is poor. (3)
