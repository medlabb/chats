## Answer

 **Neonatal vomiting** can be associated with several conditions described in the references:

- **Bilious emesis** requires urgent imaging to exclude malrotation with midgut volvulus. Projectile emesis is associated with hypertrophic pyloric stenosis; ultrasound has high sensitivity for its diagnosis. (1)
- **Intestinal obstruction** may cause vomiting, abdominal distention, and poor feeding. Failure to pass meconium is often seen with low obstruction. Abdominal radiographs are the initial imaging examination; an upper GI series is used for suspected high obstruction, and a water-soluble contrast enema for suspected low obstruction. Figures 38.21 and 38.22 show examples of high and low obstruction on radiographs. (2)
- **Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC)** may begin with feeding intolerance and emesis, alongside changes in vital signs or increased apnea. Later signs can include abdominal distention, tenderness, discoloration, and absent bowel sounds. (3)
- Vomiting may also occur with **neonatal enterovirus infection**, typically alongside fever, irritability, or poor feeding, or with **moderate to severe hypercalcemia**, often alongside anorexia or constipation. (4; 5)
