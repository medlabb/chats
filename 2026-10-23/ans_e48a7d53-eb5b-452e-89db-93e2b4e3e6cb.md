## Answer

 **Epidural hemorrhage (epidural hematoma)** is bleeding into the space between the dura and the skull, usually caused by traumatic head injury and often arterial in origin. (1) (2)

### Intracranial epidural hematoma
Acute symptomatic intracranial epidural hematoma is a **neurosurgical emergency** because it can cause brain herniation and death. Treatment generally requires urgent decompression, usually by craniotomy and evacuation; burr-hole evacuation may be used in some cases. (2)

**Posterior fossa epidural hematomas** may deteriorate suddenly with few warning signs. They are often associated with occipital fractures and may present with loss of consciousness, headache, vomiting, and an occipital scalp hematoma. (3) The referenced material includes **images 2 and 3**. (3)

### Spinal epidural hematoma
Spinal epidural hematoma may occur after spinal dural puncture or epidural anesthesia, particularly with thrombocytopenia, coagulopathy, or anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy; rarely, it occurs spontaneously or after minor trauma. (4) (5)

Typical symptoms include **local or radicular pain**, followed by sensory, motor, and bladder or bowel dysfunction. MRI is a sensitive imaging modality. (4) Significant or progressive neurologic deficits require prompt surgical decompression, usually laminectomy and evacuation; timely treatment is essential to reduce the risk of permanent neurologic loss.
