## Answer

 **Septic shock** is a severe form of sepsis and a type of **distributive (vasodilatory) shock**. It is defined clinically as:

- Sepsis with circulatory, cellular, and metabolic abnormalities  
- **Persistent need for vasopressors** to maintain a mean arterial pressure (MAP) of at least **65 mmHg despite adequate fluid resuscitation**
- **Lactate >2 mmol/L** (>18 mg/dL)  

Patients meeting these criteria have an estimated mortality of **at least 40%**, compared with at least 10% for sepsis without septic shock. (1)

Septic shock causes inadequate tissue perfusion and lactic acidosis. Contributing mechanisms include reduced systemic vascular resistance, reduced circulating volume from capillary leak and third spacing, and possible myocardial dysfunction. (2)

Management requires **urgent antibiotics, intravenous fluids, and supportive care**, with septic shock patients managed in an **intensive care unit**. (3)
