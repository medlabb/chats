## Answer

 A **seizure** is classified by how it begins and spreads: it may be **focal** (starting in one brain region), **generalized** (involving both hemispheres from the outset), or of **unknown onset**. Focal seizures may occur with preserved or impaired awareness and can spread into a bilateral tonic-clonic seizure. (1)

Seizures can cause different symptoms depending on the brain areas involved. For example, focal seizures may cause unusual sensations, fear or déjà vu, speech difficulty, visual symptoms, or involuntary movements. Generalized seizures include staring spells and brief muscle jerks, as well as tonic-clonic convulsions. (1) (2) (3)

After some seizures, a person may have confusion, fatigue, headache, or temporary weakness; the recovery period is called the **postictal phase**. (4) (2) Seizure-like episodes can also have other causes, including fainting, migraine, or psychogenic nonepileptic seizures. (5)

Witness descriptions of what happened before, during, and after an episode are important in evaluation, and EEG is described as the most helpful diagnostic test. (6)
