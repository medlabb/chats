## Answer

 Treatment depends on the malaria species, drug susceptibility, and whether the illness is **severe**. Severe malaria is a medical emergency; patients with severe malaria or who cannot take oral medication need immediate parenteral treatment. (1, 2)

- **Uncomplicated *P. falciparum* or *P. knowlesi*:** An artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT) is recommended. Options listed include artemether–lumefantrine, artesunate–mefloquine, dihydroartemisinin–piperaquine, artesunate–sulfadoxine–pyrimethamine, artesunate–amodiaquine, and artesunate–pyronaridine. The choice depends on local parasite sensitivity. (rf_c2, 3)
- **Other species:** For chloroquine-sensitive *P. vivax*, *P. malariae*, or *P. ovale*, oral chloroquine or an ACT may be used. For *P. vivax* or *P. ovale*, preventing relapse generally also requires primaquine after testing for G6PD deficiency; the references specify that primaquine should not be used in severe G6PD deficiency. (1, 3, 4)
- **Severe malaria:** IV artesunate is the preferred treatment. If it is unavailable, the table lists IM artemether or IV quinine as alternatives. For severe malaria from an area with confirmed artemisinin resistance, the references recommend parenteral artesunate and quinine together. (1, rf_c2)

The treatment table also lists specific regimens and weight-based doses; treatment should be selected and prescribed by a clinician based on the patient and local resistance patterns. If the malaria species is uncertain, the references advise treating for falciparum malaria. (1, 4)
