## Answer

 Treatment depends on the **severity and cause** of hypokalemia. Potassium replacement is the main treatment, and extra potassium in the diet alone usually isn’t enough. (1, 2)

- **Mild to moderate cases:** Oral potassium is typically used. The type of potassium salt and dose depend on the cause and acid–base status; a commonly described starting dose is 10–20 mEq two to four times daily. (3)
- **Severe or symptomatic cases**—such as potassium below about 2.5–3.0 mEq/L, arrhythmias, marked muscle weakness, or rhabdomyolysis—may need more rapid replacement. Potassium can be given orally or intravenously; IV replacement may be used for severe symptoms or when oral medication isn’t possible. (4)
- **Monitoring and treating the cause are important:** Serum potassium should be checked frequently during replacement. Clinicians may also assess and treat low magnesium, address ongoing losses, or adjust a causative medication. In potassium-wasting conditions, potassium-sparing medicines may be considered. (1, 4, 3, 2)

Potassium replacement can be risky in some forms of hypokalemia caused by potassium shifting into cells, because levels may rebound too high as that process resolves. Treatment should therefore be guided by a clinician, with close monitoring. (5, 1)
