## Answer

 **Dobutamine** is an inotropic medication that increases cardiac contractility. In cardiogenic shock, it may be used for low cardiac output, particularly when blood pressure is not low, or added to a vasopressor when heart function or tissue perfusion remains inadequate. The cited starting infusion is **0.1–0.5 mcg/kg/min**, titrated to effect; the usual range is **2–20 mcg/kg/min**. Tachyphylaxis may occur after 48 hours. (1)

In acute heart failure, dobutamine can increase cardiac output and modestly lower pulmonary capillary wedge pressure, but inotropes are generally avoided if possible because they may increase heart rate and the heart’s oxygen demand. (2)

Dobutamine is also used as a **pharmacologic stress agent** in stress echocardiography when exercise is not suitable; the cited stress-test dose is **20–40 mcg/kg/min**. (3)
