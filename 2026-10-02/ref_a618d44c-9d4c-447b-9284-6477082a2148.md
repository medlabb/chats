# SUMMARY AND RECOMMENDATIONS

- **Overview**– Superficial fungal infections are most commonly caused by dermatophytes in theTrichophyton,Epidermophyton, andMicrosporumgenera. These organisms metabolize keratin and cause a range of pathologic clinical presentations, including tinea pedis  (picture 1A-C), tinea corporis  (picture 2A-D), tinea cruris  (picture 3A-E), tinea capitis, and onychomycosis. (See'Microbiology'above and'Tinea pedis'above and'Tinea corporis'above and'Tinea cruris'above and'Other clinical variants'above.)

- **Clinical features**

**Tinea pedis**– Tinea pedis may manifest in a variety of ways, including interdigital tinea pedis  (picture 1A), hyperkeratotic (moccasin-type) tinea****pedis,****and vesiculobullous (inflammatory) tinea pedis  (picture 1C-D). (See'Tinea pedis'above.)

**Tinea corporis**– Tinea corporis often begins as a circular or oval, erythematous or hyperpigmented, scaling patch or plaque. The skin lesion spreads centrifugally and develops central clearing. The result is an annular (ring-shaped) plaque with a raised border. Pruritus is common  (picture 2A-B, 2D-E).

Tinea corporis may present as a single lesion or multiple lesions. Plaques may coalesce  (picture 25A-B). Occasionally, pustules appear  (picture 26). (See'Tinea corporis'above.)

**Tinea cruris**– Tinea cruris often begins with an erythematous or hyperpigmented patch on the proximal medial thigh. The infection spreads centrifugally, with partial central clearing and a slightly elevated, erythematous or hyperpigmented, sharply demarcated border  (picture 3A-D).

Infection may spread to the perineum and perianal areas, into the gluteal cleft, or onto the buttocks. Infection typically spares the scrotum  (picture 3D). (See'Tinea cruris'above.)

- **Diagnosis**– A diagnosis of a cutaneous dermatophyte infection may be strongly suspected based on the clinical findings. A potassium hydroxide (KOH) preparation confirms the diagnosis  (algorithm 1). Long, branching, septate hyphae support the diagnosis  (picture 7). (See"Office-based dermatologic diagnostic procedures", section on 'Potassium hydroxide preparation'.)

- For tinea corporis and tinea cruris, the highest yield is from skin scrapings taken from the active border of a plaque or patch. Fungal culture is an alternative, albeit slower, method for diagnosis. (See'Diagnosis'above.)

- **Differential diagnosis**

**Tinea pedis**– The differential diagnosis of tinea pedis is broad and varies according to the clinical subtype. It may include other cutaneous infections  (picture 14), various types of eczematous dermatitis  (picture 17), psoriasis  (picture 18), scabies  (picture 24), and other disorders. A KOH preparation is useful for distinguishing tinea pedis from other disorders  (picture 7). (See'Differential diagnosis'above.)

**Tinea corporis**– Tinea corporis may be confused with other annular skin eruptions. Examples include subacute cutaneous lupus erythematosus  (picture 27), granuloma annulare  (picture 28B), and erythema annulare centrifugum  (picture 29A-B). (See'Differential diagnosis'above and"Approach to the patient with annular skin lesions".)

Extensive tinea corporis should prompt consideration of an underlying immune disorder, such as HIV or diabetes.

**Tinea cruris**– Common skin conditions in the differential diagnosis include candidal intertrigo, seborrheic dermatitis, inverse psoriasis, and erythrasma. (See'Differential diagnosis'above.)

The clinical features of tinea cruris (eg, partial central clearing and a slightly elevated border) and the KOH preparation findings (long, septate hyphae  (picture 7)) help to distinguish tinea cruris from these disorders. ## Invasive skin infections

- **Invasive fungal dermatitis**– Invasive fungal dermatitis may be suspected based upon the appearance of skin lesions (macular, papular, vesicular, or pustular lesions located in dependent or intertriginous areas of the skin). The definitive diagnosis is confirmed by a skin biopsy with isolation or histologic identification of the organism.

- **Congenital cutaneous candidiasis****(CCC)**– In patients with CCC, a presumptive diagnosis can be made by identifying the organism by Gram stain of the vesicular contents or by potassium hydroxide preparations of skin scrapings  (picture 6). Confirmation of the diagnosis is made with isolation of the organism from a culture of the discrete lesions or swabs of the affected skin areas.

## Invasive fungal dermatitis
Invasive fungal dermatitis is a condition that is unique to ELBW infants during the first two weeks after birth. Most affected patients have evidence of disseminated fungal disease. Thus, we suggest initiating systemic therapy with amphotericin B while awaiting culture results.
