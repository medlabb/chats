## Answer

 **5-aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA, usually mesalamine)** is used to treat active mild-to-moderate ulcerative colitis and to help maintain remission. The approach depends on how much of the colon is affected:

- **Proctitis or proctosigmoiditis:** rectal mesalamine is preferred—1 g suppository at bedtime for proctitis or a 4 g enema at bedtime for proctosigmoiditis, typically for 4–8 weeks. (1)
- **Colitis extending above the sigmoid:** oral mesalamine, 2–4.8 g once daily, together with a 1 g rectal suppository at bedtime is described for inducing remission. Many patients improve within 4–8 weeks. (1)
- If mild-to-moderate colitis does not improve after 4–8 weeks of 5-ASA, the reference describes adding an oral corticosteroid. Moderate-to-severe colitis is generally treated with corticosteroids rather than relying on 5-ASA alone. (2)

The references do not provide a specific 5-ASA plan for every type or severity of flare, so treatment should be guided by the extent and severity of your colitis. (1)
