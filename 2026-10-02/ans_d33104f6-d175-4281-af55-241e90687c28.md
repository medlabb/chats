## Answer

 **Pleural effusion** is an abnormal accumulation of fluid in the pleural space. It occurs when fluid production increases or drainage is impaired. Contributing mechanisms include increased systemic or pulmonary venous pressure, increased pleural-vessel permeability, reduced pleural pressure, low oncotic pressure, and blocked lymphatic drainage. (1)

**Detection and evaluation**
- Chest radiography commonly detects an effusion; more than **250 mL** is generally needed for it to be visible. Costophrenic-angle blunting is a typical finding. A decubitus radiograph can assess whether fluid is free-flowing or loculated; chest CT can better define loculations and distinguish pleural from lung abnormalities. (2)
- **Thoracentesis** obtains pleural fluid; ultrasound or CT guidance can help direct catheter placement and minimize complications. Fluid analysis helps establish the cause and distinguish a transudate from an exudate. (2, 3)

**Classification and causes**
- **Transudates** result from altered hydrostatic or oncotic forces and usually have low protein. Congestive heart failure is a common cause; other listed causes include cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, and hypoalbuminemia. (4)
- **Exudates** are associated with altered vascular permeability or impaired fluid resorption, including inflammatory, infectious, and neoplastic conditions. Causes listed include infection, malignancy, pulmonary embolism, and autoimmune disease. (5, 4)
- An effusion is classified as an exudate if **any** of these criteria is met: pleural-fluid-to-serum protein ratio **>0.5**, pleural-fluid-to-serum LDH ratio **>0.6**, or pleural-fluid LDH **> two-thirds** the upper limit of normal. These criteria appear in **Table 19.2**. (5)

Management depends on the cause and fluid findings. For example, uncomplicated parapneumonic effusions are treated with antibiotics for the underlying pneumonia, whereas complicated effusions require drainage; pus or bacteria on Gram stain or culture indicates empyema and requires immediate drainage. (5)
