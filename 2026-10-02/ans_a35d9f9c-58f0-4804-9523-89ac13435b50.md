```
Differential diagnosis for a 60 year female with elevated iron and ferritin and transferrin saturation
```

## Answer

 **Supported differential diagnoses include:**

- **Hereditary hemochromatosis:** Most established cases involve *HFE* C282Y homozygosity. Compound C282Y/H63D and other, rarer iron-regulation gene variants are also described (1). Elevated plasma iron with transferrin saturation above 45% and elevated ferritin are characteristic findings; the cited ferritin threshold is above 300 mcg/L for postmenopausal women (2).
- **Secondary iron overload:** Liver inflammation or injury may contribute; elevated ALT/AST makes C282Y homozygosity less likely and may reflect inflammation with secondary iron overload (2). Iron overload related to thalassemia or blood transfusions is also mentioned (3).
- **Other genetic explanations for elevated ferritin or iron:** *FTL*-related hyperferritinemia-cataract syndrome can cause high ferritin, while the *HFE* S65C variant may raise serum iron and ferritin without clinical significance (1). The reference does not establish that these explain elevated transferrin saturation.
- **Contributing factors:** Alcohol use and metabolic syndrome can influence iron overload risk (1).

The selected references do not provide a complete differential for elevated iron, ferritin, and transferrin saturation; other causes cannot be found in them.
