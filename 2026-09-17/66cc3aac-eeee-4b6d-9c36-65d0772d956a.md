## Answer

 The statement is **not completely correct**. **Gilbert syndrome usually becomes apparent after puberty**, but reduced UGT1A1 activity associated with Gilbert syndrome can contribute to neonatal unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia. It is listed as a genetic risk factor for severe neonatal hyperbilirubinemia, and co-inheritance with G6PD deficiency can cause more severe neonatal jaundice. (1) (2) (3)

Therefore, neonatal jaundice should not automatically be attributed to Gilbert syndrome; other causes must be evaluated, especially if it appears during the first 24–36 hours, is severe, or persists. (4)
