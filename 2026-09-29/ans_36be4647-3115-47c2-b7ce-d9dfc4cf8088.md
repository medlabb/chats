## Answer

 **Frontal lobe lesions** may produce different findings depending on the affected region and cause:

- **Bifrontal lesions:** akinetic mutism, reduced spontaneity, impaired judgment, problems with recent or working memory, blunted or labile emotional responses, and incontinence. These may resemble delirium; neuroimaging may be needed for differentiation. (1)
- **Motor involvement:** weakness, often affecting the opposite side; lesions near the motor cortex may cause weakness related to surrounding edema. (2)
- **Dominant frontal lobe involvement:** tumors near Broca’s area can cause **nonfluent/expressive aphasia**. Broca’s aphasia is characterized by sparse, effortful speech, agrammatism, impaired repetition, relatively preserved comprehension, and may be associated with contralateral hemiparesis and oral apraxia. (2) (3)
- **Lesions associated with subdural hematoma:** frontal involvement may cause hemiparesis, while interhemispheric involvement may cause paraparesis without facial weakness. (4)
- **Frontal lobe epilepsy:** seizures are often brief, may occur during sleep, and can involve hypermotor behaviors, bicycling or pelvic-thrusting automatisms, tonic posturing, contralateral hemiclonic movements, “fencing posture,” vocalizations, or staring/trance-like episodes. Awareness may be relatively preserved, and the postictal state may be brief or absent. (rf_37) (5)
