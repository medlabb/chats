## Answer

 **Nitroglycerin** is a rapid-acting predominantly **venodilator** that reduces ventricular preload and pulmonary congestion in acute cardiogenic pulmonary edema. (1)

- **Sublingual:** 0.4 mg, repeated up to 3 doses every 5 minutes; described as first-line therapy for acute cardiogenic pulmonary edema. (1)  
- **IV nitroglycerin:** If edema persists **without hypotension**, begin at **5–10 μg/min** and titrate with close blood-pressure monitoring. (1)  
- It is commonly used as an adjunct to diuretics in acute decompensated heart failure and pulmonary edema. (2)  
- Use particular caution because vasodilators can cause **hypotension, coronary hypoperfusion, and shock**. Patients with a hypertensive response generally tolerate and benefit from them; in normotensive patients, use low doses of a single agent sequentially with close monitoring. (3)  
- The selected references list **hypotension, recent PDE-5 inhibitor use, right-ventricular infarction, and severe aortic stenosis** as situations to avoid nitrates in the acute coronary syndrome context. (4)
