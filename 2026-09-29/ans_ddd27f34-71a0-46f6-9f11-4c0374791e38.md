 ## Diseases of bone

### **Metabolic bone disease**
Metabolic bone disease includes conditions causing diffuse reductions or increases in bone density that affect bone integrity. Reduced bone density occurs in **osteopenia/osteoporosis** and **osteomalacia**; increased density occurs in **osteopetrosis** and **fluorosis**. **Fibrous dysplasia** and **Paget disease of bone** involve disorganized bone and abnormal bone quality. These disorders may disturb calcium or phosphate homeostasis and cause bone pain and fractures. (1)

### **Osteoporosis and osteopenia**
- Osteoporosis involves reduced bone mass and deterioration of bone microstructure, resulting in reduced bone strength.
- It is often **asymptomatic until a fracture occurs**.
- Fracture risk may be estimated using the **FRAX tool**.
- Diagnosis is made with **DXA bone-density scanning**.
- Serum calcium, phosphorus, PTH, and 25-hydroxyvitamin D may be normal.
- Osteoporosis should ideally be prevented because it is only partially reversible. (2) (3)

### **Osteomalacia, rickets, and vitamin D deficiency**
- Osteomalacia and rickets involve **low bone density caused by defective mineralization**. (3)
- **Vitamin D deficiency is the most common cause of osteomalacia**.
- Vitamin D is produced mainly in the skin after ultraviolet-B exposure and is also obtained from dietary sources. It is converted to active vitamin D, which increases intestinal calcium absorption and renal calcium reabsorption. (4)
- Risk factors for vitamin D deficiency include limited sunlight exposure, older age, obesity, dark skin, inadequate dietary intake, malabsorption, severe nephrotic syndrome, and certain anticonvulsants. (4)
- Vitamin D–dependent rickets types 1 and 2 are rare inherited disorders that can cause childhood rickets and adult osteomalacia. (4)
- **Hypophosphatasia** is a hereditary form of rickets or osteomalacia associated with low alkaline phosphatase; adults may develop premature tooth loss, stress fractures, thigh pain, pseudofractures, or chondrocalcinosis. (5)

Relevant references include **Table 30–2** for causes of osteoporosis and **Table 30–3** for vitamin D sources and metabolism. (4)

### **Paget disease of bone**
Paget disease is characterized by localized areas of high bone turnover and disorganized osteoid formation. Bones may become vascular, weak, enlarged, and deformed. Radiographs may show **osteolytic or sclerotic lesions and expanded bones**; alkaline phosphatase and biochemical markers of bone turnover may be elevated. (6)

About 75% of cases are mild and asymptomatic. The disease may be discovered incidentally on imaging or because of an elevated serum alkaline phosphatase level. Approximately 20% of cases are familial, and mutations in **SQSTM1** occur in some familial and sporadic cases. (6)

The differential diagnosis includes osteogenic sarcoma, plasma-cell myeloma, fibrous dysplasia, osteitis fibrosa cystica, metastatic carcinoma, and other sclerosing bone dysplasias. (7)

### **Bone and joint tumors**
Possible features of bone tumors or tumor-like lesions include:
- Persistent skeletal pain, swelling, or tenderness
- Pathologic fractures
- Bony enlargement or deformity
- Radiodense or radiolucent areas on x-ray
- Histologic evidence of a bone neoplasm on biopsy (8)

Primary bone tumors in adults are relatively uncommon compared with secondary or metastatic bone neoplasms. (8)

### **Other bone diseases**
The selected references also mention **osteopetrosis, fluorosis, fibrous dysplasia, hypophosphatasia, axial osteomalacia, and fibrogenesis imperfecta ossium**. Fibrogenesis imperfecta ossium may cause progressive bone pain and pathologic fractures, with dense “fishnet” bones on radiographs and elevated alkaline phosphatase. (1) (5)
