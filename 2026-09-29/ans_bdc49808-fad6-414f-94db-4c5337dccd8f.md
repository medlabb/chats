```
What is cortical neurolocalization
```

## Answer

 **Cortical neurolocalization** is the process of identifying a lesion in the **cerebral cortex** based on characteristic neurological findings. It is part of anatomic diagnosis—determining where in the nervous system a lesion is located. (1)

Features suggesting a cortical lesion include:

- **Contralateral focal weakness or sensory loss**, often involving the face, arm, and/or leg; the distribution may indicate the cortical region involved. (2)
- **Cortical sensory deficits**, such as impaired graphesthesia, stereognosis, two-point discrimination, or sensory neglect, despite intact primary sensation. (3)
- **Language disorders**, including Broca or Wernicke aphasia, when the dominant hemisphere is involved. (4)
- **Visual-field defects**, cognitive abnormalities, neglect, anosognosia, or apraxia. (2) (5)
- **Higher-order sensory deficits** from parietal involvement, such as astereognosis, agraphesthesia, extinction, and constructional apraxia. (5)

The symptoms are generally **contralateral** to the affected cerebral hemisphere, although exceptions can occur. (6)
