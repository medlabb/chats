 ## Crohn’s disease

**Crohn’s disease (CD)** is a chronic inflammatory disease that can affect any part of the gastrointestinal tract, from the mouth to the anus. It is often **segmental**, with “skip areas,” and commonly involves the terminal ileum. Unlike ulcerative colitis, the rectum is often spared. (1)

### Clinical patterns
Disease behavior is classified as:

- **B1:** Nonstricturing, nonpenetrating  
- **B2:** Stricturing  
- **B3:** Penetrating  
- **Perianal modifier:** Added when perianal disease is present  

Disease location includes ileal, colonic, ileocolonic, or isolated upper gastrointestinal disease. These classifications are summarized in **Table 337-5**. (2)

Common manifestations include:

- Recurrent right-lower-quadrant pain and diarrhea, particularly with ileocolitis  
- Weight loss, low-grade fever, and sometimes a palpable inflammatory mass  
- Strictures causing postprandial pain or bowel obstruction  
- Fistulas, abscesses, and microperforation  
- Malabsorption, steatorrhea, anemia, hypoalbuminemia, and vitamin or mineral deficiencies in extensive small-bowel disease  
- Colitis with diarrhea, crampy abdominal pain, low-grade fever, and occasional hematochezia  
- Perianal disease, including fissures, fistulas, abscesses, anal stenosis, and skin tags (2) (3) (4)

### Pathologic features
CD is a **transmural** inflammatory process. Characteristic findings include aphthous and deep linear ulcers, strictures, fistulas, bowel-wall thickening, “creeping fat,” skip lesions, and a **cobblestone** mucosal appearance. (1)

Noncaseating granulomas may occur in all layers of the bowel wall, although they are less commonly found in mucosal biopsies than in surgical specimens. (5)

**Figure 337-5** illustrates colonic wall thickening, stenosis, serpiginous ulcers, and cobblestoning; **Figure 337-6** demonstrates granulomas in Crohn’s colitis. (1) (5)

### Evaluation
Laboratory abnormalities may include elevated ESR and CRP, anemia, leukocytosis, and hypoalbuminemia. Fecal calprotectin and lactoferrin can help assess intestinal inflammation and disease activity. Endoscopic findings include rectal sparing, aphthous ulcerations, fistulas, and skip lesions. (4)

CT enterography and MR enterography are useful for evaluating active small-bowel disease, strictures, fistulas, inflammatory masses, and abscesses. Pelvic MRI is superior to pelvic CT for demonstrating perianal fistulas and ischiorectal abscesses. Wireless capsule endoscopy should not be used when a small-bowel stricture is suspected. ### Treatment information provided
Exclusive enteral nutrition or bowel rest with total parenteral nutrition can induce remission in active CD, although these approaches are less effective for maintenance therapy. The selected references also provide a stepwise treatment overview in **Figure 337-12**. (7)

Surgical indications include medically unresponsive strictures or obstruction, massive hemorrhage, refractory fistulas, abscesses, intractable or fulminant disease, obstruction, refractory perianal disease, and dysplasia or cancer. These are listed in **Table 337-10**. (7)
