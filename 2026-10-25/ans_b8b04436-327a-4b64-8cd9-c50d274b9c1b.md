## Answer

 In **ulcerative colitis (UC) flare**, Clexane (**enoxaparin**) should generally be avoided or withheld when there is:

- **Severe or active gastrointestinal bleeding**—use **mechanical VTE prophylaxis** instead; anticoagulant prophylaxis can be considered once bleeding is controlled. (1)  
- **Active major bleeding** from any site. (2)  
- **Previous immune-mediated heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)** within the past 100 days or circulating HIT antibodies. (2)  
- **Known hypersensitivity** to enoxaparin, heparin, pork products, or formulation components. (2)  
- Other labeled high-bleeding-risk conditions, including **active peptic ulceration, hemorrhagic stroke, severe uncontrolled hypertension, hemorrhagic retinopathy, major clotting disorders, or recent injury/surgery involving the brain, spinal cord, eyes, or ears**. (2)

**A UC flare alone, or mild rectal bleeding alone, is not identified in the selected references as an absolute contraindication.** Hospitalized patients with an IBD flare **without severe bleeding** are generally recommended to receive anticoagulant VTE prophylaxis. (1) Enoxaparin dosing may require adjustment in kidney impairment, but the selected references do not provide a specific renal contraindication for Clexane.
