## Answer

 In an **ulcerative colitis (UC) flare**, Clexane (**enoxaparin**) should generally be **withheld/avoided when there is severe or active gastrointestinal bleeding**, particularly active major bleeding. Mechanical VTE prophylaxis is preferred, and anticoagulation may be deferred until bleeding is controlled. (1) (2)

A UC flare **without severe bleeding is not, by itself, a contraindication**; hospitalized patients may be recommended anticoagulant VTE prophylaxis. (1)

Other listed contraindications to enoxaparin include:

- **Known hypersensitivity** to enoxaparin, heparin, pork products, or formulation components  
- **Current or recent immune-mediated heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)** with circulating antibodies  
- **Active major bleeding**
- Under Canadian labeling: **major clotting disorders, active gastric/duodenal ulcer, hemorrhagic stroke, severe uncontrolled hypertension, hemorrhagic retinopathy, or other conditions with increased hemorrhage risk** (3)

The selected references do not provide additional UC-flare-specific contraindications beyond bleeding risk.
