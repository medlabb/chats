## Answer

 The selected references do **not specify UC-flare–specific contraindications** for Clexane (low-molecular-weight heparin).

They do support avoiding or using extreme caution with Clexane in the following situations:

- **High bleeding risk**, because bleeding is the major complication of LMWH; patients at high risk for bleeding may be more safely treated with continuous IV unfractionated heparin. (1)
- **Recent surgery or trauma**, or an **underlying hemostatic defect**, which increase bleeding risk. (1)
- **Current or previous heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)**—LMWH should not be used in patients with HIT because of antibody cross-reactivity and reported thrombosis after switching to LMWH. (2)
- **Renal impairment**, particularly creatinine clearance ≤30 mL/min, where LMWH accumulation may occur and anti-factor Xa monitoring is advised. The selected references do not explicitly label this as an absolute contraindication. (1)

The references do not provide a specific platelet-count threshold or define whether a particular degree of UC rectal bleeding constitutes an absolute contraindication.
