## Answer

 ## Tetanus vaccines, immunoglobulin, and antitoxin

### 1. Tetanus vaccines — active immunization
- Tetanus-containing vaccines include **DTaP** for children <7 years, and **Tdap or Td** for older children and adults. Tdap and DTaP are not interchangeable. (1)
- Patients with tetanus should begin a **full active-immunization series once stabilized**, because illness does not reliably provide immunity. Booster doses are generally given every **10 years** in adulthood. (2)
- For wound management, vaccination depends on whether the wound is **clean/minor or contaminated**, the number of previous doses, and the interval since the last dose. (3)

| Immunization history | Clean, minor wound | Other/contaminated wound |
|---|---|---|
| Unknown or <3 doses | Vaccine: **Yes**; TIG: **No** | Vaccine: **Yes**; TIG: **Yes** |
| ≥3 doses | Vaccine only if ≥10 years since last dose; TIG: **No** | Vaccine only if ≥5 years since last dose; TIG: **No** |

Other/contaminated wounds include wounds contaminated with soil, feces, saliva, puncture wounds, crush injuries, tears, burns, and frostbite. Patients with HIV infection or severe immunodeficiency and a contaminated wound should receive TIG regardless of immunization history. (3)

### 2. Tetanus immune globulin (TIG/HTIG)
- TIG provides **passive immunity** by supplying antibodies that neutralize free tetanus toxin. (4)
- For **post-exposure prophylaxis**, the listed dose is **250 units IM once**, given with a tetanus-containing vaccine when indicated. (5)
- For **treatment of active tetanus**, a commonly recommended dose is **500 units IM once**; part of the dose may be infiltrated around the wound. Higher doses of 3,000–6,000 units have also been used, but lower dosing is described as having similar efficacy with potentially less discomfort. (3)
- Administer the vaccine and TIG using **separate syringes at different anatomical sites**; they should not be mixed in the same syringe. (6)
- TIG is for **IM use only**, not IV. (6)

### 3. Antitoxin for established tetanus
- Passive immunization with antitoxin is standard care to neutralize **unbound toxin**. Human tetanus immune globulin is preferred; if unavailable, human immunoglobulin or equine antitoxin are alternatives. (7)
- Suggested treatment dosing is **HTIG 500 units IM once**, administered as soon as tetanus is suspected, with part infiltrated around the wound. Repeat dosing is generally unnecessary because of the long half-life. (7)
- If IV human immunoglobulin is used, the suggested dose is **200–400 mg/kg**. (7)
- Equine antitoxin requires an intradermal test dose before the full dose because of hypersensitivity risk. (7)
- Antitoxin cannot reverse toxin that has already irreversibly bound to nervous tissue; symptoms may therefore continue worsening for hours to days after administration. (7)
- **Intrathecal antitoxin is generally not routinely indicated** in addition to IM antitoxin because its benefit is unclear. (7)

Wound cleaning, and when necessary surgical debridement and antibiotics, are also part of appropriate tetanus prevention or treatment. (5)
