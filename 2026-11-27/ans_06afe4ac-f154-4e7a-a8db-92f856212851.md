## Answer

 ## Treatment of gastroenteritis

- **Rehydration is the priority.** Use **oral rehydration solution (ORS)** for mild–moderate dehydration. Give IV fluids if dehydration is severe or the patient cannot tolerate oral fluids. (rf_75, 1)
- **Replace ongoing losses.** In children without dehydration, ORS may be given after each episode—approximately **50–100 mL** for children ≤2 years and **100–200 mL** for children >2 years. (2)
- **Resume nutrition early.** Restart an age-appropriate, unrestricted diet after rehydration; breastfeeding should continue. Complex carbohydrates, lean meats, yogurt, fruits, and vegetables may be better tolerated. Avoid highly sugary drinks, which may worsen stool output. (rf_75, 3)
- **Antibiotics are not useful for viral gastroenteritis** and are not routinely indicated. They may sometimes be used for bacterial gastroenteritis depending on the organism and severity. (4, 1)
- **Antidiarrheal drugs should not be used routinely**, particularly when fever or bloody/mucoid stools suggest dysentery. (5, 6)
- **Vomiting:** Ondansetron may facilitate oral rehydration in children ≥6 months with mild–moderate dehydration when vomiting interferes with ORS; it is not recommended routinely for children without dehydration. (5, 7)
- **Zinc:** Recommended for children older than six months with diarrhea in resource-limited settings where zinc deficiency is prevalent. (7)

Acute viral gastroenteritis is generally self-limited, and no specific antiviral treatment is available. (3, 1)
