## Answer

 **Bacterial gastroenteritis** is inflammation of the stomach and intestines caused by bacteria. Common bacterial causes include **Salmonella, Campylobacter, Shigella, diarrheagenic *E. coli*, *Clostridioides difficile*, Yersinia,** and others. (1) (2)

Features that suggest bacterial rather than viral gastroenteritis include:

- **Bloody or mucoid diarrhea**
- **High fever**, severe abdominal pain, tenesmus, or smaller-volume stools
- Exposure to international travel, poultry or farm animals, processed meat, untreated water, or natural bodies of water
- An elevated band count, when a complete blood count is performed (2)

Examples of clinical patterns include:

- **Salmonella:** diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, and fever, often after exposure to poultry, eggs, or milk products; symptoms typically begin within 8–72 hours. (3)
- **Campylobacter:** often linked to undercooked poultry; diarrhea may be watery or bloody and usually begins 2–5 days after exposure. (3)
- **Shigella:** abdominal cramps, fever, and mucoid or bloody diarrhea, typically beginning 1–7 days after exposure. (3)
- **Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli*:** abdominal pain and bloody diarrhea, often with absent or low-grade fever; it may be complicated by hemolytic uremic syndrome. (3)

Diagnosis may involve stool culture or other stool testing, depending on the suspected organism. (3) (4)
