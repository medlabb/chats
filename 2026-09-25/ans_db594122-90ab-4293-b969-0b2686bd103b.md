```
In a patient with suspected infective endocarditis, blood cultures are positive for Staphylococcus aureus. The patient develops acute severe mitral regurgitation with pulmonary edema. According to Harrison’s Principles of Internal Medicine, what are the indications for urgent/early cardiac surgery in infective endocarditis, and how does the presence of acute severe valvular regurgitation with heart failure affect the timing of surgery?
```

- - -

## Answer

 According to **Harrison’s**, indications for cardiac surgery in infective endocarditis include:

### Surgery required for optimal outcome
- **Moderate or severe congestive heart failure or shock due to valve dysfunction**
- **Paravalvular extension** with abscess, fistula, or heart block
- **Persistent bacteremia** without an extracardiac source despite 7–10 days of optimal antimicrobial therapy
- Infection for which there is **no effective antimicrobial therapy**
- **Prosthetic-valve endocarditis**, particularly with a partially dehisced or unstable prosthesis (1)

### Surgery strongly considered
- Native-valve vegetation **>10 mm** with prior systemic embolus and significant valve dysfunction
- Very large vegetation, **>30 mm**
- Relapsed or poorly responsive infection due to highly resistant organisms
- **Staphylococcal prosthetic-valve endocarditis with intracardiac complications** (1)

For **S. aureus native-valve endocarditis**, surgery is guided by the standard indications rather than by the organism alone. In S. aureus prosthetic-valve endocarditis, the greatest surgical benefit is seen with paravalvular infection, valve dysfunction, or heart failure (2).

### Effect of acute severe mitral regurgitation with pulmonary edema

**Valve dysfunction causing pulmonary edema is an emergent indication for surgery—typically the same day.** The timing table specifically lists “valve dysfunction with pulmonary edema or cardiogenic shock” under emergent intervention. Acute mitral regurgitation with NYHA class III or IV heart failure is otherwise listed as requiring **urgent surgery within 1–2 days** (1).

Thus, in this patient, acute severe mitral regurgitation with pulmonary edema should prompt **immediate surgical evaluation and emergent mitral-valve surgery**, rather than waiting for completion of antibiotic therapy. Harrison’s emphasizes that surgery should not be delayed when hemodynamics are deteriorating, because outcomes are better with surgery during the initial days of therapy in life-threatening cases (2; 3).
