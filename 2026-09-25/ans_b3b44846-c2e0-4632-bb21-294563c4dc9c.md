 ## Treatment of acute cystitis

Treatment depends on whether cystitis is **uncomplicated**, occurs in a **man**, is **pregnancy-associated**, or is **catheter-associated**.

### Acute uncomplicated cystitis in women

Preferred oral options include:

| Antibiotic | Typical regimen | Duration |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Nitrofurantoin** | 100 mg twice daily | 5 days |
| **TMP-SMX** | 1 double-strength tablet twice daily | 3 days |
| **Fosfomycin** | 3-g sachet orally once | 1 day |
| **Pivmecillinam** | 400 mg twice daily | 3–7 days |

These regimens and common adverse effects are summarized in **Table 140-1**. Antibiotic selection should consider prior cultures, local resistance patterns, and recent antibiotic exposure. (1) (2) (3)

**β-lactams** are alternative agents, generally given for 5–7 days, but may have higher clinical failure rates. **Fluoroquinolones** are effective but should generally be avoided for uncomplicated cystitis when other effective options are available because of resistance concerns and potentially serious adverse effects. (1) (4)

### Cystitis in men

Cystitis in men is uncommon and should be distinguished from **prostatitis, pyelonephritis, and urinary obstruction**. Afebrile men with true uncomplicated cystitis are generally treated for **7 days**. If prostatic involvement is suspected, **TMP-SMX or a fluoroquinolone** is preferred over β-lactams because of better prostatic penetration. (5) (1)

### Symptom relief

**Phenazopyridine** and other urinary analgesics may help bladder discomfort, but symptomatic treatment alone is not an adequate substitute for antibiotics in true bacterial UTI. (4)

### Important special situations

- **Pregnancy:** cystitis is generally treated for **5–7 days**. Aminopenicillins or cephalosporins are commonly used, guided by urine culture because of resistance concerns. (6)
- **Catheter-associated UTI:** obtain a urine culture and exchange or remove the catheter when possible; treatment is usually **7–14 days**, depending on clinical response. (6)
- **Suspected pyelonephritis or systemic illness:** this requires treatment directed at tissue-invasive infection rather than uncomplicated cystitis. Nitrofurantoin and oral fosfomycin should not be used for pyelonephritis because renal tissue levels are inadequate. (2)
