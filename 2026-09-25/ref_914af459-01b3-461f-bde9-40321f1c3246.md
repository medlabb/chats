### Dandy-Walker Malformation



The Dandy-Walker malformation is a fluid-filled posterior fossa cyst, with variable occurrence of lateral ventriculomegaly; the posterior fossa is always enlarged and the tentorium uplifted. 62 The cerebellar hemispheres, separated by the cyst, are rudimentary. Agenesis of the cerebellar vermis is difficult to confirm before 20 weeks because wide variations in developmental timing mean that full formation may not yet be present. The Dandy-Walker variant consists of direct communication between the fourth ventricle and cisterna magna, without posterior fossa enlargement and with only mild hypoplasia of the inferior vermis. The subtle ultrasound findings can be associated with serious abnormalities of neuronal migration; confirmation from MRI is helpful ( Fig. 10.49 ). The fetal brainstem-vermis (BV) and brainstem-tentorium (BT) angles are of great help in the differential diagnosis of upward rotation of the fetal cerebellar vermis. These simple and reproducible measurements provide valuable additional information for the categorization of upward rotation of the fetal cerebellar vermis and posterior fossa cyst. A BV angle >45 degrees is strongly suggestive of a Dandy-Walker malformation, whereas a measurement of <30 degrees favors the diagnosis of a Blake’s pouch cyst. 62

# Brain



Sonography is the primary means of evaluating cranial and neck pathology in the newborn. The most frequent indication for cranial ultrasound is to evaluate for preterm brain injury in premature infants. Sonography is performed at the bedside through the anterior fontanelle with a high-frequency sector transducer. 66 Imaging through the anterior fontanelle provides coronal and sagittal images of the near and far-field brain anatomy. Near-field images assess gray-white matter differentiation, extra-axial fluid compartment, meninges, and dural venous sinuses. Far-field images depict ventricles and cisterns and sulcal/gyral morphology. Imaging through the mastoid fontanelle best characterizes the anatomy of the tentorium, brainstem, and occipital horns and improves detection of findings in the posterior fossa, such as cerebellar hemorrhage. Color, power, and spectral Doppler ultrasound techniques offer effective evaluation of morphology and flow characteristics of large intracranial arteries and veins for sinovenous thrombosis and vascular malformations. Newer scanners and transducers offer novel advanced imaging techniques such as microvessel imaging that may contribute functional insights impacting diagnostic and therapeutic decision-making. 67 Evaluation of parenchymal, subarachnoid, and subdural abnormalities is limited on sonographic examination, and they are better characterized with cross-sectional imaging. CT is rarely used and should be limited for emergencies such as evaluation of intracranial hemorrhage and masses with increased intracranial pressure. MRI is used to optimally characterize congenital anomalies, infectious, and neoplastic processes. MRI is more sensitive for detection of cerebellar hemorrhage, even when mastoid views are performed on cranial ultrasound. 68 MRI is also the gold standard for evaluation of preterm white matter brain injury but is performed less frequently in light of risks related to transport, need for sedation, and high cost. Although MRI provides optimal anatomic detail, the value of routine MRI screening of preterm infants at term-equivalent or discharge is questioned because findings do not correlate with long-term neurodevelopmental outcomes. 69

# Detailed Neurologic Assessment



If a neurologic concern is raised based on the history, prenatal imaging, physical exam, or postnatal course, a more detailed neurologic assessment should be performed, directed toward the area of concern. The infant’s neurologic evaluation combines elements of the standard neurologic examination and a developmental assessment of gestational age ( Box 27.4 ). It also highlights the marked effect of gestational age on neurologic development. With experience, the examiner can accomplish a standard neurologic evaluationwith minimal or no discomfort to the infant—an important skill, especially in fragile premature infants.
