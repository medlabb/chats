## Answer

 An apparent **absent cerebellar vermis on cranial ultrasound should not be considered definitive**, particularly if only the anterior-fontanel view was obtained. Posterior fossa assessment can be suboptimal with that window; mastoid-fontanel views provide better visualization of the vermis, fourth ventricle, and cisterna magna, while transnuchal ultrasound is another option. (1) (2)

The finding should therefore be **confirmed with dedicated posterior-fossa imaging and expert review**. MRI may be considered if the abnormality remains uncertain, since ultrasound is less sensitive for subtle cerebellar abnormalities. (1) (3)

The selected references do not specify whether MRI or other work-up must be completed **before discharge**. They do note that serial ultrasound is performed in high-risk neonates through discharge or term-equivalent age. (4)
