What are the indication of cholecystectomy?

## Answer

 **Indications for cholecystectomy supported by the references include:**

- **Symptomatic gallstone disease:** Frequent or severe biliary symptoms that interfere with daily activities. (1)
- **Previous complications of gallstone disease**, including:
  - Acute cholecystitis
  - Gallstone pancreatitis
  - Gallstone fistula (1)
- **Acute cholecystitis:** Early laparoscopic cholecystectomy is preferred, ideally within **48–72 hours** of diagnosis. (2)
- **Complicated acute cholecystitis**, such as suspected or confirmed:
  - Empyema
  - Emphysematous cholecystitis
  - Perforation  
  These generally require urgent or emergency cholecystectomy, although drainage may be used initially in seriously ill patients. (2) (3)
- **Acute acalculous cholecystitis**, for which early diagnosis and surgical intervention are important. (4)
- **Mirizzi syndrome:** Removal of the impacted stone, cystic duct, and diseased gallbladder is indicated. (4)
- **Acalculous cholecystopathy** with recurrent typical right-upper-quadrant biliary pain, gallbladder ejection fraction **<40%**, and reproduction of pain during CCK infusion. (3)
- Selected patients with **very large gallstones (>3 cm)** or gallstones in a **congenitally anomalous gallbladder** may be considered for prophylactic cholecystectomy. (1)
- **Porcelain gallbladder:** Cholecystectomy may be considered, but it is **not mandatory in all cases**; close surveillance is also acceptable. (5)

Routine prophylactic cholecystectomy is generally **not warranted for asymptomatic gallstones** because the risk of symptoms or complications is small. (6) (1)
