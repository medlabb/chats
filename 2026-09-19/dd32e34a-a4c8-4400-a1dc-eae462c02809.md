## Answer

 If the mother’s episode is considered a **primary genital HSV infection acquired near delivery**—particularly within approximately six weeks—then **observation alone is not supported** by the selected references. Even without active lesions at delivery, this situation is considered higher risk, although management is described as uncertain for delivery decisions. (1)

For an **asymptomatic newborn in the high-risk primary-exposure category**, the recommended evaluation at approximately **24 hours of age** includes:

- Surface HSV cultures or PCR from conjunctivae, mouth, nasopharynx, and rectum  
- Whole-blood or plasma HSV PCR  
- **CSF HSV PCR, cell count, and chemistries**  
- AST/ALT  
- CBC with differential and platelets (2)

The reference also supports starting **empiric IV acyclovir while awaiting virologic results**, at 60 mg/kg/day divided every 8 hours. (2) Acyclovir should be started promptly when neonatal HSV is suspected. (3)

Thus, in the scenario as described—**presumed maternal primary infection close to delivery**—the selected references support **CSF evaluation plus blood PCR and surface testing, with empiric IV acyclovir**, rather than blood PCR and observation alone.

Maternal type-specific HSV serology should be obtained or reviewed to better characterize whether the maternal infection was truly primary; the provided references do not define or interpret a result described as “blood PCR negative for IgG and IgM.” (2)
