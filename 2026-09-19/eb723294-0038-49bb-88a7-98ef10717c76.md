## Answer

 **Actual task:** Determine whether a term, asymptomatic infant needs CSF testing, blood HSV PCR, observation, and/or empiric acyclovir after maternal primary HSV infection treated for 4–6 weeks, with no lesions at delivery.

Based on the provided references:

- If the mother has a **history of genital HSV but no active lesions at delivery**, and the infant is asymptomatic, the infant generally requires **clinical observation rather than routine CSF testing, blood PCR, or acyclovir**. Monitoring should continue for the first **6 weeks**, with education regarding vesicular rash, conjunctivitis, irritability, poor feeding, temperature instability, lethargy, seizures, or sepsis-like illness. (1)
- However, a **primary or first-episode infection within approximately 6 weeks of delivery** is a higher-risk and less clearly defined situation, particularly if maternal antibody status is uncertain. The references specifically recommend **pediatric infectious-disease consultation** in challenging cases. (2)
- If the infant develops symptoms or any HSV testing is positive, a complete evaluation is indicated, including **CSF HSV PCR, blood HSV PCR, surface cultures/PCR, and other laboratory studies**, with IV acyclovir while results are pending. (1) (3)
- For an infant exposed to a mother with **active lesions and presumed primary infection at delivery**, the suggested evaluation at about 24 hours includes surface PCR/cultures, blood PCR, CSF HSV PCR, CSF studies, liver enzymes, and CBC, with empiric IV acyclovir. That scenario is different from the case described because there were no active lesions at delivery. (4)

The maternal result is described unusually: the references discuss **type-specific maternal HSV serology** to characterize infection, not “blood PCR negative for IgG and IgM.” Maternal blood testing alone does not establish that the infant is uninfected. (4)

Therefore, for an **asymptomatic infant with no maternal lesions at delivery**, routine CSF testing and empiric acyclovir are not supported by these references; arrange close observation and urgent neonatal infectious-disease input because the episode was reportedly primary and occurred near delivery.
