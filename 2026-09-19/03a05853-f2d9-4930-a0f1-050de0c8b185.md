### Prevention

The Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine (SMFM), American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), and the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommend low-dose aspirin (81 mg/day) prophylaxis in women at high risk of preeclampsia or with more than one moderate-risk factor. Prophylaxis should be initiated between 12 and 28 weeks’ gestation (optimally before 16 weeks) and continued daily until delivery. High-risk factors include a history of preeclampsia, multifetal gestation, chronic hypertension, pregestational diabetes, chronic renal disease, and autoimmune disease (systemic lupus erythematosus, antiphospholipid syndrome). Moderate-risk factors include nulliparity, obesity (body mass index [BMI] >30), family history of preeclampsia (mother or sister), Black race, age greater than 35 years, and personal history factors (low birth weight or small for gestational age, previous adverse pregnancy outcome, >10-year pregnancy interval).
