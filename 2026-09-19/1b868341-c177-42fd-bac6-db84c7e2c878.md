```
Term  baby born to a mother with primary hsv who was receiving treatment for the past 4-6 weeks before delivery . No active lesions at the time of delivery. 
Mother's blood PCR is negative for igg and igm .  Is there any indication for CSF ? Only observation or blood PCR needed along with acivir ?
```

- - -

## Answer

 A **primary maternal HSV infection at the time of delivery** carries an approximate neonatal infection risk of **50%**, whereas recurrent infection carries a risk of **less than 1%**. (1)

If no genital, cervical, or vulvar lesions are present at labor, **vaginal delivery is considered safe** according to the reference. Cesarean delivery is recommended when lesions are present at labor or after rupture of membranes. (2)

Maternal HSV serology may be falsely negative early in infection: median seroconversion occurs around **22 days**, and some patients—particularly those receiving antiviral therapy—may remain seronegative for longer. Type-specific HSV-1/HSV-2 antibody testing is the relevant serologic test; the reference does not support using a negative blood PCR for IgG or IgM to exclude infection. (3)

The selected references **do not provide guidance on neonatal CSF testing, neonatal blood PCR, empiric neonatal acyclovir, or observation alone**. These decisions therefore cannot be determined from the selected references.
