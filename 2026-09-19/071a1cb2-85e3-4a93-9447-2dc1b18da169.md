```
/q Term  baby born to a mother with primary hsv who was receiving treatment for the past 4-6 weeks before delivery . No active lesions at the time of delivery. 
Mother's blood PCR is negative for igg and igm .  Is there any indication for CSF ? Only observation or blood PCR needed along with acivir ?
```

## Answer

 A **primary HSV infection at the time of delivery** carries an approximately **50% risk of neonatal infection**, whereas recurrent infection has a risk below 1%. (1)

If no genital, cervical, or vulvar lesions are present at labor, **vaginal delivery is considered safe**; cesarean delivery is recommended when lesions are identified. (2)

Negative maternal HSV serology does **not necessarily exclude recent infection**: antibodies may be negative early, with a median seroconversion time of 22 days, and approximately 20% may remain seronegative after 3 months, particularly after antiviral therapy. (3)

The selected references **do not provide guidance on neonatal CSF testing, neonatal blood PCR, observation alone, or empiric neonatal acyclovir**.
