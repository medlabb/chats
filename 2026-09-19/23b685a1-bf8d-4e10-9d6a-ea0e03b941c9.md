## Answer

 If the mother has **no active genital lesions at delivery**, an asymptomatic infant born by elective cesarean delivery without rupture of membranes requires **no routine HSV testing or antiviral treatment**. The infant should be monitored clinically for signs of HSV infection during the first **six weeks**, and parents/caregivers should be educated about concerning symptoms. (1)

If the mother had **active lesions at delivery**, surface HSV cultures/PCR and whole-blood or plasma HSV PCR should be obtained at approximately **24 hours of age**; routine prophylactic acyclovir is generally not recommended when the infant remains asymptomatic, although additional risk factors may alter management.
