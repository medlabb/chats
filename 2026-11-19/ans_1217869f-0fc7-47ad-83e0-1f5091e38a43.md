## Answer

 **TTP is a medical emergency** requiring urgent treatment; therapy should not be delayed while awaiting ADAMTS13 results when the clinical suspicion is high. (1; 2)

### Suspected or confirmed immune TTP

- **Therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE):** Start promptly, using plasma as the replacement fluid, and continue daily until platelet recovery. TPE both replaces ADAMTS13 and removes ADAMTS13 autoantibodies and ultralarge von Willebrand factor multimers. (3; 1)
- **Glucocorticoid:** Give in addition to TPE. A typical regimen is prednisone 1 mg/kg/day orally; high-risk patients may receive methylprednisolone 1000 mg/day intravenously for 3 days, followed by prednisone 1 mg/kg/day. (1)
- **Rituximab:** Once severe ADAMTS13 deficiency is confirmed (<10%), it may be added; a listed regimen is 375 mg/m² weekly for 4 weeks. (1)
- **Caplacizumab:** Consider adding it in patients with severe features such as critical illness, neurologic findings, or high troponin. Its use in other patients is variable. (1)

**Plasma infusion** does not replace TPE and should not delay it, but may be used temporarily if TPE is not immediately available. (3; 1)

### Monitoring and stopping therapy

Monitor clinically with daily CBC and LDH when abnormal. TPE can generally be stopped when the platelet count is at least 150,000/µL for 2 days, or normal/supranormal for 3 days. Glucocorticoids are tapered over approximately 2–3 weeks. (1)

### Congenital TTP

Acute episodes require an urgent source of ADAMTS13, usually plasma; if recombinant ADAMTS13 and plasma are both available, recombinant ADAMTS13 is suggested in the cited guidance. (4)

Management should occur urgently with hematology/apheresis expertise.
