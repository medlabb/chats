## APPROACH TO DIAGNOSIS

The first step in evaluating cognitive impairment is to determine whether the problem affects the level of consciousness (as in confusional state or coma) or the content of consciousness. **[Table 5-3](#greenberg11_ch5tb3)** lists key differences that may be useful in making this distinction. If the content of consciousness is impaired, but the level of consciousness is preserved, a diffuse cognitive disorder (dementia) must then be distinguished from a more circumscribed deficit, such as amnesia or aphasia. This distinction is important because it determines the subsequent diagnostic approach.

### MENTAL STATUS EXAMINATION

The mental status examination (**[Table 5-5](#greenberg11_ch5tb5)**) helps determine whether the level or the content of consciousness is impaired and whether cognitive dysfunction is diffuse or circumscribed. A disorder of the level of consciousness (eg, confusional state) is suggested by sleepiness, inattention, impairment of immediate recall, or disorientation regarding place or time. Abnormalities in these areas are unusual in dementia until the disorder is far advanced.
