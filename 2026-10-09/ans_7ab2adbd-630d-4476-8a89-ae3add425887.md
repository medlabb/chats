## Answer

 ## Organophosphate toxicity

Organophosphates are potent **acetylcholinesterase inhibitors**, causing excess acetylcholine after **ingestion, inhalation, or dermal exposure**. (1) (2)

### Onset
- Oral or respiratory exposure usually causes symptoms within **3 hours**.
- Dermal absorption may be delayed up to **12 hours**.
- Lipophilic agents may cause delayed coma or respiratory failure—sometimes up to **5 days**—and prolonged illness. (3)

### Clinical features
Acute toxicity causes **cholinergic excess**, including:

- **Muscarinic:** bradycardia, miosis, lacrimation, salivation, bronchorrhea, bronchospasm, urination, vomiting, diarrhea, diaphoresis
- **Nicotinic:** fasciculations, muscle weakness, and paralysis
- **CNS:** lethargy, seizures, coma, and central respiratory depression

Useful mnemonics include **SLUDGE/BBB** and **DUMBELS**, although they do not capture all CNS and nicotinic effects. (3)

Severe poisoning can cause **respiratory failure** from CNS depression, neuromuscular weakness, bronchospasm, and excessive secretions. Cardiovascular collapse, dysrhythmias, QTc prolongation, hypotension, and myocardial ischemia may also occur. (3)

### Delayed complications
- **Intermediate neurologic syndrome:** occurs in approximately 10–40% of patients, typically **24–96 hours** after exposure, with neck-flexion weakness, reduced reflexes, cranial nerve abnormalities, proximal weakness, and respiratory insufficiency. (4)
- **Organophosphate-induced delayed neuropathy:** typically occurs **1–3 weeks** after exposure, beginning with painful stocking-glove paresthesias followed by symmetrical, ascending motor weakness. (5)

### Diagnosis
Diagnosis is primarily **clinical**, based on cholinergic findings, and treatment should not be delayed while awaiting laboratory confirmation. Confirmation may be obtained using red blood cell acetylcholinesterase or butyrylcholinesterase activity, although the latter correlates poorly with severity. (4)

### Initial management
- Provide **100% oxygen** and immediate tracheal intubation for markedly depressed mental status.
- Use a **nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocker**, such as rocuronium, rather than succinylcholine.
- Give isotonic crystalloid for volume resuscitation.
- Monitor with ECG and continuous cardiac monitoring.
- Treat seizures or agitation with a **benzodiazepine**. (6)

### Antidotal therapy
- **Atropine:** recommended for cholinergic toxicity. In moderate-to-severe toxicity, adult treatment begins with **2–5 mg IV**, doubling the dose every 3–5 minutes if needed until pulmonary muscarinic manifestations improve; a continuous infusion is then required. (4)
- **Pralidoxime:** suggested for OP exposure with cholinergic toxicity, fasciculations, neuromuscular dysfunction, or risk of delayed neurotoxicity. It should be administered **only with concurrent atropine**, slowly over 15–30 minutes, followed by continuous infusion when indicated. (7)
- **Decontamination:** remove contaminated clothing and vigorously irrigate affected skin. After ingestion within one hour, activated charcoal may be given at **1 g/kg, maximum 50 g**, following initial resuscitation. (rf_25)
