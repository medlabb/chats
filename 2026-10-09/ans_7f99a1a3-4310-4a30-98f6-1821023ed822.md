 ## Treatment of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF)

- **SGLT-2 inhibitors:** Dapagliflozin or empagliflozin are the only therapies identified in the references as reducing cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalization in HFpEF. They cause mild diuresis and blood-pressure reduction, so caution is needed in patients who are dehydrated or hypotensive. (1) (2)

- **Diuretics:** Use diuretics to control symptoms caused by fluid overload. (2)

- **Treat comorbidities and reversible causes:** Management should address **hypertension, diabetes, obesity, and arrhythmias**, including atrial fibrillation and high-burden premature ventricular contractions. Control of tachycardia may be important because it shortens diastolic filling time. Pericardial disease and atrial tachycardias should also be considered as potentially reversible contributors. (1) (3)

- **Weight-management therapies in patients with obesity:** Semaglutide and tirzepatide have been reported to improve heart-failure symptoms and exercise function in patients with HF and obesity. (3)

- **Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system therapies:**
  - ACE inhibitors and ARBs have not been shown to improve HFpEF outcomes, but may be used for associated hypertension. (2)
  - Sacubitril/valsartan did not substantially improve outcomes in patients with preserved LVEF, although it may benefit patients with mildly reduced LVEF (41–50%). (2)
  - Spironolactone may be considered selectively, particularly in patients with hypertension. The cited criteria include LVEF ≥45%, elevated BNP or heart-failure hospitalization within 1 year, eGFR >30 mL/min/1.73 m², creatinine <2.5 mg/dL, and potassium <5.0 mEq/L. It may reduce hospitalization but carries risks of hyperkalemia and kidney dysfunction. (2) (4)

- **Devices and revascularization:** ICD and cardiac-resynchronization therapies do not have a role in HFpEF. Revascularization should be guided by the same considerations used for patients with reduced EF. (5)

- **Lifestyle and follow-up measures:** Moderate salt restriction, home monitoring of weight and clinical status, multidisciplinary case management, patient-directed diuretic adjustment, and regular gradual exercise when clinically stable are described as useful heart-failure management strategies. (6)
