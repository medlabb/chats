 ## Status epilepticus management

**Generalized convulsive status epilepticus (GCSE)** is an unremitting generalized convulsive seizure lasting **>5 minutes**, or recurrent convulsive seizures without return to baseline consciousness. It is a time-sensitive medical and neurologic emergency. (1) (2)

### 1. Immediate stabilization and assessment
Proceed simultaneously with treatment:

- Maintain **airway, breathing, and circulation**; provide cardiorespiratory monitoring and continuous pulse oximetry. (3) (2)
- Establish IV or intraosseous access, but **do not delay benzodiazepine treatment while awaiting access**. (2)
- Check rapid glucose, electrolytes, calcium, and—when relevant—magnesium and antiseizure medication levels; obtain toxicology studies when indicated and blood cultures if infection is suspected. (2)
- Correct hypoglycemia and metabolic abnormalities and treat suspected life-threatening causes, including trauma, sepsis, meningitis, encephalitis, or structural brain lesions. (3)
- Treat clinically obvious GCSE immediately; do not wait for EEG or other studies. (4) If consciousness does not return within **10–20 minutes** after convulsions stop, continuous EEG is needed to evaluate for ongoing nonconvulsive seizures. (2)

### 2. First-line treatment: benzodiazepine
- **IV access:** lorazepam **0.1 mg/kg IV**, at a maximum rate of **2 mg/minute**; reassess after approximately 3–5 minutes and give additional treatment if seizures persist. A fixed adult dose is **4 mg IV**, which may be repeated if still seizing. (2) (4)
- **No IV access / prehospital treatment:** midazolam **10 mg IM** is supported; intranasal or buccal midazolam is also an option. (2) (4)
- Other routes described include rectal or intranasal diazepam and buccal midazolam. (4)

### 3. Longer-acting antiseizure medication
Give an IV loading dose of a longer-acting nonbenzodiazepine antiseizure medication **even if convulsions stop after benzodiazepine treatment**, to reduce recurrence. (4) Options include:

- **Levetiracetam:** **60 mg/kg IV**, maximum **4500 mg**, infused over 15 minutes. (5)
- **Fosphenytoin:** **20 mg phenytoin equivalents/kg IV**; an additional 5–10 mg PE/kg may be given if seizures persist, up to 30 mg/kg cumulatively. (5)
- **Valproate:** **40 mg/kg IV**, maximum **3000 mg**, infused at 10 mg/kg/minute in adults. (5)

### 4. Refractory status epilepticus
If seizures continue despite two appropriately dosed antiseizure medications—typically a benzodiazepine followed by a longer-acting antiseizure medication—prepare continuous sedative-anesthetic infusion, commonly **midazolam**, with propofol or pentobarbital as other options. (2) (5)

This stage requires **endotracheal intubation, mechanical ventilation, neurologic consultation, intensive care admission, and continuous EEG monitoring**. Hemodynamic complications, including hypotension, may require crystalloid and vasopressor support. (2) (5)
