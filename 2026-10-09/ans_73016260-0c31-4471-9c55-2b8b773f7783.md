## Answer

 - **Normal blood pressure:** approximately **120/80 mm Hg**; normal **mean arterial pressure (MAP)** is **70–90 mm Hg**. (1)  
- **Continuous BP monitoring:** an arterial catheter allows continuous measurement, but readings may be inaccurate with transducer malposition, air bubbles, excessive tubing length, or incorrect calibration. (2)  
- **Hypotension clues:** low pulmonary artery wedge pressure, low central venous pressure, and low cardiac index usually support reduced circulating volume or hypovolemic shock. (1)  
- **Renovascular hypertension:** renal artery stenosis can cause poorly controlled hypertension through renin–angiotensin–aldosterone activation; profound diastolic hypertension may occur. (3)  
- **Aortic dissection:** initial management includes strict BP and heart-rate control, with a target systolic BP of **100–120 mm Hg** and heart rate around **60–80 beats/minute**, provided organ perfusion remains adequate. (4)
