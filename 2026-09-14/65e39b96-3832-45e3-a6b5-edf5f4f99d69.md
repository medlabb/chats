## Answer

 The selected references do not provide **Hajj-specific instructions**, but they support the following precautions for diabetic travelers:

- **Consult your healthcare provider before travel** to develop a diabetes-management plan, review planned activities, and determine whether insulin doses or schedules need adjustment. Obtain a medical letter and prescription copies if needed. (1) (2)
- Wear a **medical alert bracelet or necklace** identifying your diabetes and emergency contact information. (3) (4)
- Keep insulin, glucose-testing supplies, medications, glucagon if prescribed, syringes, and snacks in your **carry-on bag and accessible at all times**. Do not place insulin in checked baggage because extreme cold can damage it. (3) (4)
- Carry your own **rapid glucose-raising treatment**, such as glucose tablets, hard candies, juice, or other snacks, because meals and food access may be irregular. (3) (5)
- **Monitor blood glucose regularly**, particularly when diet, activity, or schedule changes. (6) (7)
- Maintain your eating plan and **drink plenty of water**, especially in hot weather or during physical activity. (6) (8)
- Wear **well-fitting, protective, comfortable footwear**, avoid walking barefoot, and check your feet for sores or blisters. (6) (8)
- During prolonged activity, monitor glucose before, during, and after exercise and keep rapidly absorbed carbohydrates with you. Stop exercising and seek medical attention for chest pain, nausea, or sudden shortness of breath. (8) (5)
- Know the symptoms of **high and low blood sugar** and when to seek emergency help; identify nearby pharmacies and clinics. (6) (9)
