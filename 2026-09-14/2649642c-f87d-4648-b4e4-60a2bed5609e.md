## Answer

 **ALT of 500–700 U/L, even if it falls to 150 U/L, requires prompt evaluation by a pediatrician/pediatric gastroenterologist.** For children aged 1–12 years, an ALT above approximately 30 U/L is above the suggested upper limit of normal; ALT above 80 U/L on two occasions is an indication for further evaluation. (1)

### Important next steps

1. **Repeat a complete liver assessment**, not ALT alone:
   - AST and ALT  
   - Total and direct bilirubin  
   - GGT and alkaline phosphatase  
   - Albumin  
   - PT/INR  
   - Blood glucose and ammonia when clinically indicated  
   - CBC, as part of the full assessment  
   Pediatric evaluation of significant liver enzyme elevation should include a full liver profile and prothrombin time/INR. (2) (3)

2. **Assess urgently for liver dysfunction.** Jaundice, dark urine, bleeding, abdominal swelling, marked sleepiness/confusion, vomiting, or an enlarged liver require urgent medical assessment. INR is particularly important. Acute liver failure criteria include persistent PT ≥15 seconds or INR ≥1.5 with encephalopathy, or PT ≥20 seconds/INR ≥2 even without encephalopathy; children meeting these criteria should be evaluated at a pediatric liver-transplant center. (4)

3. **Review possible causes with the doctor**, including:
   - Recent viral illness and viral hepatitis testing  
   - All medicines, paracetamol/acetaminophen exposure, supplements, and herbal products  
   - Autoimmune hepatitis testing  
   - Inherited/metabolic liver disease, particularly if recurrent or unexplained  
   - Muscle disease or injury when appropriate  
   Causes of marked aminotransferase elevation include viral hepatitis, medication or toxin injury, autoimmune hepatitis, metabolic disease, ischemic injury, and muscle disorders. (5) (3)

4. **Imaging:** An abdominal ultrasound, often with Doppler assessment, is an important part of evaluating the liver and hepatic blood vessels. (3) (5)

5. **Treatment should not be based on ALT alone.** It depends on the identified cause. Until reviewed, the child should not receive non-prescribed medicines, supplements, or herbal remedies; any prescribed medicine should be changed only after discussion with the treating clinician.

Normal TLC, hemoglobin, and CRP do not provide the complete assessment of liver function; **bilirubin and PT/INR are especially important**. The available references do not provide a single diagnosis or a complete age-specific treatment plan for this child.
