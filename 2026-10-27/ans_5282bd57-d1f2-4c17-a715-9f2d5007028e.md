## Answer

 **Do not stop furosemide solely because serum creatinine is elevated.** In cirrhotic ascites, reduce the diuretic dose—or discontinue it if the patient is on a low dose—when:

- **Serum creatinine increases by >100% from baseline or rises to >2 mg/dL**
- Significant hyponatremia, potassium abnormality, or encephalopathy develops (1)

Assess for **intravascular volume depletion/overdiuresis**, monitor urine output, daily weight, electrolytes, blood pressure, and kidney function. Rapid diuresis can cause plasma-volume depletion and azotemia; weight loss should generally not exceed **0.5 kg/day without edema or 1 kg/day with edema** (2).

Because the ascites is **tense**, if rapid fluid removal is needed, the reference recommends **initial large-volume paracentesis** rather than overly aggressive diuresis (2). The selected references do not provide specific additional recommendations for managing diuretics during SBP.
