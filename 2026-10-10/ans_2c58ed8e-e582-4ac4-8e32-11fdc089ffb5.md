## Answer

 **Corticosteroid (CS) doses in adult asthma:**

### **1. Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS)**
Adult daily dose categories vary by drug and device (**Table 9–5**):

| ICS | Low dose | Medium dose | High dose |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Beclomethasone HFA | 80–240 mcg | >240–480 mcg | >480 mcg |
| Budesonide DPI | 180–400 mcg | >400–800 mcg | >800 mcg |
| Fluticasone propionate HFA/MDI | 88–264 mcg | >264–440 mcg | >440 mcg |
| Fluticasone propionate DPI | 100–300 mcg | >300–500 mcg | >500 mcg |
| Mometasone DPI | 200 mcg | 400 mcg | >400 mcg |

Other ICS doses are also listed in **Table 9–5**. Most clinical benefit occurs at low doses, and dosing should be adjusted according to response. (rf_27)

### **2. Oral/systemic corticosteroids for an exacerbation**
- **Prednisone or prednisolone:** **0.5–1 mg/kg/day**, typically **40–60 mg/day**, given in 1–2 doses for **3–7 days**. (1)
- The reference also lists **methylprednisolone, prednisolone, or prednisone 40–60 mg/day**; short “bursts” generally last **3–10 days**. (2) (3)
- For mild-to-moderate exacerbations not improving after 48 hours, a **5–7-day course of prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/day** may be necessary. (4)
- Severe hospitalized exacerbations may require **prednisone or methylprednisolone 1 mg/kg every 6–12 hours initially**, under medical supervision. (1)

The appropriate dose depends on asthma severity, response, inhaler technique, adherence, and comorbidities; these references do not provide pediatric dosing.
