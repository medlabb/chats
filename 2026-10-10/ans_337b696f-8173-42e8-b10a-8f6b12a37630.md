## Answer

 **Yes—phototherapy may still be indicated after 14 days**, but treatment should be based on the **total serum bilirubin (TSB)** and the infant’s risk factors, not jaundice appearance alone. For persistent hyperbilirubinemia beyond 14 days, the available guidance is to use the **14-day TSB phototherapy threshold as the trigger for intervention** while evaluating the underlying cause. (1)

The infant should undergo additional evaluation, particularly measurement of the **direct bilirubin** and assessment for hemolysis, metabolic disease, hypothyroidism, infection, and other causes. Clinically significant hyperbilirubinemia after 14 days is concerning and warrants expert consultation, including pediatric gastroenterology, hematology, and metabolic specialists. (1) (2)

Phototherapy is generally **not useful when the direct bilirubin exceeds 50% of the TSB**. In mixed hyperbilirubinemia, phototherapy can cause transient **bronze baby syndrome**, which is usually harmless and resolves after several weeks or months. (1)

Thus, while evaluation proceeds, phototherapy should be given when the **TSB meets or exceeds the 14-day treatment threshold**, especially if the unconjugated fraction predominates. The selected references do not provide a separate post-14-day treatment chart or more specific thresholds beyond this approach.
