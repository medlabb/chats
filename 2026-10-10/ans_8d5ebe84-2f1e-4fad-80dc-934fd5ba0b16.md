## Answer

 ## Acute bacterial diarrhea management

- **Rehydrate first:** Assess volume status in all patients. Use an **oral rehydration solution (ORS)** containing water, salts, and sugar. Severe hypovolemia requires initial **intravenous fluid replacement**, followed by oral fluids. (1)  
- **Maintain nutrition:** Continue feeding as tolerated. In children, continue breastfeeding and resume nutrient-rich foods promptly after dehydration is corrected. (2) (3)  
- **Avoid routine antibiotics:** Most acute diarrhea is self-limited. Do not routinely give empiric antibiotics for stable, nonbloody, non–travel-associated diarrhea because of adverse effects, antimicrobial resistance, and *C. difficile* risk. (2) (4)  
- **Consider stool testing**—culture and/or multiplex molecular testing—for severe illness, inflammatory diarrhea, high-risk patients, symptoms lasting longer than one week, or public-health concerns. Bloody diarrhea should be tested for Shiga toxin/STEC. (1)  
- **Withhold antibiotics when STEC is suspected**—particularly bloody diarrhea without fever—until testing is performed, because antibiotics may increase the risk of hemolytic uremic syndrome. (4)  
- **Consider empiric antibiotics** for severe illness, inflammatory diarrhea, or high-risk hosts such as older adults, pregnant patients, immunocompromised patients, and those with inflammatory bowel disease. (4)  
  - Suggested options are **azithromycin** or a **fluoroquinolone**. Azithromycin is preferred with fever or dysentery, or when fluoroquinolone resistance is a concern. Regimens listed include azithromycin 1 g once for patients without dysentery or 500 mg daily for 3 days; ciprofloxacin 750 mg once or 500 mg twice daily for 3–5 days; or levofloxacin 500 mg once daily or as a single dose. (4)  
- **Avoid antimotility agents** when fever, bloody stool, or mucoid stool suggests dysentery, because they may prolong or worsen illness. (5)  
- **Children:** Management centers on replacement and maintenance fluids, continued nutrition, and frequent reassessment. Children with severe acute malnutrition should receive empiric broad-spectrum antibiotics. (6) (3)
