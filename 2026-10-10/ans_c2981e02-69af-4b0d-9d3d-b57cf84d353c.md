```
Volume of fluids in acute pancreatitis
```

- - -

## Answer

 - **Preferred fluid:** Lactated Ringer’s solution in most patients; use normal saline instead when pancreatitis is due to hypercalcemia. (1)  
- **Initial volume:** For hypovolemic patients, give a **10 mL/kg IV bolus**, followed by **1.5 mL/kg/hour**. Use goal-directed therapy during the first **24–48 hours**. (1)  
- **Avoid routine aggressive hydration:** A regimen of 20 mL/kg bolus followed by 3 mL/kg/hour caused more fluid overload than moderate hydration in a randomized trial. (1)  
- **Titrate frequently:** Reassess volume status often, particularly during the first 6 hours and over the next 24–48 hours. Targets include heart rate <120/min, MAP 65–85 mmHg, urine output >0.5–1 mL/kg/hour, and decreasing hematocrit and BUN. (2)  
- **Caution:** Continued fluid resuscitation after 48 hours may not be beneficial and excessive fluids increase the risk of respiratory failure, kidney failure, pulmonary edema, and abdominal compartment syndrome. (2)  

A single fixed total volume cannot be determined from the selected references; fluids should be adjusted according to hypovolemia, perfusion, urine output, laboratory trends, and signs of fluid overload.
