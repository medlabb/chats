## Answer

 **Toxoplasma** usually refers to *Toxoplasma gondii*, an intracellular protozoan parasite that infects humans and animals worldwide. Infection is often asymptomatic in immunocompetent people, but serious disease may occur with immunosuppression or congenital infection. (1)

- **Transmission:** Eating food or beverages contaminated with infectious oocysts, eating undercooked meat containing tissue cysts, and transmission from an infected pregnant individual to the fetus are important routes. Cats are the definitive hosts and shed oocysts in feces. (2)  
- **Clinical features:** Acute infection may cause fever and bilateral, symmetrical, nontender cervical lymphadenopathy; most immunocompetent people have no symptoms. Severe manifestations can include pneumonitis, myocarditis, hepatitis, uveitis, or encephalitis. (3)  
- **Pregnancy:** Fetal infection can cause congenital toxoplasmosis, including intracranial calcifications, neurologic disability, and retinal disease. (4)  
- **Prevention:** Avoid rare or uncooked meat, use gloves when gardening, wash hands carefully, and—if pregnant—avoid changing cat litter when possible or use gloves and wash hands afterward. (5)  
- **Diagnosis and treatment:** Diagnosis is typically confirmed with serologic testing. Most immunocompetent, nonpregnant adults do not require treatment because infection is usually self-limited; severe or prolonged illness may require therapy. (3)
