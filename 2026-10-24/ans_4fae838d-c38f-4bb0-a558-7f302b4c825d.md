 ## Diabetes diagnosis

**Diagnostic criteria**—Any one of the following supports diabetes:

- **A1C ≥6.5%** (48 mmol/mol), using a laboratory assay—not point-of-care testing  
- **Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) ≥126 mg/dL** (7.0 mmol/L)  
- **Random plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL** (11.1 mmol/L) with symptoms of hyperglycemia  
- **2-hour plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL** (11.1 mmol/L) during an oral glucose-tolerance test (OGTT) (1)

If the patient is asymptomatic, an abnormal result should generally be **repeated to confirm** the diagnosis. Unexpected or discordant results should be investigated and the qualifying test repeated. (2)

**Interpretation:**

- A1C **5.7–6.4%** or FPG **100–125 mg/dL**: prediabetes  
- A1C **<5.7%** or FPG **<100 mg/dL**: normal range (2)

To distinguish likely type 2 from type 1 diabetes, consider obesity/overweight, insulin-resistance features such as acanthosis nigricans, hypertension, dyslipidemia or PCOS, family history, and clinical course. If the type is uncertain, testing at least two pancreatic autoantibodies may help; two or more positive autoantibodies support treating the patient as having type 1 diabetes with insulin replacement. (1) (3)

## Initial treatment described for type 2 diabetes

- **Diabetes education**, including self-management training or support groups when available.  
- **Lifestyle modification:** reduce caloric intake, eliminate refined carbohydrates, and begin progressively increasing physical activity. One example is walking 10–15 minutes daily and gradually working toward 30 minutes at least five days per week.  
- **Medication:** initiate metformin and increase gradually to approximately **2 g/day in divided doses**, when appropriate. A weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist or GIP/GLP-1 dual agonist is described as an alternative when affordable. (4)  
- **Glucose monitoring:** initially monitor fasting glucose each morning, with occasional post-meal and exercise-related checks. Stated targets are fasting/premeal glucose **80–130 mg/dL** and 2–3-hour postprandial glucose **<180 mg/dL**. (4)

**When insulin is often indicated:** catabolic presentation with hyperglycemic symptoms and unintentional weight loss, **A1C >10%**, or glucose readings **>300 mg/dL**. Treatment commonly begins with basal insulin, adding mealtime insulin if needed; some patients require ongoing insulin. (4)
