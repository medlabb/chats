 # Atlas of Skin

The selected references provide an illustrated overview of **normal skin, skin cells, disease patterns, tumors, dermoscopy, and clinical distribution**.

## Normal skin and cellular structures
- **Normal palm skin:** compact, thick stratum corneum typical of palms and soles (1).
- **Normal melanocyte:** dendritic melanocytes deliver protective melanosomes to nearby keratinocytes (1).
- **Langerhans cells:** dendritic cells within the epidermis highlighted by CD1a staining (1).

## Infectious and inflammatory conditions
- **Dermatophytosis:** fungal hyphae within the stratum corneum demonstrated by PAS staining (1).
- **Herpes zoster:** unilateral grouped vesicles and serous crusts on an erythematous background (1).
- **Toxic epidermal necrolysis:** full-thickness epidermal cell death with epidermal stripping (1).
- **Henoch–Schönlein purpura:** ulcers preceded by purpuric papules, bullae, and necrosis (1).

## Pigmented lesions and melanoma
- **Cutaneous melanoma:** a black T4 tumor with ulceration, color variation, and an irregular outline (1).
- **Melanoma histopathology:** tumor thickness and lack of an inflammatory host response are illustrated (1).
- **Common melanoma types:** superficial melanoma, thick melanoma, and facial melanoma in situ are shown in **eFig. 0.3** (2).
- **Increasing melanoma thickness:** melanoma in situ through a 0.9-mm melanoma are shown in **eFig. 0.4** (2).
- **Common melanocytic nevi:** acquired nevus, congenital nevus, and Reed nevus are shown in **eFig. 0.5** (2).

- **Pigmented tumors:** pigmented basal cell carcinoma, angiokeratoma, and dermatofibroma are shown in **eFig. 0.6** (3).

## Distribution patterns
Major patterns include **localized or disseminated, unilateral or bilateral, symmetric or asymmetric, sun-exposed, flexural, extensor, intertriginous, acral, palmoplantar, seborrheic, periorificial, mucosal, and linear** distributions. These are summarized in **Table 0.8** (4).

Examples include:

- Photodistributed disorders involving ultraviolet-exposed areas (4).

## Pigmentary demarcation lines
- Dorsal skin is relatively more hyperpigmented than ventral skin.
- Pigmentary demarcation lines are illustrated on the upper arm, posterior thighs, and upper chest in **Fig. 67.11** and **Fig. 67.12**; their classifications are listed in **Table 67.6** (5).
