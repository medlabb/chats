 ## **Diabetes diagnosis**

Diabetes can be diagnosed by any of the following:

- **A1C ≥6.5%** (48 mmol/mol), measured by a laboratory assay  
- **Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) ≥126 mg/dL** (7 mmol/L)  
- **Random plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL** (11.1 mmol/L) with symptoms of hyperglycemia  
- **Two-hour glucose ≥200 mg/dL** (11.1 mmol/L) during an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) (1)

If symptoms are absent, abnormal results should generally be **repeated to confirm the diagnosis**. (2)

**Prediabetes** is defined as A1C **5.7–6.4%** or FPG **100–125 mg/dL**. (2)

## **Initial treatment for type 2 diabetes**

- **Diabetes education**
- **Lifestyle changes:** reduce calorie intake, eliminate refined carbohydrates, and gradually increase exercise; one example is working toward 30 minutes of walking at least five days per week.
- **Medication:** metformin may be started and gradually increased to 2 g daily in divided doses. A weekly GLP-1 or GIP/GLP-1 medication may be an alternative when affordable. (3)
- **Glucose monitoring:** fasting and occasional post-meal measurements. Suggested goals are fasting/premeal glucose **80–130 mg/dL** and two- to three-hour post-meal glucose **below 180 mg/dL**. (3)

**Insulin** is often indicated initially when there is unintentional weight loss or catabolic symptoms, **A1C >10%**, or glucose levels **>300 mg/dL**. Basal insulin may be started, with mealtime insulin added if needed. (3)

Type 1 diabetes results from absolute insulin deficiency and requires insulin replacement therapy; distinguishing type 1 from type 2 may involve pancreatic autoantibody testing. (4)

The selected references do not provide a complete treatment plan for all diabetes types, pregnancy-related diabetes, or diabetes complications.
