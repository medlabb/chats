 ## **Diabetes: diagnosis and treatment**

### **Diagnosis**

- **Symptoms:** Increased thirst, frequent urination, weight loss, blurred vision, and fatigue may occur. Type 2 diabetes is often asymptomatic and found during routine testing. (1)
- **Symptomatic patients:** Diabetes can be diagnosed when classic hyperglycemia symptoms occur with a random plasma glucose **≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L)**. (1)
- **Asymptomatic patients:**
  - **A1C <5.7%** or fasting plasma glucose (FPG) **<100 mg/dL**: normal.
  - **A1C 5.7–6.4%** or FPG **100–125 mg/dL**: prediabetes.
  - **A1C ≥6.5%** or FPG **≥126 mg/dL**: consistent with diabetes. (2)
- In the absence of unequivocal symptoms, an abnormal result should generally be **repeated to confirm** the diagnosis. If A1C and FPG are both diagnostic and concordant, additional testing is not needed. (1)
- Further assessment may distinguish **type 1 from type 2 diabetes**. If the type is unclear, islet autoantibody testing and sometimes C-peptide with glucose measurement may be used. (1)

### **Treatment**

#### **Type 1 diabetes**
- **Insulin is started at diagnosis.**
- Treatment includes blood-glucose monitoring, education about insulin administration, hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia, nutrition, exercise, and ketone testing. (3)
- Continuous glucose monitoring may be offered soon after diagnosis. A usual A1C goal for many patients is **<7%**, individualized to the patient. (3)

#### **Type 2 diabetes**
- All newly diagnosed patients should receive comprehensive diabetes education, including nutrition, physical activity, weight management, metabolic control, and prevention of complications. (4)
- For most patients without a specific contraindication, **metformin** is suggested as initial medication, together with lifestyle intervention. It may be reasonable to try lifestyle modification for **3–6 months** before medication in selected patients with A1C near target and clear, modifiable contributors to high glucose. (4)
- Metformin should not be used when estimated glomerular filtration rate is **<30 mL/min/1.73 m²** or when conditions predispose to lactic acidosis. (4)
- When cardiovascular or kidney disease is present, a **GLP-1 receptor agonist** or an **SGLT2 inhibitor** with demonstrated cardiorenal benefit may be selected based on comorbidities, benefits, adverse effects, and patient preferences. (4)
- A reasonable A1C goal for many adults with type 2 diabetes is **<7%**, but targets should be individualized, particularly in older adults or those with comorbidities. (4)

### **Screening and follow-up**
- Screening is suggested for adults aged **35–70 years** who are overweight, obese, or have cardiovascular risk factors or other increased-risk conditions. (2)
- If screening is normal, repeat testing about every **3 years**; with prediabetes, repeat testing about **yearly**. (2)
