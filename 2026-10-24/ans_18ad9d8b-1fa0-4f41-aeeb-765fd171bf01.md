## Answer

 Before entering the NICU, it may help to know:

- **The NICU cares for sick or premature newborns.** Babies may be admitted because they were born very early, had breathing problems during birth, or developed conditions such as jaundice, infection, or heart, lung, or intestinal problems. (1) The NICU is also called the neonatal intensive care unit or special care nursery. (2)
- **You may see an incubator or warmer, dim lighting, tubes, wires, monitors, and machines.** These help keep your baby warm, monitor vital signs, provide medicines or fluids, support breathing, and deliver other treatments. (3)
- **Alarms are common.** They may signal that a monitor has come off or that medicine has run out and do not always mean your baby is in distress. Ask the NICU staff to explain what the alarms mean. (3)
- **Clean your hands before entering and before touching your baby.** Use hand sanitizer or soap and water; staff may ask you to clean your hands again. (4)
- **You can and should spend time with your baby.** Depending on your baby’s condition, you may be able to hold them, including **skin-to-skin care**—holding the diapered baby against your bare chest while keeping them covered and warm (figure 1). If holding is not yet safe, you may be able to hold their hand, rest your hand on their head, talk, or sing to them. (4)
- **Learn your baby’s cues.** Arching the back, scowling, or looking away may indicate stress and a need for rest. Open eyes, comfortable breathing, or turning toward your voice may indicate relaxation. (4)
- **Visiting rules may apply.** Family members may be allowed to visit, but they might need to follow rules such as wearing gowns or masks; children may not be allowed to visit because of infection concerns. (4)
- **The experience can be stressful.** Social workers, chaplains, support groups, relatives, and friends may provide support during the hospitalization and after returning home. (5)
