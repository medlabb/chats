```
explain why renal perfusion decreases in early shock
```

- - -

## Answer

 In early shock, **effective arterial volume and renal perfusion fall** because of reduced circulating volume, decreased cardiac output, or systemic vasodilation. This lowers mean arterial pressure and triggers sympathetic nervous system, renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system, and vasopressin responses. The resulting renal vasoconstriction promotes sodium and water retention and helps preserve perfusion to vital organs, but reduces renal blood flow. (1) (2)

Initially, renal autoregulation attempts to preserve GFR through **afferent arteriolar dilation** and **angiotensin II–mediated efferent arteriolar constriction**. However, these mechanisms have a limit; when mean arterial pressure falls below approximately **80 mmHg**, GFR declines steeply. (3) (1)

In **septic shock**, generalized arterial vasodilation may reduce GFR, while sympathetic and hormonal activation can cause renal vasoconstriction; endothelial injury, microvascular thrombosis, and increased interstitial pressure may further reduce local intrarenal blood flow. (4)
