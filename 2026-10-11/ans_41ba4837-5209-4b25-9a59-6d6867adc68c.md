Can I use Diazoxide to treat A 35 weeks 1600 gram baby with persistent Hypoglycemia?

## Answer

 **Diazoxide may be considered if persistent hypoglycemia is confirmed to be due to hyperinsulinism (HI)**; it is described as the **first-line treatment for HI** after the diagnosis is established. The listed dose is **5–15 mg/kg/day orally, divided every 12 hours**. (1; 2)

For a neonate, the reference table lists important potential adverse effects, including **fluid overload, respiratory distress, cytopenias, hyperglycemia, and hypertrichosis**; concomitant diuretic use is noted as **strongly recommended in neonates and infants**. (2)

Diazoxide response should be assessed by whether the infant maintains plasma glucose **>70 mg/dL** while feeding and fasting and/or develops appropriate ketones. Some forms of HI, particularly KATP-related HI, may not respond. (1; 3)

If the infant currently has persistent or symptomatic hypoglycemia, **IV glucose treatment is required**, while the underlying cause is evaluated. (4)
