Want to know how to follow up the baby in case mother was severely anemic during pregnancy

## Answer

 If the mother had **severe anemia during pregnancy**, the baby’s follow-up should be individualized based on gestational age, birth condition, and the cause of the maternal anemia. The degree of anemia at birth may correlate with the mother’s hemoglobin level. (1)

The selected references support the following:

- Check the baby’s **hemoglobin/hematocrit**, and consider a **reticulocyte count** if anemia is suspected. Neonatal anemia may result from blood loss, increased red-cell destruction, or reduced production; maternal health and the perinatal course are important parts of the assessment. (2)
- Account for the normal **physiologic fall in hemoglobin during the first 6–10 weeks**. In healthy term infants, hemoglobin commonly falls to about 9–11 g/dL; premature infants have an earlier and more pronounced decline. (3)
- If the infant has suspected or confirmed **hemolytic disease**, serial outpatient hemoglobin and reticulocyte counts should be monitored under an expert pediatric hematologist. Late anemia may appear at **4–6 weeks**, sometimes with poor feeding or poor growth. (4)
- For infants with **ABO hemolytic disease**, outpatient monitoring is considered essential because hemolysis may persist for weeks and, rarely, transfusion may be needed several weeks after birth. (5)
- Interventions should be guided by the baby’s clinical signs and symptoms rather than hemoglobin alone, with specialist referral when appropriate. (3)

The selected references do **not** provide a specific follow-up schedule for an otherwise well baby solely because the mother had severe, non-hemolytic anemia during pregnancy.
