## Answer

 **Gestational trophoblastic disease (GTD)** comprises lesions caused by abnormal proliferation of placental trophoblasts, including hydatidiform moles and other placental implantation-site lesions. Some GTD can progress to **gestational trophoblastic neoplasia (GTN)**. (1)

**GTN** is characterized by malignant trophoblastic proliferation and may follow either a molar or nonmolar pregnancy. Histologic types include **invasive mole, choriocarcinoma, placental-site trophoblastic tumor (PSTT), and epithelioid trophoblastic tumor (ETT)**. (2)

GTN is generally diagnosed clinically through **persistently elevated serum hCG** after pregnancy, after excluding other causes of elevated hCG; tissue confirmation is not always required before treatment. Evaluation typically includes chest imaging for pulmonary metastases and FIGO staging with WHO risk scoring. (2)
