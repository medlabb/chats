The optimal timing of coronary angiography and revascularization is not known, although the TIMACS trial found better outcomes among high-risk patients (GRACE risk > 140 [or > 3% estimated in-hospital risk of death]) treated with early coronary angiography, within 24 hours of admission. Another smaller trial (ABOARD) found no advantage of immediate catheterization.

Two risk-stratification tools are available that can be used at the bedside, the **GRACE Risk Score** ([https://www.mdcalc.com/calc/1099/grace-acs-risk-mortality-calculator](https://www.mdcalc.com/calc/1099/grace-acs-risk-mortality-calculator)) and the **TIMI Risk Score** ([http://www.timi.org](http://www.timi.org)). The GRACE Risk Score, which applies to patients with or without ST elevation, was developed in a more generalizable registry population and has better discrimination of risk. It includes age (as a continuous variable), Killip class, BP, ST-segment deviation, cardiac arrest at presentation, serum creatinine, elevated creatine kinase (CK)-MB or troponin, and heart rate. The TIMI Risk Score includes seven variables: age 65 years or older, three or more cardiac risk factors, prior coronary stenosis of 50% or more, ST-segment deviation, two anginal events in prior 24 hours, [aspirin](/drug.aspx?gbosId=660322) in prior 7 days, and elevated cardiac markers.

Blood chemistry tests play a major role in defining cardiac risk factors (ie, serum lipid levels, serum human CRP level, serum creatinine) and can help with determining the etiology of some symptoms (ie, serum BNP or NT-proBNP level). Myocardial injury is defined based on the presence of released troponin. The use of high-sensitivity troponin has increased awareness of the frequency of some myocardial injury in patients with non-ACS and has been shown to allow for early discharge of patients with chest pain and no evidence for ACS. A number of other biomarkers are also being studied to better quantitate the severity of HF, but none have entered routine use as yet (other than BNP and NT-proBNP).
