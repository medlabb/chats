## Answer

 **Adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC)** is a rare, often aggressive adrenal tumor. It may produce hormones, causing **Cushing’s syndrome and/or virilization**, or be nonfunctional and present as an abdominal mass or incidental finding. (1)

ACC occurs at about **1–2 cases per million people per year**. It can arise at any age, with peaks before age five and in the fourth to fifth decades; adults generally experience more aggressive disease progression than children. (2)

For potentially resectable stage I–III disease, **complete surgical removal is the only potentially curative treatment**. Hormone assessment is important before surgery; people with cortisol-producing tumors may need glucocorticoid coverage afterward. Surgery is recommended at a specialized referral center. (3)

For metastatic or recurrent ACC, there is **no curative therapy**; steroid-excess symptoms can be managed medically, and metastatic disease is often fatal within a year, although rare long-term responses to chemotherapy with or without mitotane have been reported. (4)
