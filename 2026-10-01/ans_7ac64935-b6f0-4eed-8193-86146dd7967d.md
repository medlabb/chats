## Answer

 **Knee osteoarthritis (OA)** can cause symptoms ranging from mild to disabling. It is described as progressive, and obesity is a risk factor; weight loss has been shown to slow progression. (1)

**Initial treatment is generally nonoperative.** Options include physical therapy, activity changes, bracing or orthoses, walking aids, anti-inflammatory medicines, injections, and analgesics. The cited AAOS recommendations support self-management, strengthening and low-impact aerobic exercise, neuromuscular education, physical activity, weight loss for people with BMI above 25, and NSAIDs or tramadol. Recommendations for some other treatments—including corticosteroid injections—are inconclusive in the table; it does not recommend hyaluronic acid, acupuncture, glucosamine/chondroitin, or lateral wedge insoles for symptomatic medial-compartment OA. (1)

If symptoms remain severe, surgical options may include osteotomy or partial or total knee replacement; the choice depends on age, activity expectations, disease severity, and how many knee compartments are involved. (2) Total knee arthroplasty is primarily considered to relieve pain from severe arthritis, after other pain sources are excluded and conservative measures have been exhausted; X-ray findings should match the clinical impression.
