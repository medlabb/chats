## Answer

 **Behçet syndrome** is a systemic vasculitis that can affect the mouth, genitals, skin, eyes, joints, blood vessels, gastrointestinal tract, and nervous system. Its cause is unknown; genetic predisposition and immune mechanisms may contribute. (1; 2)

- **Common features:** Recurrent oral ulcers occur in nearly all patients and are often the first symptom. Genital ulcers tend to be deeper and may scar; skin lesions and joint pain or arthritis are also common. Eye inflammation—often involving both eyes—can threaten vision. (3)
- **Other organ involvement:** Vascular disease may include blood clots in veins and pulmonary artery involvement. Less commonly, the disease affects the nervous system or gastrointestinal tract. Intestinal ulcers often occur near the ileocecal area and can resemble Crohn’s disease. (4)
- **Diagnosis:** It is made clinically; there is no specific laboratory, imaging, or tissue test. The commonly used International Study Group criteria require recurrent oral ulcers plus at least two of: recurrent genital ulcers, skin lesions, eye lesions, or a positive pathergy test. (5)
- **Who is affected:** It is most prevalent in Turkey and is more common in people from the Middle East, Mediterranean, and Far East; men and women are affected equally, though disease may be more severe in men. (1)
