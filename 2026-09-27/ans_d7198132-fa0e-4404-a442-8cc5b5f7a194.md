## Answer

 **Exchange transfusion is not automatically obligatory once the TSB has fallen to 19 mg/dL**, provided that:

- There are **no signs of acute bilirubin encephalopathy (ABE/BIND)**.
- The current TSB and bilirubin/albumin ratio are **below the applicable exchange-transfusion thresholds**.

In that situation, the references support **continuing intensive phototherapy**, with IV hydration if needed, and checking TSB **every 2 hours** until it is more than **2 mg/dL below the hour-specific exchange threshold**. Exchange may be deferred with close monitoring. (1) (2)

However, exchange transfusion is warranted if there are any neurologic signs—such as lethargy, abnormal tone, poor suck, high-pitched cry, apnea, seizures, retrocollis, or opisthotonos—even if the bilirubin is falling. (3)

A TSB of **30 mg/dL at approximately 4.5 days** is severe and appears above the exchange-threshold range cited for around 96 hours, but the exact decision must use the infant’s completed gestational age, neurotoxicity risk factors, bilirubin/albumin ratio, bilirubin trajectory, and local hour-specific chart. (3) The infant should remain under continuous neonatal specialist/NICU supervision while phototherapy and exchange preparations continue.
