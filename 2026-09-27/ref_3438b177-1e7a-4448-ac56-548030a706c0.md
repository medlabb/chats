## Criteria for an exchange transfusion
An exchange transfusion is performed for newborns with signs of acute bilirubin encephalopathy (ABE) and elevated total serum or plasma bilirubin (TSB) levels, and for those without signs of ABE whose TSB level or bilirubin/albumin (B/A) ratio is at or above the exchange transfusion thresholds  (algorithm 1) [1]:
- **Signs of ABE**– For newborns with elevated TSB levels and any sign of ABE (eg, lethargy, hyper- or hypotonia, poor suck, high-pitched cry, recurrent apnea, opisthotonos, retrocollis, seizures), exchange transfusion is warranted even if the TSB level is not above the treatment threshold  (figure 1A-B) or is declining. The BIND score  (table 1) can be used to determine the presence, severity, and progression of ABE and the need for exchange transfusion. For newborns with subtle neurologic findings, measuring the brainstem auditory-evoked response (BAER), also known as auditory brainstem response (ABR), may be helpful to assess for ABE; an abnormal BAER is highly suggestive of ABE [14]. However, BAER testing may not be readily available in many settings, and this assessment should not delay treatment. (See"Hearing loss in children: Screening and evaluation", section on 'Brainstem response'.)

- Newborns with signs of encephalopathy in the setting of TSB levels that are well below the threshold for exchange transfusion should undergo evaluation for other causes of their symptoms (eg, sepsis, hypoglycemia). ABE is unlikely at TSB levels <20 mg/dL (342 micromol/L) unless the bilirubin binding capacity of albumin is severely compromised. Most reported cases of ABE in term neonates have occurred at TSB levels ≥30 mg/dL (513 micromol/L). Thus, neurologic abnormalities (lethargy, poor tone, seizures) in a newborn with TSB <20 mg/dL (342 micromol/L) are more likely to be caused by other conditions rather than ABE. (See"Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia in newborns ≥35 weeks of gestation: Risk factors, clinical manifestations, and neurologic complications", section on 'Acute bilirubin encephalopathy (ABE)'and"Neonatal encephalopathy: Clinical features and diagnosis".)
